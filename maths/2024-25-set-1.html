Q1(i)Determinants[1]

If \(A=\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\7&5\end{bmatrix}\) then \(|A\,\operatorname{adj}A|\) is equal to:

Answer

\(|A|=2(5)-1(7)=3\).

\(A(\operatorname{adj}A)=|A|\,I_2\), so \(|A\,\operatorname{adj}A|=\big||A|I_2\big|=|A|^2=3^2=9\).

(b) 9

Concepts to recall

Q1(iv)Differential Equations[1]

Assertion: Degree of the differential equation \(\left\{1+\left(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right)^2\right\}^{3/2}=\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}\) is not defined.
Reason: If each term involving derivatives of the differential equation is a polynomial or can be expressed as a polynomial then the highest exponent of the highest order derivative is known as degree of the differential equation. Hence, or otherwise, the degree of the differential equation is not defined.

(a) Both Assertion and Reason are true, and Reason is the correct explanation for Assertion.
(b) Both Assertion and Reason are true, but Reason is not the correct explanation of the Assertion.
(c) Assertion is true, but Reason is false.
(d) Assertion is false, but Reason is true.

Answer

Squaring both sides makes the equation a polynomial in the derivatives: \(\left\{1+\left(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right)^2\right\}^{3}=\left(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}\right)^2\). Highest order derivative \(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}\) occurs with power 2, so the order is 2 and the degree is 2 — it is defined. The Assertion is false.

The Reason states the definition of degree correctly (it is that definition which gives degree 2).

(d)

The last sentence of the printed Reason (“Hence … not defined”) contradicts its own definition; the Reason is judged on the definition it states, which is true. Degree is undefined only when a derivative sits inside a non-polynomial function such as \(\sin\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)\) or \(e^{y'}\).

Concepts to recall

  • Clear fractional powers of derivatives (raise both sides to a power) before reading the degree.
  • Degree is not defined when a derivative appears inside sin, log, exp, etc.

Q1(xiii)Relations and Functions[1]

If \(R\) is a relation on set \(A=\{a,b,c\}\) represented as \(R:A\to A\) defined as \(R=\{(b,c)\}\) then classify the relation on set \(A\).

Answer

Reflexive? \((a,a)\notin R\): not reflexive.
Symmetric? \((b,c)\in R\) but \((c,b)\notin R\): not symmetric.
Transitive? There is no pair \((b,c),(c,z)\) in R, so the condition is never violated: transitive (vacuously).

R is transitive, but neither reflexive nor symmetric (so not an equivalence relation).

Concepts to recall

  • Transitive: \((a,b),(b,c)\in R\Rightarrow(a,c)\in R\); with no such chain it holds vacuously.

Q2Continuity, Differentiability and Differentiation[2]

If \(y=e^{\sin^{-1}x}\) and \(z=e^{-\cos^{-1}x}\) where \(|x|\le1\) then find \(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}\).

Answer

\(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=e^{\sin^{-1}x}\cdot\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}=\dfrac{y}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\), i.e. \(\sqrt{1-x^2}\,y_1=y\).

Differentiate again: \(\sqrt{1-x^2}\,y_2-\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}y_1=y_1\Rightarrow(1-x^2)y_2-xy_1=\sqrt{1-x^2}\,y_1=y\).

So \((1-x^2)y_2=xy_1+y\) and

\(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}=\dfrac{e^{\sin^{-1}x}\left(x+\sqrt{1-x^2}\right)}{(1-x^2)^{3/2}}\)   (\(|x|\lt 1\)).

z is not used in what is printed. The question was evidently meant to be about y and z together: \(\dfrac yz=e^{\sin^{-1}x+\cos^{-1}x}=e^{\pi/2}\), so \(y=e^{\pi/2}z\), \(\dfrac{dy}{dz}=e^{\pi/2}\) and \(\dfrac{d^2y}{dz^2}=0\).

  • \(\sin^{-1}x+\cos^{-1}x=\frac\pi2\).

Q7Inverse Trigonometric Functions[4]

\(\Rightarrow xy+z=\sqrt{(1-x^2)(1-y^2)}\).

Square: \(x^2y^2+2xyz+z^2=1-x^2-y^2+x^2y^2\)

\(\Rightarrow\) \(x^2+y^2+z^2+2xyz=1\)   Hence proved.

Divide by \(8y_1\) (\(y_1\ne0\) for \(x\gt 1\)): \((x^2-1)y_2+xy_1=\dfrac y4\).

\((x^2-1)\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}+x\dfrac{dy}{dx}-\dfrac y4=0\)   Hence proved.

  • Divide by \(y_1\) only after noting it is non-zero.
Q10Probability (Bayes' theorem)[4]

From a medical survey it was found that chances of a patient having heart attack is 40%. The survey states that a meditation and yoga course reduce the risk of heart attack by 30% while prescription of certain drug reduces it chances by 25%. At any given point in time, a patient can choose any one of the two options with equal probabilities. It is given that after going through one of the two options the patient selected at random suffers a heart attack. Find the probability that the patient followed a course of meditation and yoga.

Answer

Let \(E_1\): patient follows meditation and yoga, \(E_2\): patient takes the drug, H: patient has a heart attack.

\(P(E_1)=P(E_2)=\tfrac12\).

\(P(H|E_1)=0.40(1-0.30)=0.28\),   \(P(H|E_2)=0.40(1-0.25)=0.30\).

By Bayes' theorem,

\[P(E_1|H)=\frac{P(E_1)P(H|E_1)}{P(E_1)P(H|E_1)+P(E_2)P(H|E_2)}\]

\[=\frac{\frac12(0.28)}{\frac12(0.28)+\frac12(0.30)}=\frac{0.28}{0.58}=\frac{14}{29}.\]

\(\dfrac{14}{29}\approx0.483\)

Concepts to recall

  • “Reduces the risk by 30%” means the new risk is 70% of 0.40.
  • Bayes: \(P(E_i|H)=\dfrac{P(E_i)P(H|E_i)}{\sum_jP(E_j)P(H|E_j)}\).

What is the ideal price for each ticket that the department of the school set to maximise the profitability from the entry tickets.

Answer

\(P'(x)=-24x^2+29400=0\Rightarrow x^2=1225\Rightarrow x=35\) (\(x\gt 0\)).

\(P''(x)=-48x\); \(P''(35)=-1680\lt 0\): maximum.

Ideal ticket price ₹35 (maximum profit \(P(35)=₹6{,}26{,}000\)).

Concepts to recall

  • Maximum: \(P'(x)=0\) and \(P''(x)\lt 0\).
Q20 (OR)Applications of Derivatives[2]

A person runs a company that produces and sells a particular model of laptop. If demand function of the company is given by \(x=80-4p\), where \(x\) is the number of units produced and sold and \(p\) is the price per unit then find the value of \(x\) for which the revenue is maximum.

Answer

\(p=\dfrac{80-x}{4}\); revenue \(R=px=\dfrac{80x-x^2}{4}\).

\(\dfrac{dR}{dx}=\dfrac{80-2x}{4}=0\Rightarrow x=40\); \(\dfrac{d^2R}{dx^2}=-\dfrac12\lt 0\): maximum.

\(x=40\) units (at price \(p=10\), \(R_{\max}=400\)).

Concepts to recall

  • Write p in terms of x, then \(R=px\).
  • Revenue is maximum where marginal revenue \(\frac{dR}{dx}=0\) and \(\frac{d^2R}{dx^2}\lt 0\).
