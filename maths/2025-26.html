Q1(iii)Differential Equations[1]

If m and n are respectively the order and degree of the differential equation \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right)^3=0\), then the value of \((m-n)\) is

(a) 0   (b) 1   (c) 2   (d) 3

Answer

Carry out the differentiation first: \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right)^3=3\left(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right)^2\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}=0\).

  • Order = order of the highest derivative; degree = its power once the equation is a polynomial in the derivatives.

Q1(xii)Relations and Functions[1]

If a relation R on the set \(\{a,b,c\}\) defined by R = {(b, b)}, then classify the relation.

Answer

Reflexive? No: \((a,a)\notin R\) (and \((c,c)\notin R\)).

Symmetric? Yes: the only pair \((b,b)\) is its own reverse.

Transitive? Yes: the only chain is \((b,b),(b,b)\Rightarrow(b,b)\in R\).

R is symmetric and transitive but not reflexive (so not an equivalence relation).

Concepts to recall

  • Reflexive needs \((x,x)\) for every element of the set.
  • Symmetric/transitive are “if … then” conditions — satisfied vacuously when no pair breaks them.

Q1(xiv)Continuity, Differentiability and Differentiation[1]

Find the number of point(s) at which the function \(f(x)=\dfrac{1}{[x]-x}\) is discontinuous.

Answer

\([x]-x=-\{x\}\), which is 0 exactly when \(x\) is an integer. So \(f\) is not defined — hence discontinuous — at every integer; between consecutive integers \([x]\) is constant and \(f(x)=\dfrac{1}{k-x}\) is continuous.

The question intends the usual answer “at every integer”. (Under the strict NCERT convention, continuity is judged only at points of the domain, \(\mathbb R\setminus\mathbb Z\), where f is continuous; the integers are then points where f is not defined. Either way the trouble points are exactly the integers.)

Infinitely many points: every integer \(x\in\mathbb Z\).

Concepts to recall

  • \([x]\) is constant on \([k,k+1)\) and jumps at each integer.
  • A quotient is discontinuous where its denominator vanishes.

Q2Continuity, Differentiability and Differentiation[2]

If \(x^y=y^x\), find \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\)

Answer

Take logs: \(y\log x=x\log y\).

Differentiate w.r.t. \(x\): \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\log x+\dfrac yx=\log y+\dfrac xy\dfrac{dy}{dx}\).

\(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\left(\log x-\dfrac xy\right)=\log y-\dfrac yx\)

\(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{\log y-\frac yx}{\log x-\frac xy}=\dfrac{y(x\log y-y)}{x(y\log x-x)}\)

Concepts to recall

  • Logarithmic differentiation for variable powers \(u^v\).
  • Product rule on \(y\log x\).

Q7Inverse Trigonometric Functions[4]

Prove that \(\cos[\tan^{-1}\{\cot(\sin^{-1}x)\}]=x\).

Answer

Let \(\sin^{-1}x=\theta\), so \(x=\sin\theta\). For the identity take \(0\lt x\le1\), i.e. \(\theta\in(0,\tfrac\pi2]\) (see the note below).

Then \(\cot\theta=\tan\left(\dfrac\pi2-\theta\right)\), and \(\dfrac\pi2-\theta\in[0,\tfrac\pi2)\) lies in the principal range of \(\tan^{-1}\):

\[\tan^{-1}\{\cot(\sin^{-1}x)\}=\tan^{-1}\left\{\tan\left(\frac\pi2-\theta\right)\right\}=\frac\pi2-\theta.\]

\[\therefore\ \cos[\tan^{-1}\{\cot(\sin^{-1}x)\}]=\cos\left(\frac\pi2-\theta\right)=\sin\theta=x.\]

\(\cos[\tan^{-1}\{\cot(\sin^{-1}x)\}]=x\)  Hence proved.

Alternative (triangle): \(\cot\theta=\dfrac{\sqrt{1-x^2}}{x}\); \(\tan^{-1}\dfrac{\sqrt{1-x^2}}{x}=\phi\) gives \(\tan\phi=\dfrac{\sqrt{1-x^2}}{x}\), hypotenuse 1, so \(\cos\phi=x\).

Note: the identity holds for \(0<x\le1\). For \(-1\le x<0\) the left side equals \(-x\) (e.g. \(x=-\tfrac12\): \(\cot(-\tfrac\pi6)=-\sqrt3\), \(\tan^{-1}(-\sqrt3)=-\tfrac\pi3\), \(\cos(-\tfrac\pi3)=\tfrac12\)), and at \(x=0\) cot is undefined.

Concepts to recall

  • \(\cot\theta=\tan(\frac\pi2-\theta)\).
  • \(\tan^{-1}(\tan\alpha)=\alpha\) only for \(\alpha\in(-\frac\pi2,\frac\pi2)\).
  • \(\cos(\frac\pi2-\theta)=\sin\theta\).
Q8Continuity, Differentiability and Differentiation[4]

If \(y=\left(x+\sqrt{a^2+x^2}\right)^m\), prove that \((a^2+x^2)\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}+x\dfrac{dy}{dx}-m^2y=0\)

Answer

\[\frac{dy}{dx}=m\left(x+\sqrt{a^2+x^2}\right)^{m-1}\left(1+\frac{x}{\sqrt{a^2+x^2}}\right) =m\left(x+\sqrt{a^2+x^2}\right)^{m-1}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{a^2+x^2}+x}{\sqrt{a^2+x^2}}=\frac{my}{\sqrt{a^2+x^2}}.\]

So \(\sqrt{a^2+x^2}\,y_1=my\). Squaring: \((a^2+x^2)y_1^2=m^2y^2\).

Differentiate w.r.t. \(x\): \(2xy_1^2+(a^2+x^2)\cdot2y_1y_2=m^2\cdot2yy_1\).

Divide by \(2y_1\) (\(y_1\ne0\)): \((a^2+x^2)y_2+xy_1=m^2y\).

\((a^2+x^2)\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}+x\dfrac{dy}{dx}-m^2y=0\)  Hence proved.

Concepts to recall

  • Chain rule; \(\frac{d}{dx}\sqrt{a^2+x^2}=\frac{x}{\sqrt{a^2+x^2}}\).
  • Square to remove the root before differentiating a second time.

Q9Integrals[4]

Evaluate: \(\displaystyle\int\frac{\cos^{-1}x}{x^2}\,dx\)

Answer

By parts with \(u=\cos^{-1}x\), \(dv=x^{-2}dx\): \(du=-\dfrac{dx}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\), \(v=-\dfrac1x\).

\[I=-\frac{\cos^{-1}x}{x}-\int\left(-\frac1x\right)\left(-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\right)dx=-\frac{\cos^{-1}x}{x}-\int\frac{dx}{x\sqrt{1-x^2}}.\]

For \(J=\int\dfrac{dx}{x\sqrt{1-x^2}}\) put \(1-x^2=t^2\): \(x\,dx=-t\,dt\), so \(J=\int\dfrac{x\,dx}{x^2\sqrt{1-x^2}}=\int\dfrac{-t\,dt}{(1-t^2)t}=-\int\dfrac{dt}{1-t^2}=-\dfrac12\log\left|\dfrac{1+t}{1-t}\right|\).

Since \(\dfrac{1+t}{1-t}=\dfrac{(1+t)^2}{1-t^2}=\dfrac{(1+t)^2}{x^2}\), \(J=-\log\left|\dfrac{1+\sqrt{1-x^2}}{x}\right|\).

\(\displaystyle\int\frac{\cos^{-1}x}{x^2}\,dx=-\frac{\cos^{-1}x}{x}+\log\left|\frac{1+\sqrt{1-x^2}}{x}\right|+C\)

Concepts to recall

  • By parts \(\int u\,dv=uv-\int v\,du\); take the inverse-trig function as \(u\) (ILATE).
  • \(\frac{d}{dx}\cos^{-1}x=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\).
  • \(\int\frac{dt}{1-t^2}=\frac12\log\left|\frac{1+t}{1-t}\right|\).
Q10Probability[4]

A school offers its students, three modes for attending classes:

  • Mode A: Offline (in-person) – 40% of students
  • Mode B: Online (live virtual classes) – 35% of students
  • Mode C: Recorded lectures – 25% of students

After a feedback survey: • 20% of students from Mode A reported the class as “Excellent” • 30% from Mode B rated it as “Excellent” • 50% from Mode C rated it as “Excellent”

A student is selected at random, and it is found that he rated the class as “Excellent.” Using Bayes’ Theorem, find the probability that the student attended the Recorded lectures (Mode C).

Answer

Let \(E_1,E_2,E_3\): student attended Mode A, B, C; \(E\): rated “Excellent”.

\(P(E_1)=0.40,\ P(E_2)=0.35,\ P(E_3)=0.25\); \(P(E|E_1)=0.20,\ P(E|E_2)=0.30,\ P(E|E_3)=0.50\).

By the theorem of total probability, \(P(E)=0.40(0.20)+0.35(0.30)+0.25(0.50)=0.08+0.105+0.125=0.31\).

By Bayes’ theorem:

Q14Differential Equations[6]

Solve the differential equation: \((x\,dy-y\,dx)\,y\sin\left(\dfrac yx\right)=(y\,dx+x\,dy)\,x\cos\left(\dfrac yx\right)\). Find the particular solution satisfying the condition that \(y=\pi\) when \(x=1\).

\[\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{y^2\sin\frac yx+xy\cos\frac yx}{xy\sin\frac yx-x^2\cos\frac yx}=\frac{\frac yx\left(\frac yx\sin\frac yx+\cos\frac yx\right)}{\frac yx\sin\frac yx-\cos\frac yx},\]

a homogeneous equation. Put \(y=vx\), \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=v+x\dfrac{dv}{dx}\):

\[x\frac{dv}{dx}=\frac{v(v\sin v+\cos v)}{v\sin v-\cos v}-v=\frac{2v\cos v}{v\sin v-\cos v}.\]

With \(v=\tfrac yx\): general solution \(xy\cos\dfrac yx=C\)

At \(x=1,\ y=\pi\): \(C=1\cdot\pi\cos\pi=-\pi\).

Particular solution: \(xy\cos\left(\dfrac yx\right)=-\pi\)

Concepts to recall

  • Homogeneous equation: \(\frac{dy}{dx}=F(\frac yx)\); substitute \(y=vx\).
  • \(\int\tan v\,dv=-\log|\cos v|\).
  • Use the initial condition to fix the constant.

Two different types of books have to be stacked in the shelf of a library. The first type of book weighs 1 kg and has a thickness of 6 cm. The second type of book weighs 1.5 kg and has a thickness of 4 cm. The shelf is 96 cm long and can support a maximum weight of 21 kg. How should both the types of books be placed in the shelf to include the maximum number of books? Formulate a Linear Programming Problem and solve it graphically.

Answer

Let \(x\) books of the first type and \(y\) of the second be placed.

Maximise \(Z=x+y\) subject to

\(6x+4y\le96\) i.e. \(3x+2y\le48\) (shelf length),   \(x+1.5y\le21\) i.e. \(2x+3y\le42\) (weight),   \(x\ge0,\ y\ge0\).

Lines: \(3x+2y=48\) through \((16,0),(0,24)\); \(2x+3y=42\) through \((21,0),(0,14)\). Both inequalities are satisfied by O, so the feasible region lies on the origin side of each line.

Intersection: \(9x+6y=144,\ 4x+6y=84\Rightarrow5x=60\Rightarrow x=12,\ y=6\).

CornerO(0, 0)(16, 0)P(12, 6)(0, 14)
Z = x + y0161814

The region is bounded, so the maximum is at a corner: \(Z_{\max}=18\) at \((12,6)\).

12 books of the first type and 6 of the second: 18 books in all

Concepts to recall

  • Define the variables, write the objective and every constraint (including non-negativity).
  • Bounded feasible region: optimum at a corner point — evaluate Z at each.
