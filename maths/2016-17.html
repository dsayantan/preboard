Q1(a)Matrices[3]

Express the matrix \(A=\begin{bmatrix}3&-4\\1&-1\end{bmatrix}\) as a sum of symmetric and skew-symmetric matrix.

Answer

Any square matrix can be written as \(A=P+Q\) with \(P=\tfrac12(A+A')\) symmetric and \(Q=\tfrac12(A-A')\) skew-symmetric.

\(A'=\begin{bmatrix}3&1\\-4&-1\end{bmatrix}\).

\[P=\tfrac12(A+A')=\tfrac12\begin{bmatrix}6&-3\\-3&-2\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}3&-\tfrac32\\-\tfrac32&-1\end{bmatrix}, \quad P'=P\ \text{(symmetric)}\]

\[Q=\tfrac12(A-A')=\tfrac12\begin{bmatrix}0&-5\\5&0\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}0&-\tfrac52\\\tfrac52&0\end{bmatrix}, \quad Q'=-Q\ \text{(skew-symmetric)}\]

Check: \(P+Q=\begin{bmatrix}3&-4\\1&-1\end{bmatrix}=A\).

\(A=\begin{bmatrix}3&-\tfrac32\\-\tfrac32&-1\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}0&-\tfrac52\\\tfrac52&0\end{bmatrix}\)

Concepts to recall

  • \(A=\tfrac12(A+A')+\tfrac12(A-A')\).
  • Symmetric: \(P'=P\); skew-symmetric: \(Q'=-Q\) (diagonal entries 0).
Q1(b)Inverse Trigonometric Functions[3]

Prove that: \(\sin^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac45\right)+\cos^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac{2}{\sqrt5}\right)=\cot^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac{2}{11}\right)\)

Answer

Let \(\alpha=\sin^{-1}\tfrac45\). Then \(\sin\alpha=\tfrac45,\ \cos\alpha=\tfrac35\), so \(\tan\alpha=\tfrac43\), i.e. \(\alpha=\tan^{-1}\tfrac43\).

Let \(\beta=\cos^{-1}\tfrac{2}{\sqrt5}\). Then \(\cos\beta=\tfrac{2}{\sqrt5},\ \sin\beta=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt5}\), so \(\tan\beta=\tfrac12\), i.e. \(\beta=\tan^{-1}\tfrac12\).

Both angles lie in \((0,\tfrac\pi2)\) and \(\tfrac43\cdot\tfrac12=\tfrac23<1\), so

\[\alpha+\beta=\tan^{-1}\frac{\tfrac43+\tfrac12}{1-\tfrac43\cdot\tfrac12}=\tan^{-1}\frac{\tfrac{11}{6}}{\tfrac13}=\tan^{-1}\frac{11}{2}.\]

Since \(\tfrac{11}{2}>0\), \(\tan^{-1}\tfrac{11}{2}=\cot^{-1}\tfrac{2}{11}\).

\(\sin^{-1}\tfrac45+\cos^{-1}\tfrac{2}{\sqrt5}=\cot^{-1}\tfrac{2}{11}\)  Hence proved.

Concepts to recall

  • Convert every term to \(\tan^{-1}\) using a right triangle.
  • \(\tan^{-1}x+\tan^{-1}y=\tan^{-1}\dfrac{x+y}{1-xy}\) when \(xy<1\).
  • \(\tan^{-1}x=\cot^{-1}\dfrac1x\) for \(x>0\).
Q1(e)Integrals[3]

Evaluate: \(\displaystyle\int\frac{(x-\sin x)\,dx}{1-\cos x}\)

Answer

Use \(1-\cos x=2\sin^2\tfrac x2\) and \(\sin x=2\sin\tfrac x2\cos\tfrac x2\):

\[I=\int\frac{x}{2\sin^2\frac x2}\,dx-\int\frac{2\sin\frac x2\cos\frac x2}{2\sin^2\frac x2}\,dx =\int\frac x2\,\operatorname{cosec}^2\tfrac x2\,dx-\int\cot\tfrac x2\,dx.\]

By parts in the first integral, with \(u=x\), \(dv=\tfrac12\operatorname{cosec}^2\tfrac x2\,dx\Rightarrow v=-\cot\tfrac x2\):

\[\int\frac x2\,\operatorname{cosec}^2\tfrac x2\,dx=-x\cot\tfrac x2+\int\cot\tfrac x2\,dx.\]

The two \(\int\cot\frac x2\,dx\) terms cancel:

\(\displaystyle\int\frac{x-\sin x}{1-\cos x}\,dx=-x\cot\frac x2+C\)

Check: \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(-x\cot\frac x2\right)=-\cot\frac x2+\dfrac x2\operatorname{cosec}^2\frac x2 =\dfrac{x-\sin x}{2\sin^2\frac x2}\). ✓

Concepts to recall

  • \(1-\cos x=2\sin^2\frac x2\), \(\sin x=2\sin\frac x2\cos\frac x2\).
  • \(\int\operatorname{cosec}^2(ax)\,dx=-\frac1a\cot(ax)\).
  • By parts: \(\int u\,dv=uv-\int v\,du\); look for a term that cancels.
Q1(f)Integrals[3]

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{dx}{1+\tan x}\)

Answer

\[I=\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{\cos x}{\cos x+\sin x}\,dx\qquad(1)\]

Using \(\int_0^a f(x)\,dx=\int_0^a f(a-x)\,dx\) with \(a=\tfrac\pi2\) (\(\cos\to\sin,\ \sin\to\cos\)):

\[I=\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{\sin x}{\sin x+\cos x}\,dx\qquad(2)\]

Adding (1) and (2): \(2I=\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/2}1\,dx=\frac\pi2\).

\(I=\dfrac\pi4\)

Concepts to recall

  • \(\int_0^a f(x)\,dx=\int_0^a f(a-x)\,dx\).
  • On \([0,\frac\pi2]\) this swaps \(\sin x\) and \(\cos x\) — add the two forms.
Q1(i)Probability[3]

Two cards are drawn without replacement from a well shuffled pack of 52 cards. Find the probability that neither a jack nor a card of spades is drawn.

Answer

Cards that are a jack or a spade: 13 spades + 3 non-spade jacks = 16.

Cards that are neither a jack nor a spade: \(52-16=36\).

Both cards must come from these 36:

\[P=\frac{{}^{36}C_2}{{}^{52}C_2}=\frac{630}{1326}=\frac{105}{221}\]

\(P=\dfrac{105}{221}\approx0.475\)

Concepts to recall

  • \(n(J\cup S)=n(J)+n(S)-n(J\cap S)=4+13-1\).
  • Without replacement, “both from a group of \(k\)”: \({}^kC_2/{}^{52}C_2\).
Q1(j)Differential Equations[3]

Solve the differential equation: \(\sec^3x\,dy+\sec y\,dx=0\)

Answer

Separate the variables (divide by \(\sec^3x\sec y\)):

\[\cos y\,dy=-\cos^3x\,dx.\]

Write \(\cos^3x=(1-\sin^2x)\cos x\) and put \(\sin x=t\), \(\cos x\,dx=dt\):

\[\int\cos^3x\,dx=\int(1-t^2)\,dt=\sin x-\frac{\sin^3x}{3}.\]

Integrating both sides: \(\sin y=-\sin x+\dfrac{\sin^3x}{3}+C\).

\(\sin y+\sin x-\dfrac{\sin^3x}{3}=C\)

Concepts to recall

  • Variable separable: bring all \(y\) with \(dy\), all \(x\) with \(dx\).
  • Odd power of \(\cos\): keep one \(\cos x\), convert the rest with \(\cos^2x=1-\sin^2x\).
Q2(a)Determinants[5]

Using properties of determinants, prove that:

\[\begin{vmatrix}a-b-c&2a&2a\\2b&b-c-a&2b\\2c&2c&c-a-b\end{vmatrix}=(a+b+c)^3\]

Answer

Let \(\Delta\) be the determinant. Apply \(R_1\to R_1+R_2+R_3\); each entry of the new row is \(a+b+c\):

\[\Delta=\begin{vmatrix}a+b+c&a+b+c&a+b+c\\2b&b-c-a&2b\\2c&2c&c-a-b\end{vmatrix} =(a+b+c)\begin{vmatrix}1&1&1\\2b&b-c-a&2b\\2c&2c&c-a-b\end{vmatrix}\]

Apply \(C_2\to C_2-C_1\), \(C_3\to C_3-C_1\):

\[\Delta=(a+b+c)\begin{vmatrix}1&0&0\\2b&-(a+b+c)&0\\2c&0&-(a+b+c)\end{vmatrix}\]

(since \(b-c-a-2b=-(a+b+c)\) and \(c-a-b-2c=-(a+b+c)\)).

Expanding along \(R_1\): \(\Delta=(a+b+c)\cdot1\cdot\big[(-(a+b+c))(-(a+b+c))-0\big]\).

\(\Delta=(a+b+c)^3\)  Hence proved.

Concepts to recall

  • Adding rows to one row does not change the determinant.
  • A common factor of a row can be taken outside.
  • Make zeros in a row with \(C_j\to C_j-C_1\), then expand along that row.
Q2(b)Determinants[5]

If \(A=\begin{bmatrix}5&3&1\\2&1&3\\1&2&4\end{bmatrix}\) find \(A^{-1}\). Hence, solve the system of linear equations

\(5x+3y+z=16,\ 2x+y+3z=19,\) and \(x+2y+4z=25\).

Answer

\(|A|=5(4-6)-3(8-3)+1(4-1)=-10-15+3=-22\neq0\), so \(A^{-1}\) exists.

Cofactors:

\(C_{11}=-2,\ C_{12}=-5,\ C_{13}=3;\quad C_{21}=-10,\ C_{22}=19,\ C_{23}=-7;\quad C_{31}=8,\ C_{32}=-13,\ C_{33}=-1.\)

\[\operatorname{adj}A=\begin{bmatrix}-2&-10&8\\-5&19&-13\\3&-7&-1\end{bmatrix},\qquad A^{-1}=\frac{1}{|A|}\operatorname{adj}A=\frac{1}{22}\begin{bmatrix}2&10&-8\\5&-19&13\\-3&7&1\end{bmatrix}\]

The system is \(AX=B\) with \(X=\begin{bmatrix}x\\y\\z\end{bmatrix}\), \(B=\begin{bmatrix}16\\19\\25\end{bmatrix}\), so \(X=A^{-1}B\):

\[X=\frac1{22}\begin{bmatrix}32+190-200\\80-361+325\\-48+133+25\end{bmatrix}=\frac1{22}\begin{bmatrix}22\\44\\110\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1\\2\\5\end{bmatrix}\]

\(x=1,\ y=2,\ z=5\)

Check: \(5+6+5=16,\ 2+2+15=19,\ 1+4+20=25\). ✓

Concepts to recall

  • \(A^{-1}=\dfrac{\operatorname{adj}A}{|A|}\); adj \(A\) is the transpose of the cofactor matrix.
  • Cofactor \(C_{ij}=(-1)^{i+j}M_{ij}\).
  • \(AX=B\Rightarrow X=A^{-1}B\) (unique solution when \(|A|\neq0\)).
Q3(b)Inverse Trigonometric Functions[5]

Solve the for \(x\): \(\sin^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{1+x^2}}\right)-\sin^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1+x^2}}\right)=\sin^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac{1+x}{1+x^2}\right)\)

[Part (b) is printed before part (a) in the paper.]

Answer

Rewrite the left side. For every real \(x\), \(\sin^{-1}\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{1+x^2}}=\tan^{-1}x\).

Case \(x>0\). \(\sin^{-1}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1+x^2}}=\tan^{-1}\dfrac1x=\dfrac\pi2-\tan^{-1}x\). Put \(\theta=\tan^{-1}x\):

\[\text{LHS}=\theta-\left(\tfrac\pi2-\theta\right)=2\theta-\tfrac\pi2\in\left(-\tfrac\pi2,\tfrac\pi2\right).\]

Taking sine of both sides (sine is one-one on \([-\frac\pi2,\frac\pi2]\)):

\[\sin\!\left(2\theta-\tfrac\pi2\right)=-\cos2\theta=-\frac{1-x^2}{1+x^2}=\frac{x^2-1}{1+x^2}.\]

So \(\dfrac{x^2-1}{1+x^2}=\dfrac{1+x}{1+x^2}\Rightarrow x^2-x-2=0\Rightarrow(x-2)(x+1)=0\Rightarrow x=2\) or \(x=-1\). Only \(x=2\) satisfies \(x>0\).

Case \(x\le0\). Here \(\sin^{-1}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1+x^2}}=\dfrac\pi2+\tan^{-1}x\) (for \(x<0\) it equals \(\tan^{-1}\frac{1}{|x|}=\frac\pi2-|\tan^{-1}x|\); at \(x=0\) both sides are \(\frac\pi2\)), so LHS \(=\tan^{-1}x-\frac\pi2-\tan^{-1}x=-\dfrac\pi2\). That needs \(\dfrac{1+x}{1+x^2}=-1\), i.e. \(x^2+x+2=0\), which has no real root. (In particular \(x=-1\) gives LHS \(=-\frac\pi2\) but RHS \(=\sin^{-1}0=0\), so it is rejected.)

Check \(x=2\): LHS \(=\sin^{-1}\frac{2}{\sqrt5}-\sin^{-1}\frac1{\sqrt5}\), whose sine is \(\frac2{\sqrt5}\cdot\frac2{\sqrt5}-\frac1{\sqrt5}\cdot\frac1{\sqrt5}=\frac35\); RHS \(=\sin^{-1}\frac35\). ✓

\(x=2\)

Concepts to recall

  • \(\sin^{-1}\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{1+x^2}}=\tan^{-1}x\) (draw the triangle with sides \(x,1,\sqrt{1+x^2}\)).
  • \(\tan^{-1}x+\tan^{-1}\frac1x=\frac\pi2\) for \(x>0\), \(-\frac\pi2\) for \(x<0\).
  • \(\cos2\theta=\dfrac{1-\tan^2\theta}{1+\tan^2\theta}\).
  • Always substitute back: squaring/taking sines can create extra roots.
Q5(a)Continuity, Differentiability and Differentiation[5]

If \(y=\left(x+\sqrt{x^2-1}\right)^m\) then show that \((x^2-1)\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}+x\dfrac{dy}{dx}=m^2y\)

Answer

Differentiate with the chain rule:

\[\begin{aligned}\frac{dy}{dx}&=m\left(x+\sqrt{x^2-1}\right)^{m-1}\left(1+\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2-1}}\right)\\ &=m\left(x+\sqrt{x^2-1}\right)^{m-1}\cdot\frac{x+\sqrt{x^2-1}}{\sqrt{x^2-1}}=\frac{my}{\sqrt{x^2-1}}.\end{aligned}\]

So \(\sqrt{x^2-1}\,y_1=my\). Squaring: \((x^2-1)y_1^2=m^2y^2\).

Differentiate again with respect to \(x\):

\[(x^2-1)\cdot2y_1y_2+2x\,y_1^2=m^2\cdot2y\,y_1.\]

Divide by \(2y_1\) (\(y_1\neq0\)):

\((x^2-1)\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}+x\dfrac{dy}{dx}=m^2y\)  Hence proved.

Concepts to recall

  • \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\sqrt{x^2-1}=\dfrac{x}{\sqrt{x^2-1}}\).
  • For second-order identities: get \(y_1\) in terms of \(y\), square to remove the root, then differentiate again.
Q5(b)Applications of Derivatives[5]

A cylinder is such that the sum of its height and circumference of its base is 10 meters. Find the maximum volume of the cylinder.

Answer

Let radius \(=r\) m, height \(=h\) m. Given \(h+2\pi r=10\Rightarrow h=10-2\pi r\).

\[V=\pi r^2h=\pi r^2(10-2\pi r)=10\pi r^2-2\pi^2r^3,\qquad 0\lt r\lt\tfrac5\pi.\]

\(\dfrac{dV}{dr}=20\pi r-6\pi^2r^2=2\pi r(10-3\pi r)\). Setting \(\dfrac{dV}{dr}=0\) (with \(r\neq0\)): \(r=\dfrac{10}{3\pi}\).

\(\dfrac{d^2V}{dr^2}=20\pi-12\pi^2r\); at \(r=\frac{10}{3\pi}\): \(20\pi-40\pi=-20\pi<0\), so \(V\) is maximum.

Then \(h=10-2\pi\cdot\dfrac{10}{3\pi}=\dfrac{10}{3}\) m and

\[V_{\max}=\pi\left(\frac{10}{3\pi}\right)^2\cdot\frac{10}{3}=\frac{1000}{27\pi}\ \text{m}^3.\]

\(V_{\max}=\dfrac{1000}{27\pi}\ \text{m}^3\approx11.8\ \text{m}^3\) (when \(r=\frac{10}{3\pi}\) m, \(h=\frac{10}{3}\) m)

Concepts to recall

  • Use the constraint to write \(V\) in one variable.
  • \(V'=0\) for critical points; \(V''<0\Rightarrow\) maximum.
  • Circumference \(=2\pi r\), volume \(=\pi r^2h\).
Q6(a)Integrals[5]

Evaluate: \(\displaystyle\int\left(\sqrt{\tan x}+\sqrt{\cot x}\right)dx\)

Answer

(Taking \(0\lt x\lt\frac\pi2\), where both roots are real.)

\[I=\int\left(\sqrt{\frac{\sin x}{\cos x}}+\sqrt{\frac{\cos x}{\sin x}}\right)dx=\int\frac{\sin x+\cos x}{\sqrt{\sin x\cos x}}\,dx.\]

Put \(\sin x-\cos x=t\). Then \((\cos x+\sin x)\,dx=dt\) and \(t^2=1-2\sin x\cos x\Rightarrow\sin x\cos x=\dfrac{1-t^2}{2}\).

\[I=\int\frac{dt}{\sqrt{\frac{1-t^2}{2}}}=\sqrt2\int\frac{dt}{\sqrt{1-t^2}}=\sqrt2\sin^{-1}t+C.\]

\(\displaystyle\int\left(\sqrt{\tan x}+\sqrt{\cot x}\right)dx=\sqrt2\,\sin^{-1}(\sin x-\cos x)+C\)

Concepts to recall

  • \(\sqrt{\tan x}+\sqrt{\cot x}=\dfrac{\sin x+\cos x}{\sqrt{\sin x\cos x}}\).
  • Numerator \(\sin x+\cos x\) ⇒ substitute \(t=\sin x-\cos x\); then \(2\sin x\cos x=1-t^2\).
  • \(\int\dfrac{dt}{\sqrt{1-t^2}}=\sin^{-1}t\).
Q6(b)Application of Integrals[5]

Find the area enclosed by the curve \(y^2=2x-x^2\) and the straight line \(y=x\).

[Unclear in the original: the exponent on \(y\) is a faint mark, but the printed gap between \(y\) and “=” is the width of a superscript, so it is read as \(y^2\) (a circle), which is a standard question; if it is \(y=2x-x^2\) the area is \(\frac16\) sq. unit — worked at the end.]

Answer

\(y^2=2x-x^2\iff(x-1)^2+y^2=1\): a circle with centre \(C(1,0)\) and radius 1, through the origin.

Points of intersection with \(y=x\): \(x^2=2x-x^2\Rightarrow2x(x-1)=0\Rightarrow x=0,1\); points \(O(0,0)\), \(P(1,1)\).

The line cuts off the shaded segment between the upper semicircle \(y=\sqrt{2x-x^2}\) and the line, for \(0\le x\le1\):

\[A=\int_0^1\left(\sqrt{2x-x^2}-x\right)dx=\int_0^1\sqrt{1-(x-1)^2}\,dx-\int_0^1x\,dx.\]

With \(t=x-1\) and \(\int\sqrt{1-t^2}\,dt=\frac t2\sqrt{1-t^2}+\frac12\sin^{-1}t\):

\[\int_{-1}^{0}\sqrt{1-t^2}\,dt=\left[\tfrac t2\sqrt{1-t^2}+\tfrac12\sin^{-1}t\right]_{-1}^{0}=0-\left(-\tfrac\pi4\right)=\tfrac\pi4, \qquad\int_0^1x\,dx=\tfrac12.\]

\(A=\left(\dfrac\pi4-\dfrac12\right)\) sq. units \(\approx0.285\) sq. units

(Check: quarter circle \(OCP\) has area \(\frac\pi4\), triangle \(OCP\) has area \(\frac12\); the segment is their difference.)

If the curve is read as \(y=2x-x^2\): intersections are again \(x=0,1\), and \(A=\int_0^1\left[(2x-x^2)-x\right]dx=\left[\frac{x^2}{2}-\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^1=\frac16\) sq. unit.

Concepts to recall

  • Complete the square to recognise the curve: \(x^2+y^2=2x\) is a circle.
  • Area between curves \(=\int_a^b(y_{\text{upper}}-y_{\text{lower}})\,dx\), limits from the intersections.
  • \(\int\sqrt{a^2-t^2}\,dt=\frac t2\sqrt{a^2-t^2}+\frac{a^2}2\sin^{-1}\frac ta+C\).
Q8(a)Probability[5]

Two cards are drawn at random from a pack of 52 cards without replacement. Find the probability that

(i) the cards drawn are either both red cards or both jacks.
(ii) one of the cards dawn is a jack of red colour and the other one is a card of spade.

Answer

Total ways: \({}^{52}C_2=1326\).

(i) Let \(R\): both red, \(J\): both jacks. \(R\cap J\): both red jacks.

\(n(R)={}^{26}C_2=325,\quad n(J)={}^4C_2=6,\quad n(R\cap J)={}^2C_2=1\).

\[P(R\cup J)=\frac{325+6-1}{1326}=\frac{330}{1326}=\frac{55}{221}\]

(i) \(\dfrac{55}{221}\)

(ii) Choose one red jack (2 ways) and one spade (13 ways; no red jack is a spade, so the two choices never overlap):

\[P=\frac{2\times13}{1326}=\frac{26}{1326}=\frac1{51}\]

(ii) \(\dfrac{1}{51}\)

Concepts to recall

  • Addition theorem: \(P(A\cup B)=P(A)+P(B)-P(A\cap B)\).
  • Drawing 2 cards together/without replacement: count with combinations, order ignored.
Q8(b)Probability[5]

Bag A contains 4 white balls and 3 black balls, while Bag B contains 2 white balls and 2 black balls. Two balls are transferred from Bag A to Bag B. Find the probability of drawing 1 white ball and 1 black ball from Bag B.

[Two balls are drawn from Bag B, which then holds 6 balls.]

Answer

Let \(E_1\): 2 white transferred, \(E_2\): 1 white and 1 black transferred, \(E_3\): 2 black transferred. \(E_1,E_2,E_3\) are mutually exclusive and exhaustive (\({}^7C_2=21\)):

\(P(E_1)=\dfrac{{}^4C_2}{21}=\dfrac6{21},\quad P(E_2)=\dfrac{4\cdot3}{21}=\dfrac{12}{21},\quad P(E_3)=\dfrac{{}^3C_2}{21}=\dfrac3{21}.\)

Let \(A\): two balls drawn from Bag B (6 balls, \({}^6C_2=15\)) are 1 white and 1 black.

Bag B then has\(P(A\mid E_i)\)
\(E_1\)4 W, 2 B\(\frac{4\cdot2}{15}=\frac8{15}\)
\(E_2\)3 W, 3 B\(\frac{3\cdot3}{15}=\frac9{15}\)
\(E_3\)2 W, 4 B\(\frac{2\cdot4}{15}=\frac8{15}\)

By the theorem of total probability:

\[P(A)=\frac6{21}\cdot\frac8{15}+\frac{12}{21}\cdot\frac9{15}+\frac3{21}\cdot\frac8{15}=\frac{48+108+24}{315}=\frac{180}{315}=\frac47.\]

\(P=\dfrac47\)

Concepts to recall

  • Total probability: \(P(A)=\sum P(E_i)\,P(A\mid E_i)\) over exhaustive, mutually exclusive cases.
  • List every possible transfer and the new contents of the bag.
Q9(b)Differential Equations[5]

Solve the differential equation: \((x^2-y^2)\,dx+2xy\,dy=0\), given that \(y=1\) when \(x=1\).

Answer

\(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{y^2-x^2}{2xy}\) — homogeneous (degree 2 above and below). Put \(y=vx\), \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=v+x\dfrac{dv}{dx}\):

\[v+x\frac{dv}{dx}=\frac{v^2-1}{2v}\ \Rightarrow\ x\frac{dv}{dx}=\frac{v^2-1-2v^2}{2v}=-\frac{1+v^2}{2v}.\]

Separating: \(\dfrac{2v}{1+v^2}\,dv=-\dfrac{dx}{x}\). Integrating:

\[\begin{aligned}\log(1+v^2)&=-\log|x|+\log C\\ \Rightarrow\ x(1+v^2)&=C\ \Rightarrow\ x\left(1+\frac{y^2}{x^2}\right)=C\ \Rightarrow\ x^2+y^2=Cx.\end{aligned}\]

At \(x=1,\ y=1\): \(1+1=C\Rightarrow C=2\).

\(x^2+y^2=2x\)

Concepts to recall

  • Homogeneous: \(\frac{dy}{dx}=f\!\left(\frac yx\right)\); substitute \(y=vx\).
  • \(\int\dfrac{f'(v)}{f(v)}dv=\log|f(v)|\).
  • Use the given point to find \(C\) for the particular solution.

Q11(a)Three-dimensional Geometry[5]

Find the shortest distance between the lines whose vector equations are:

\(\vec r=3\hat i+8\hat j+3\hat k+\lambda(3\hat i-\hat j+\hat k)\) and \(\vec r=-3\hat i-7\hat j+6\hat k+\mu(3\hat i-2\hat j-4\hat k)\)

Answer

Here \(\vec a_1=3\hat i+8\hat j+3\hat k,\ \vec b_1=3\hat i-\hat j+\hat k;\quad \vec a_2=-3\hat i-7\hat j+6\hat k,\ \vec b_2=3\hat i-2\hat j-4\hat k\).

\[\vec b_1\times\vec b_2=\begin{vmatrix}\hat i&\hat j&\hat k\\3&-1&1\\3&-2&-4\end{vmatrix} =\hat i(4+2)-\hat j(-12-3)+\hat k(-6+3)=6\hat i+15\hat j-3\hat k,\]

\(|\vec b_1\times\vec b_2|=\sqrt{36+225+9}=\sqrt{270}=3\sqrt{30}\).

\(\vec a_2-\vec a_1=-6\hat i-15\hat j+3\hat k\), so \((\vec a_2-\vec a_1)\cdot(\vec b_1\times\vec b_2)=-36-225-9=-270\).

\[d=\left|\frac{(\vec a_2-\vec a_1)\cdot(\vec b_1\times\vec b_2)}{|\vec b_1\times\vec b_2|}\right|=\frac{270}{3\sqrt{30}}=\frac{90}{\sqrt{30}}=3\sqrt{30}.\]

Shortest distance \(=3\sqrt{30}\) units \(\approx16.43\) units

(Since \(d\neq0\), the lines are skew.)

Concepts to recall

  • Skew lines \(\vec r=\vec a_1+\lambda\vec b_1,\ \vec r=\vec a_2+\mu\vec b_2\): \(d=\dfrac{|(\vec a_2-\vec a_1)\cdot(\vec b_1\times\vec b_2)|}{|\vec b_1\times\vec b_2|}\).

Q11(b)Three-dimensional Geometry[5]

Find the equation of the plane passing through the line of intersection of the planes \(\vec r\cdot(4\hat i-\hat j+\hat k)=0\), \(\vec r\cdot(3\hat i+\hat j-\hat k)=5\) and parallel to the straight line \(\vec r=4\hat i-3\hat j+\hat k+\lambda(\hat i-2\hat j+3\hat k)\)

Answer

Any plane through the line of intersection:

\[\begin{aligned}&\vec r\cdot(4\hat i-\hat j+\hat k)+k\left[\vec r\cdot(3\hat i+\hat j-\hat k)-5\right]=0\\ \Rightarrow\ &\vec r\cdot\big[(4+3k)\hat i+(k-1)\hat j+(1-k)\hat k\big]=5k.\end{aligned}\]

The plane is parallel to the line, so its normal is perpendicular to the line's direction \(\hat i-2\hat j+3\hat k\):

\[(4+3k)(1)+(k-1)(-2)+(1-k)(3)=0\ \Rightarrow\ 9-2k=0\ \Rightarrow\ k=\tfrac92.\]

Normal \(=\tfrac{35}{2}\hat i+\tfrac72\hat j-\tfrac72\hat k\), right side \(=\tfrac{45}{2}\). Multiplying by 2:

\(\vec r\cdot(35\hat i+7\hat j-7\hat k)=45\), i.e. \(35x+7y-7z=45\)

Q12(a)Probability[5]

There are three urns, Urn I contains 1 white, 2 black, and 3 red balls. Urn II contains 2 white, 1 black and 1 red balls. Urn III contains 4 white, 5 black, and 3 red balls. One urn is chosen at random and two balls drawn without replacement. They happen to be 1 white and 1 red ball. What is the probability that they belongs to Urn II.

Answer

Let \(E_1,E_2,E_3\): urn I, II, III is chosen; \(P(E_i)=\frac13\). Let \(A\): the two balls are 1 white and 1 red.

\(P(A\mid E_1)=\dfrac{1\cdot3}{{}^6C_2}=\dfrac3{15}=\dfrac15,\quad P(A\mid E_2)=\dfrac{2\cdot1}{{}^4C_2}=\dfrac26=\dfrac13,\quad P(A\mid E_3)=\dfrac{4\cdot3}{{}^{12}C_2}=\dfrac{12}{66}=\dfrac2{11}.\)

By Bayes' theorem:

\[P(E_2\mid A)=\frac{\frac13\cdot\frac13}{\frac13\left(\frac15+\frac13+\frac2{11}\right)} =\frac{\frac13}{\frac{33+55+30}{165}}=\frac{\frac13}{\frac{118}{165}}=\frac{165}{354}=\frac{55}{118}.\]

\(P(\text{Urn II}\mid\text{1 white, 1 red})=\dfrac{55}{118}\approx0.466\)

Concepts to recall

  • Bayes: \(P(E_i\mid A)=\dfrac{P(E_i)P(A\mid E_i)}{\sum_j P(E_j)P(A\mid E_j)}\).
  • Equal priors cancel — only the likelihoods \(P(A\mid E_i)\) matter.

Q13(b)Linear Programming[5]

Suppose every gram of wheat provides 0.1 gm of proteins and 0.25 gm of carbohydrates, while every gram of rice provides 0.05 gm of proteins and 0.5 gm of proteins. Wheat cost Rs 5 per kg while rice cost Rs 20 per kg. The minimum daily requirements of protein and carbohydrate for a normal person are 50 gm and 200 gm respectively. In what quantities should wheat and rice be mixed in the daily diet to provide minimum daily requirements of proteins and carbohydrates at minimum cost, assuming that both wheat and rice are to be taken in diet?

[Misprint in the original: rice's “0.5 gm of proteins” must be 0.5 gm of carbohydrates.]

Answer

Formulation. Let the diet contain \(x\) g of wheat and \(y\) g of rice. Cost: wheat Rs 5/kg = Rs 0.005 per g, rice Rs 20/kg = Rs 0.02 per g.

Minimise \(Z=0.005x+0.02y\) subject to

\[0.1x+0.05y\ge50\ \Rightarrow\ 2x+y\ge1000\quad\text{(protein)}\] \[0.25x+0.5y\ge200\ \Rightarrow\ x+2y\ge800\quad\text{(carbohydrate)}\] \[x\ge0,\ y\ge0.\]

Graph. \(2x+y=1000\) through \((500,0),(0,1000)\); \(x+2y=800\) through \((800,0),(0,400)\). Both are “\(\ge\)”, so the feasible region lies on the side away from the origin — the unbounded shaded region. The lines meet where \(2x+y=1000,\ x+2y=800\Rightarrow x=400,\ y=200\).

Corner point\(Z=0.005x+0.02y\) (Rs)
A(0, 1000)20
B(400, 200)6
C(800, 0)4 (least)

The region is unbounded, so check the open half-plane \(Z<4\), i.e. \(x+4y<800\): it has no point in common with the feasible region (any feasible point has \(x+2y\ge800\), so \(x+4y\ge800\)). Hence the minimum is Rs 4 at C.

Minimum cost Rs 4 per day: 800 g of wheat and no rice

On “both wheat and rice are to be taken”: with these prices wheat is cheaper per gram of protein and per gram of carbohydrate, so the optimum uses no rice. If some rice is insisted on, the cost can be brought as close to Rs 4 as we like (points on CB near C), but no positive-rice mix attains a minimum; the only corner with both foods, B (400 g wheat, 200 g rice), costs Rs 6. The data as printed (probably copied with altered prices) do not support a mixed optimum. In the familiar version of this problem neither food dominates — e.g. wheat Rs 4 per kg and rice Rs 6 per kg give corner costs A Rs 6, B Rs 2.80, C Rs 3.20 — and the optimum is then the mixed corner B: 400 g of wheat and 200 g of rice. That is evidently the answer the question's last line expects.

Concepts to recall

  • Convert all units first (Rs per kg → Rs per g).
  • Corner-point method: evaluate \(Z\) at each vertex of the feasible region.
  • Unbounded region: the minimum \(m\) is valid only if \(Z\lt m\) has no point in common with the region.
Q14(b)Applications of Derivatives (rate of change)[5]

The average cost function for \(x\) units of a commodity is \(AC=\dfrac{x^2}{3}+3x-7+\dfrac{16}{x}\).

(i) Find the Marginal Cost.
(ii) Show that the Marginal Average cost is given by \(\dfrac{x(MC)-C(x)}{x^2}\)

Answer

(i) Total cost \(C(x) = x\cdot AC\):

\[C(x)=x\left(\frac{x^2}{3}+3x-7+\frac{16}{x}\right)=\frac{x^3}{3}+3x^2-7x+16.\]

Marginal cost is the rate of change of total cost with output:

\[MC=\frac{dC}{dx}=x^2+6x-7.\]

\(MC = x^2+6x-7\)

(ii) Marginal average cost is the rate of change of average cost: \(MAC=\dfrac{d}{dx}(AC)=\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(\dfrac{C(x)}{x}\right)\).

By the quotient rule,

\[\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{C(x)}{x}\right)=\frac{x\,C'(x)-C(x)\cdot 1}{x^2}=\frac{x(MC)-C(x)}{x^2},\]

since \(C'(x)=MC\). Hence \(MAC=\dfrac{x(MC)-C(x)}{x^2}\). \(\blacksquare\)

Check with the given function: \(\dfrac{d}{dx}(AC)=\dfrac{2x}{3}+3-\dfrac{16}{x^2}\), and \(\dfrac{x(x^2+6x-7)-\left(\frac{x^3}{3}+3x^2-7x+16\right)}{x^2}=\dfrac{\frac{2x^3}{3}+3x^2-16}{x^2}=\dfrac{2x}{3}+3-\dfrac{16}{x^2}\). ✓

Concepts to recall

  • \(C(x)=x\cdot AC(x)\)
  • Marginal cost \(MC=\dfrac{dC}{dx}\); marginal average cost \(=\dfrac{d(AC)}{dx}\)
  • Quotient rule: \(\left(\dfrac{u}{v}\right)'=\dfrac{vu'-uv'}{v^2}\)
