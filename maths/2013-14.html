Q1(i)Matrices[3]

Express the matrix \(A=\begin{bmatrix}3&-4\\1&-1\end{bmatrix}\) as the sum of a symmetric and a skew-symmetric matrix.

Answer

Every square matrix can be written as \(A=P+Q\) with \(P=\tfrac12(A+A')\) symmetric and \(Q=\tfrac12(A-A')\) skew-symmetric.

\(A'=\begin{bmatrix}3&1\\-4&-1\end{bmatrix}\).

\[P=\tfrac12(A+A')=\tfrac12\begin{bmatrix}6&-3\\-3&-2\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}3&-\tfrac32\\-\tfrac32&-1\end{bmatrix},\qquad P'=P\ \text{(symmetric)}\]

\[Q=\tfrac12(A-A')=\tfrac12\begin{bmatrix}0&-5\\5&0\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}0&-\tfrac52\\\tfrac52&0\end{bmatrix},\qquad Q'=-Q\ \text{(skew-symmetric)}\]

Check: \(P+Q=\begin{bmatrix}3&-4\\1&-1\end{bmatrix}=A\).

\(A=\begin{bmatrix}3&-\tfrac32\\-\tfrac32&-1\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}0&-\tfrac52\\\tfrac52&0\end{bmatrix}\)

Concepts to recall

  • \(A=\tfrac12(A+A')+\tfrac12(A-A')\).
  • Symmetric: \(P'=P\); skew-symmetric: \(Q'=-Q\) (diagonal entries 0).
  • Always verify the two parts add back to \(A\).
Q1(iii)Inverse Trigonometric Functions[3]

Solve for \(x\): \(\tan^{-1}(2x)+\tan^{-1}(3x)=\dfrac{\pi}{4}\).

Answer

Using \(\tan^{-1}a+\tan^{-1}b=\tan^{-1}\dfrac{a+b}{1-ab}\) (valid when \(ab<1\)):

\[\tan^{-1}\frac{2x+3x}{1-6x^2}=\frac{\pi}{4}\ \Rightarrow\ \frac{5x}{1-6x^2}=1\ \Rightarrow\ 6x^2+5x-1=0\]

\[(6x-1)(x+1)=0\ \Rightarrow\ x=\tfrac16\ \text{or}\ x=-1.\]

Check: for \(x=-1\) the left side is \(\tan^{-1}(-2)+\tan^{-1}(-3)\), a sum of two negative angles, so it cannot equal \(\pi/4\); also \(ab=6>1\). Reject.

For \(x=\tfrac16\): \(\tan^{-1}\tfrac13+\tan^{-1}\tfrac12=\tan^{-1}\dfrac{5/6}{5/6}=\tan^{-1}1=\tfrac{\pi}{4}\). ✓

\(x=\tfrac16\)

Concepts to recall

  • \(\tan^{-1}a+\tan^{-1}b=\tan^{-1}\frac{a+b}{1-ab}\) for \(ab<1\).
  • Always substitute back: a root can make the left side negative or need \(ab>1\).
Q1(v)Integrals[3]

Evaluate: \(\displaystyle\int e^x\left(\frac{1+\sin x}{1+\cos x}\right)dx\).

Answer

Use \(1+\cos x=2\cos^2\frac x2\) and \(\sin x=2\sin\frac x2\cos\frac x2\):

\[\frac{1+\sin x}{1+\cos x}=\frac{1}{2\cos^2\frac x2}+\frac{2\sin\frac x2\cos\frac x2}{2\cos^2\frac x2}=\tfrac12\sec^2\tfrac x2+\tan\tfrac x2.\]

So the integral has the form \(\int e^x\{f(x)+f'(x)\}\,dx\) with \(f(x)=\tan\frac x2\), \(f'(x)=\tfrac12\sec^2\frac x2\).

\[\int e^x\{f(x)+f'(x)\}dx=e^x f(x)+C\]

\(\displaystyle\int e^x\frac{1+\sin x}{1+\cos x}\,dx=e^x\tan\frac x2+C\)

Concepts to recall

  • \(\int e^x\{f(x)+f'(x)\}dx=e^xf(x)+C\) (from \((e^xf)'\)).
  • Half-angle forms: \(1+\cos x=2\cos^2\frac x2\), \(\sin x=2\sin\frac x2\cos\frac x2\).
Q1(vi)Definite Integrals[3]

If \(\displaystyle\int_0^K\frac{dx}{2+8x^2}=\frac{\pi}{16}\), then find K.

Answer

\[\int_0^K\frac{dx}{2+8x^2}=\frac18\int_0^K\frac{dx}{x^2+\left(\frac12\right)^2}=\frac18\cdot\frac{1}{1/2}\Big[\tan^{-1}2x\Big]_0^K=\frac14\tan^{-1}2K.\]

\[\frac14\tan^{-1}2K=\frac{\pi}{16}\ \Rightarrow\ \tan^{-1}2K=\frac{\pi}{4}\ \Rightarrow\ 2K=1.\]

\(K=\tfrac12\)

Concepts to recall

  • \(\int\frac{dx}{x^2+a^2}=\frac1a\tan^{-1}\frac xa+C\).
  • Take the coefficient of \(x^2\) out first.
Q1(ix)Differential Equations[3]

Find the particular solution of \(y(1-x^2)\,dy+x(1-y^2)\,dx=0\) given that \(y=1\) when \(x=0\).

Answer

Separating the variables (for \(x^2\ne1,\ y^2\ne1\)):

\[\frac{y\,dy}{1-y^2}=-\frac{x\,dx}{1-x^2}.\]

Integrate: \(-\tfrac12\log|1-y^2|=\tfrac12\log|1-x^2|+c\), i.e.

\[\log|1-y^2|+\log|1-x^2|=-2c\ \Rightarrow\ \boxed{(1-x^2)(1-y^2)=C}\quad\text{(general solution).}\]

Particular solution. Putting \(x=0,\ y=1\): \(C=(1)(0)=0\), so \((1-x^2)(1-y^2)=0\). Near \(x=0\) the factor \(1-x^2\ne0\), so \(1-y^2=0\), and the branch through \((0,1)\) is

\(y=1\)

Check: with \(y=1\), \(dy=0\) and \(1-y^2=0\), so both terms of the equation vanish. ✓

[The given condition lies on the line \(y=1\), where the separation step divides by \(1-y^2=0\); the answer is therefore the constant solution. The question was very likely meant to read \(y(1+x^2)dy+x(1+y^2)dx=0\): then \(\frac{y\,dy}{1+y^2}=-\frac{x\,dx}{1+x^2}\) gives \((1+x^2)(1+y^2)=C\), and \(y=1\) at \(x=0\) gives the particular solution \((1+x^2)(1+y^2)=2\).]

Concepts to recall

  • Variable separable: collect \(y\) with \(dy\), \(x\) with \(dx\), integrate both sides, one constant.
  • \(\int\frac{x\,dx}{1\pm x^2}=\pm\frac12\log|1\pm x^2|\).
  • Particular solution: substitute the condition to fix \(C\).
Q1(x)Probability[3]

Bag P contains 5 red and 3 green balls and bag Q contains 3 red and 4 green balls. A ball is transferred from bag P to Q and then a ball is drawn from bag Q. What is the probability that the ball drawn is green?

Answer

Let \(E_1\): the transferred ball is red, \(E_2\): it is green; \(G\): the ball drawn from Q is green.

\(P(E_1)=\tfrac58,\ P(E_2)=\tfrac38\). After transfer Q has 8 balls:

\(P(G\mid E_1)=\tfrac48\) (3 red + 1 red, 4 green); \(P(G\mid E_2)=\tfrac58\) (3 red, 4 + 1 green).

By the theorem of total probability,

\[P(G)=\tfrac58\cdot\tfrac48+\tfrac38\cdot\tfrac58=\tfrac{20}{64}+\tfrac{15}{64}=\tfrac{35}{64}.\]

\(P(\text{green})=\dfrac{35}{64}\)

Concepts to recall

  • Total probability: \(P(A)=\sum P(E_i)P(A\mid E_i)\) over a partition \(E_1,E_2,\dots\)
  • Recount the second bag after the transfer (it now holds 8 balls).
Q2(a)Determinants[5]

Using properties of determinants, prove that:

\[\begin{vmatrix}b+c&c+a&a+b\\c+a&a+b&b+c\\a+b&b+c&c+a\end{vmatrix}=-2(a^3+b^3+c^3-3abc)\]

Answer

Let \(\Delta\) be the determinant. Apply \(C_1\to C_1+C_2+C_3\); every entry of \(C_1\) becomes \(2(a+b+c)\):

\[\Delta=\begin{vmatrix}2(a+b+c)&c+a&a+b\\2(a+b+c)&a+b&b+c\\2(a+b+c)&b+c&c+a\end{vmatrix}=2(a+b+c)\begin{vmatrix}1&c+a&a+b\\1&a+b&b+c\\1&b+c&c+a\end{vmatrix}\]

(taking the common factor \(2(a+b+c)\) out of \(C_1\)). Now \(R_2\to R_2-R_1,\ R_3\to R_3-R_1\):

\[\Delta=2(a+b+c)\begin{vmatrix}1&c+a&a+b\\0&b-c&c-a\\0&b-a&c-b\end{vmatrix}\]

Expanding along \(C_1\):

\[\begin{aligned}\Delta&=2(a+b+c)\big[(b-c)(c-b)-(c-a)(b-a)\big]\\&=2(a+b+c)\big[-(b^2-2bc+c^2)-(bc-ab-ac+a^2)\big]\\&=-2(a+b+c)(a^2+b^2+c^2-ab-bc-ca).\end{aligned}\]

Since \(a^3+b^3+c^3-3abc=(a+b+c)(a^2+b^2+c^2-ab-bc-ca)\),

\(\Delta=-2(a^3+b^3+c^3-3abc)\)  Hence proved.

Concepts to recall

  • Adding all columns to one column does not change the determinant; a common factor of a row/column comes out.
  • \(R_i\to R_i-R_j\) creates zeros without changing the value.
  • \(a^3+b^3+c^3-3abc=(a+b+c)(a^2+b^2+c^2-ab-bc-ca)\).

Find \(A^{-1}\) when \(A=\begin{bmatrix}4&2&3\\1&1&1\\3&1&-2\end{bmatrix}\). Hence solve the system of equations

\(4x+2y+3z=2,\ x+y+z=1,\ 3x+y-2z=5\).

[Unclear in the original: the first row of \(A\) is cut off at the page break of the scan. It is read as \(4\ \ 2\ \ 3\), the coefficients of the first equation, which the 'hence' requires.]

\(|A|=4(-2-1)-2(-2-3)+3(1-3)=-12+10-6=-8\ne0\), so \(A^{-1}\) exists.

\[\operatorname{adj}A=\begin{bmatrix}-3&7&-1\\5&-17&-1\\-2&2&2\end{bmatrix},\qquad A^{-1}=\frac{\operatorname{adj}A}{|A|}=-\frac18\begin{bmatrix}-3&7&-1\\5&-17&-1\\-2&2&2\end{bmatrix}.\]

The system is \(AX=B\) with \(X=\begin{bmatrix}x\\y\\z\end{bmatrix},\ B=\begin{bmatrix}2\\1\\5\end{bmatrix}\), so \(X=A^{-1}B\):

Q3(b)Inverse Trigonometric Functions[5]

Prove that: \(\sin\!\left(2\tan^{-1}\dfrac35-\sin^{-1}\dfrac{7}{25}\right)=\dfrac{304}{425}\).

Answer

Let \(\alpha=\tan^{-1}\frac35\) and \(\beta=\sin^{-1}\frac{7}{25}\) (both acute).

With \(\tan\alpha=\frac35\):

\[\sin2\alpha=\frac{2\tan\alpha}{1+\tan^2\alpha}=\frac{6/5}{34/25}=\frac{15}{17},\qquad \cos2\alpha=\frac{1-\tan^2\alpha}{1+\tan^2\alpha}=\frac{16/25}{34/25}=\frac{8}{17}.\]

With \(\sin\beta=\frac7{25}\): \(\cos\beta=\sqrt{1-\frac{49}{625}}=\frac{24}{25}\).

\[\sin(2\alpha-\beta)=\sin2\alpha\cos\beta-\cos2\alpha\sin\beta=\frac{15}{17}\cdot\frac{24}{25}-\frac{8}{17}\cdot\frac{7}{25}=\frac{360-56}{425}.\]

\(\sin\!\left(2\tan^{-1}\tfrac35-\sin^{-1}\tfrac7{25}\right)=\dfrac{304}{425}\)  Hence proved.

Concepts to recall

  • \(\sin2\theta=\frac{2\tan\theta}{1+\tan^2\theta}\), \(\cos2\theta=\frac{1-\tan^2\theta}{1+\tan^2\theta}\).
  • \(\sin(A-B)=\sin A\cos B-\cos A\sin B\).
  • Name each inverse-trig term as an angle and read its other ratios off a right triangle.
Q5(a)Differentiation (second order)[5]

If \(x=\sin\phi,\ y=\sin\lambda\phi\), then prove that \((1-x^2)\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}-x\dfrac{dy}{dx}+\lambda^2y=0\).

Answer

\(\dfrac{dx}{d\phi}=\cos\phi,\quad\dfrac{dy}{d\phi}=\lambda\cos\lambda\phi\), so

\[\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{\lambda\cos\lambda\phi}{\cos\phi}.\]

Since \(\cos\phi=\sqrt{1-x^2}\) and \(\cos\lambda\phi=\sqrt{1-y^2}\) (up to sign, which squaring removes):

\[\sqrt{1-x^2}\,y_1=\lambda\cos\lambda\phi\ \Rightarrow\ (1-x^2)y_1^2=\lambda^2\cos^2\lambda\phi=\lambda^2(1-y^2).\]

Differentiate both sides with respect to \(x\):

\[(1-x^2)\,2y_1y_2-2x\,y_1^2=-2\lambda^2y\,y_1.\]

Divide by \(2y_1\) (\(y_1\ne0\)):

\((1-x^2)\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}-x\dfrac{dy}{dx}+\lambda^2y=0\)  Hence proved.

Concepts to recall

  • Parametric: \(\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{dy/d\phi}{dx/d\phi}\).
  • Square first to clear roots, then differentiate implicitly — much shorter than differentiating the quotient.
  • \(\frac{d}{dx}(y_1^2)=2y_1y_2\).
Q5(b)Applications of Derivatives (maxima and minima)[5]

A cone is inscribed in a sphere of radius \(r\). If the volume of the cone is maximum then find the height of the cone.

Answer

Let the cone have height \(h\) and base radius \(\rho\); its vertex V is on the sphere and its axis passes through the centre O.

In the right triangle formed by O, the base centre M and a point on the base rim: \(OM=h-r\), so

\[\rho^2=r^2-(h-r)^2=2rh-h^2.\]

\[V=\frac13\pi\rho^2h=\frac{\pi}{3}(2rh^2-h^3),\qquad 0<h<2r.\]

\[\frac{dV}{dh}=\frac{\pi}{3}(4rh-3h^2)=0\ \Rightarrow\ h=\frac{4r}{3}\quad(h\ne0).\]

\[\frac{d^2V}{dh^2}=\frac{\pi}{3}(4r-6h)\Big|_{h=4r/3}=-\frac{4\pi r}{3}<0,\]

so V is maximum there.

Height of the cone \(=\dfrac{4r}{3}\)  (maximum volume \(=\frac{32}{81}\pi r^3=\frac{8}{27}\times\) volume of the sphere).

Concepts to recall

  • Express the quantity in one variable using the geometry (Pythagoras in the axial section).
  • \(V_{\text{cone}}=\frac13\pi\rho^2h\).
  • Second-derivative test: \(f''<0\) ⇒ maximum.
Q6(a)Integrals (substitution, partial fractions)[5]

Evaluate: \(\displaystyle\int\frac{\cos x}{(1+\sin x)(2+\sin x)}\,dx\).

Answer

Put \(\sin x=t\), \(\cos x\,dx=dt\):

\[I=\int\frac{dt}{(1+t)(2+t)}.\]

Partial fractions: \(\dfrac{1}{(1+t)(2+t)}=\dfrac{A}{1+t}+\dfrac{B}{2+t}\); \(t=-1\Rightarrow A=1\); \(t=-2\Rightarrow B=-1\).

\[I=\int\left(\frac{1}{1+t}-\frac{1}{2+t}\right)dt=\log|1+t|-\log|2+t|+C.\]

\(I=\log\left|\dfrac{1+\sin x}{2+\sin x}\right|+C\)

Concepts to recall

  • Spot \(f'(x)\) beside \(f(x)\): here \(\cos x\) is \((\sin x)'\).
  • Distinct linear factors: \(\frac{1}{(t-a)(t-b)}=\frac{A}{t-a}+\frac{B}{t-b}\), cover-up rule.
Q6(b)Application of Integrals (area)[5]

Find the area of the region bounded by the curve \(y^2=4x\) and the straight line \(y=2x-4\).

Answer

Points of intersection. From the line, \(x=\frac{y+4}{2}\). Substituting in \(y^2=4x\): \(y^2=2y+8\Rightarrow y^2-2y-8=0\Rightarrow y=4\) or \(y=-2\). The points are \(A(4,4)\) and \(B(1,-2)\).

Integrating with respect to \(y\) (the line is to the right of the parabola for \(-2<y<4\)):

\[\text{Area}=\int_{-2}^{4}\left[\frac{y+4}{2}-\frac{y^2}{4}\right]dy=\left[\frac{y^2}{4}+2y-\frac{y^3}{12}\right]_{-2}^{4}\]

\[=\left(4+8-\tfrac{16}{3}\right)-\left(1-4+\tfrac{2}{3}\right)=\tfrac{20}{3}+\tfrac{7}{3}=9.\]

Area \(=9\) square units

Concepts to recall

  • Find the intersection points first; they are the limits.
  • When the region is bounded left/right, integrate in \(y\): \(\int(x_{\text{right}}-x_{\text{left}})\,dy\).
  • Area is always positive — sketch to see which curve is on the right.
Q8(a)Probability[5]

An urn contains 9 balls, 2 of which are white, 3 blue and 4 black. 3 balls are drawn at random. What is the chance that:

(i) Three balls will be of different colors?
(ii) 2 balls will be of same colors and third of different color?
(iii) 3 balls will be of same color?

Answer

Total number of ways of drawing 3 balls from 9: \(\binom93=84\).

(i) One of each colour: \(\binom21\binom31\binom41=2\cdot3\cdot4=24\).

\[P=\frac{24}{84}=\frac27.\]

(iii) All three the same colour: only blue or black is possible (there are just 2 white): \(\binom33+\binom43=1+4=5\).

\[P=\frac{5}{84}.\]

(ii) Exactly two of one colour and the third different:

\(\binom22\cdot7+\binom32\cdot6+\binom42\cdot5=7+18+30=55\), so \(P=\dfrac{55}{84}\).

(Check: the three cases are exhaustive, \(24+55+5=84\).)

(i) \(\tfrac27\)   (ii) \(\tfrac{55}{84}\)   (iii) \(\tfrac{5}{84}\)

Concepts to recall

  • Classical probability with combinations: favourable \(\binom{}{}\) products over \(\binom{n}{r}\).
  • Mutually exclusive, exhaustive cases must add to the total — a free check.
Q8(b)Probability (independent events)[5]

A and B cut a pack of 52 playing cards alternately and every time the card is put back. The pack is shuffled after each cut. A starts the game and the one who cuts a heart first wins. Find the probability that B wins the game.

Answer

On each cut, \(p=P(\text{heart})=\frac{13}{52}=\frac14\), \(q=\frac34\); cuts are independent (the card is replaced and the pack shuffled).

B wins on the 2nd, 4th, 6th, … cut:

\[P(B)=qp+q^3p+q^5p+\cdots=\frac{qp}{1-q^2}\quad\text{(infinite G.P., ratio }q^2<1).\]

\[P(B)=\frac{\frac34\cdot\frac14}{1-\frac9{16}}=\frac{3/16}{7/16}=\frac37.\]

\(P(\text{B wins})=\dfrac37\)  (and \(P(\text{A wins})=\frac47\)).

Concepts to recall

  • Independent trials: multiply probabilities along the sequence of failures then success.
  • Sum of infinite G.P.: \(\frac{a}{1-r}\), \(|r|<1\).
  • First player: \(\frac{p}{1-q^2}\); second: \(\frac{qp}{1-q^2}\).
Q9(b)Differential Equations (linear)[5]

Solve the differential equation: \(\left(\dfrac{e^{-2\sqrt x}}{\sqrt x}-\dfrac{y}{\sqrt x}\right)\dfrac{dx}{dy}=1,\ (x\ne0)\).

Answer

Rewrite: \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{e^{-2\sqrt x}}{\sqrt x}-\dfrac{y}{\sqrt x}\), i.e.

\[\frac{dy}{dx}+\frac{1}{\sqrt x}\,y=\frac{e^{-2\sqrt x}}{\sqrt x},\]

a linear equation \(\frac{dy}{dx}+Py=Q\) with \(P=\frac1{\sqrt x}\), \(Q=\frac{e^{-2\sqrt x}}{\sqrt x}\).

Integrating factor: \(\int P\,dx=\int x^{-1/2}dx=2\sqrt x\), so \(\text{I.F.}=e^{2\sqrt x}\).

Solution: \(y\cdot\text{I.F.}=\int Q\cdot\text{I.F.}\,dx+C\):

\[y\,e^{2\sqrt x}=\int\frac{e^{-2\sqrt x}}{\sqrt x}\,e^{2\sqrt x}dx=\int x^{-1/2}dx=2\sqrt x+C.\]

\(y\,e^{2\sqrt x}=2\sqrt x+C\)

Concepts to recall

  • Linear form \(\frac{dy}{dx}+Py=Q\): I.F. \(=e^{\int P\,dx}\), solution \(y\cdot\text{I.F.}=\int Q\cdot\text{I.F.}\,dx+C\).
  • Turn \(\frac{dx}{dy}\) upside-down first to see which variable is dependent.

Q11(a)Three-dimensional Geometry (planes)[5]

Find the vector equation of the plane containing the line \(\dfrac{x+2}{2}=\dfrac{y+3}{3}=\dfrac{z-4}{-2}\) and the point \((0,6,0)\).

Answer

The line passes through \(A(-2,-3,4)\) with direction \(\vec b=2\hat i+3\hat j-2\hat k\). The plane also contains \(P(0,6,0)\), so it contains \(\overrightarrow{AP}=2\hat i+9\hat j-4\hat k\).

A normal to the plane is perpendicular to both:

\[\vec n=\vec b\times\overrightarrow{AP}=\begin{vmatrix}\hat i&\hat j&\hat k\\2&3&-2\\2&9&-4\end{vmatrix}\]

\[\vec n=(-12+18)\hat i-(-8+4)\hat j+(18-6)\hat k=6\hat i+4\hat j+12\hat k.\]

Take \(\vec n=3\hat i+2\hat j+6\hat k\). The plane through \(P\): \(\vec r\cdot\vec n=\vec p\cdot\vec n=0+12+0=12\).

\(\vec r\cdot(3\hat i+2\hat j+6\hat k)=12\)  (Cartesian: \(3x+2y+6z=12\)).

Check with A: \(-6-6+24=12\) ✓.

Concepts to recall

  • A plane through a line and a point: normal \(=\) line direction \(\times\) (vector from a point of the line to the given point).
  • Plane through \(\vec a\) with normal \(\vec n\): \(\vec r\cdot\vec n=\vec a\cdot\vec n\).
Q11(b)Three-dimensional Geometry (lines)[5]

Find the vector equation of the line passing through the point \((2,3,2)\) and parallel to the line \(\vec r=-2\hat i+3\hat j+\lambda(2\hat i-3\hat j+6\hat k)\) and find the distance between them.

Answer

Parallel lines have the same direction \(\vec b=2\hat i-3\hat j+6\hat k\). Required line:

\(\vec r=2\hat i+3\hat j+2\hat k+\mu(2\hat i-3\hat j+6\hat k)\)

Distance between the parallel lines \(\vec r=\vec a_1+\lambda\vec b\) and \(\vec r=\vec a_2+\mu\vec b\):

\[d=\frac{|(\vec a_2-\vec a_1)\times\vec b|}{|\vec b|}\]

Here \(\vec a_1=-2\hat i+3\hat j\), \(\vec a_2=2\hat i+3\hat j+2\hat k\), so \(\vec a_2-\vec a_1=4\hat i+0\hat j+2\hat k\).

\[(\vec a_2-\vec a_1)\times\vec b=\begin{vmatrix}\hat i&\hat j&\hat k\\4&0&2\\2&-3&6\end{vmatrix}=(0+6)\hat i-(24-4)\hat j+(-12-0)\hat k\]

\[=6\hat i-20\hat j-12\hat k.\]

\(|6\hat i-20\hat j-12\hat k|=\sqrt{36+400+144}=\sqrt{580}=2\sqrt{145}\); \(|\vec b|=\sqrt{4+9+36}=7\).

\(d=\dfrac{2\sqrt{145}}{7}\approx3.44\) units

Concepts to recall

  • Line through \(\vec a\) parallel to \(\vec b\): \(\vec r=\vec a+\lambda\vec b\).
  • Distance between parallel lines: \(\frac{|(\vec a_2-\vec a_1)\times\vec b|}{|\vec b|}\).
Q12(a)Probability (Bayes' theorem)[5]

In a bolt factory machines \(M_1,M_2\) and \(M_3\) manufacture respectively 25, 35, and 40 percent of the total of their output, out of which 5, 4 and 2 percent respectively are defective bolts. One bolt is drawn at random from the product and is found defective. What is the probability that it is manufactured in the machine \(M_2\)?

Answer

Let \(E_1,E_2,E_3\): the bolt is made by \(M_1,M_2,M_3\); \(D\): the bolt is defective.

\(P(E_1)=0.25,\ P(E_2)=0.35,\ P(E_3)=0.40\); \(P(D|E_1)=0.05,\ P(D|E_2)=0.04,\ P(D|E_3)=0.02\).

Total probability: \(P(D)=0.25(0.05)+0.35(0.04)+0.40(0.02)=0.0125+0.0140+0.0080=0.0345\).

By Bayes' theorem,

\[P(E_2\mid D)=\frac{P(E_2)P(D|E_2)}{\sum P(E_i)P(D|E_i)}=\frac{0.0140}{0.0345}=\frac{140}{345}=\frac{28}{69}.\]

\(P(M_2\mid\text{defective})=\dfrac{28}{69}\approx0.406\)

Concepts to recall

  • Bayes: \(P(E_i|A)=\dfrac{P(E_i)P(A|E_i)}{\sum_j P(E_j)P(A|E_j)}\).
  • The denominator is the total probability of the observed event.

Q14(a)Linear Programming[5]

A company produces soft drinks that has a contract which requires that a minimum of 80 units of chemical A and 60 units of chemical B to go into each bottle of the drink. The chemicals are available in a prepared mixture from two different suppliers. Supplier S has a mix of 4 units of A and 2 units of B that cost Rs 10, and supplier T has a mix of 1 unit of A and 1 unit of B that cost Rs 4. How many mixes from S and T should the company purchase to honor contract requirement and yet minimize cost?

Answer

Formulation. Let \(x\) mixes be bought from S and \(y\) from T.

Minimise \(Z=10x+4y\) subject to

\[4x+y\ge80\ \text{(chemical A)},\qquad 2x+y\ge60\ \text{(chemical B)},\qquad x\ge0,\ y\ge0.\]

Graph. \(4x+y=80\) meets the axes at \((20,0),(0,80)\); \(2x+y=60\) at \((30,0),(0,60)\). Both inequalities are \(\ge\), so the feasible region lies on the side away from the origin (O does not satisfy them). The lines meet where \(2x=20\): \(x=10,\ y=40\).

The feasible region is unbounded with corner points \(A(0,80),\ B(10,40),\ C(30,0)\).

Corner\(Z=10x+4y\)
A(0, 80)320
B(10, 40)260
C(30, 0)300

Since the region is unbounded, check the open half-plane \(10x+4y<260\): it has no point in common with the feasible region (the dashed line touches the region only at B). So 260 is the minimum.

Buy 10 mixes from S and 40 from T; minimum cost Rs 260

Concepts to recall

  • Formulate: decision variables, objective, constraints, non-negativity.
  • Corner-point method; for an unbounded region confirm the optimum by checking the half-plane \(Z<Z_{\min}\) (or \(>Z_{\max}\)).
  • 'Minimum of … units' gives \(\ge\) constraints.
Q14(b)Applications of Derivatives (maxima and minima)[5]

The cost function of a firm is \(c(x)=5x^2+28x+5\) where 'c' is the cost & 'x' is the level of output. A tax at the rate of Rs 2 per unit output is imposed and the producer adds it to his cost. Find the minimum value of the average cost.

Answer

With the tax of Rs 2 per unit added, the new total cost is

\[C(x)=5x^2+28x+5+2x=5x^2+30x+5.\]

Average cost: \[AC(x)=\frac{C(x)}{x}=5x+30+\frac{5}{x},\qquad x>0.\]

\[\frac{d(AC)}{dx}=5-\frac{5}{x^2}=0\ \Rightarrow\ x^2=1\ \Rightarrow\ x=1\quad(x>0).\]

\[\frac{d^2(AC)}{dx^2}=\frac{10}{x^3}=10>0\ \text{at}\ x=1,\]

so AC is minimum at \(x=1\): \(AC(1)=5+30+5=40\).

Minimum average cost \(=\) Rs 40 (at output \(x=1\) unit)

Concepts to recall

  • Average cost \(AC=\dfrac{C(x)}{x}\); a per-unit tax \(t\) adds \(tx\) to \(C(x)\).
  • Stationary point: \(\frac{d}{dx}(AC)=0\); second-derivative test \(>0\Rightarrow\) minimum.
  • Only \(x>0\) is meaningful for an output.
