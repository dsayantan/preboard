Q1(i)Matrices[3]

Show that \(A=\begin{bmatrix}2&-3\\3&4\end{bmatrix}\) satisfies the equation \(x^2-6x+17I=0\). Hence find \(A^{-1}\).

Answer

\[A^2=\begin{bmatrix}2&-3\\3&4\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}2&-3\\3&4\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}4-9&-6-12\\6+12&-9+16\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}-5&-18\\18&7\end{bmatrix}.\]

\[A^2-6A+17I=\begin{bmatrix}-5&-18\\18&7\end{bmatrix}-\begin{bmatrix}12&-18\\18&24\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}17&0\\0&17\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\0&0\end{bmatrix}.\]

So \(A\) satisfies \(x^2-6x+17I=0\). Since \(|A|=8+9=17\ne0\), \(A^{-1}\) exists. Multiply \(A^2-6A+17I=O\) by \(A^{-1}\):

\[A-6I+17A^{-1}=O\ \Rightarrow\ A^{-1}=\frac{1}{17}(6I-A)=\frac1{17}\begin{bmatrix}6-2&3\\-3&6-4\end{bmatrix}.\]

\(A^{-1}=\dfrac{1}{17}\begin{bmatrix}4&3\\-3&2\end{bmatrix}\)

Concepts to recall

  • A matrix 'satisfies' a polynomial when substituting \(A\) (with \(I\) for the constant) gives \(O\).
  • Multiplying the equation by \(A^{-1}\) gives the inverse without cofactors.
  • For 2×2: \(A^{-1}=\frac1{|A|}\begin{bmatrix}d&-b\\-c&a\end{bmatrix}\) — a check.
Q1(iii)Inverse Trigonometric Functions[3]

Solve for x: \(\sin(2\tan^{-1}x)=1\).

Answer

Let \(\tan^{-1}x=\theta\), so \(x=\tan\theta\), \(-\frac{\pi}{2}<\theta<\frac{\pi}{2}\).

\[\sin2\theta=\frac{2\tan\theta}{1+\tan^2\theta}=\frac{2x}{1+x^2}.\]

\[\frac{2x}{1+x^2}=1\ \Rightarrow\ x^2-2x+1=0\ \Rightarrow\ (x-1)^2=0\ \Rightarrow\ x=1.\]

Check: \(\sin(2\tan^{-1}1)=\sin\frac{\pi}{2}=1\). ✓

\(x=1\)

Concepts to recall

  • \(\sin(2\tan^{-1}x)=\dfrac{2x}{1+x^2}\) (i.e. \(2\tan^{-1}x=\sin^{-1}\frac{2x}{1+x^2}\) for \(|x|\le1\)).
  • Principal range of \(\tan^{-1}\): \((-\frac\pi2,\frac\pi2)\).
Q1(v)Integrals (substitution)[3]

\(\displaystyle\int\frac{(x+1)e^x}{\cot^2(xe^x)}\,dx\).

Answer

Put \(xe^x=t\); then \(\dfrac{dt}{dx}=e^x+xe^x=(x+1)e^x\), so \((x+1)e^x\,dx=dt\).

\[I=\int\frac{dt}{\cot^2t}=\int\tan^2t\,dt=\int(\sec^2t-1)\,dt=\tan t-t+C.\]

\(I=\tan(xe^x)-xe^x+C\)

Concepts to recall

  • \(\frac{d}{dx}(xe^x)=(x+1)e^x\) — the numerator is the derivative of the inner function.
  • \(\tan^2t=\sec^2t-1\).
Q1(ix)Probability[3]

The probability of an event A occurring is \(\dfrac23\) and the probability of an event B occurring is \(\dfrac49\). If the probability of getting success in atleast one of the two events is \(\dfrac45\), what is the probability of success in both the events?

Answer

Given \(P(A)=\frac23,\ P(B)=\frac49,\ P(A\cup B)=\frac45\). By the addition theorem,

\[P(A\cap B)=P(A)+P(B)-P(A\cup B)=\frac23+\frac49-\frac45=\frac{30+20-36}{45}.\]

\(P(A\cap B)=\dfrac{14}{45}\)

(Note \(P(A)P(B)=\frac{8}{27}\ne\frac{14}{45}\), so A and B are not independent — the product rule must not be used here.)

Concepts to recall

  • 'At least one' = \(A\cup B\); 'both' = \(A\cap B\).
  • \(P(A\cup B)=P(A)+P(B)-P(A\cap B)\).
  • \(P(A\cap B)=P(A)P(B)\) only for independent events.
Q1(x)Differential Equations (variable separable)[3]

Solve the differential equation: \(y-x\dfrac{dy}{dx}=a\left(y^2+\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right)\).

Answer

Collect the \(\frac{dy}{dx}\) terms: \(y-ay^2=(x+a)\dfrac{dy}{dx}\), so the variables separate:

\[\frac{dy}{y(1-ay)}=\frac{dx}{x+a}.\]

Partial fractions: \(\dfrac{1}{y(1-ay)}=\dfrac1y+\dfrac{a}{1-ay}\). Integrating,

\[\log|y|-\log|1-ay|=\log|x+a|+\log|C|\ \Rightarrow\ \frac{y}{1-ay}=C(x+a).\]

\(y=C(x+a)(1-ay)\)  (C an arbitrary constant)

Concepts to recall

  • Bring every \(\frac{dy}{dx}\) term to one side; if what remains factorises as \(f(x)g(y)\), separate.
  • \(\int\frac{a\,dy}{1-ay}=-\log|1-ay|\).
  • Write the constant as \(\log|C|\) to combine logs.
Q2(a)Determinants[5]

Using properties of determinants, show that

\[\begin{vmatrix}(b+c)^2&a^2&a^2\\b^2&(c+a)^2&b^2\\c^2&c^2&(a+b)^2\end{vmatrix}=2abc(a+b+c)^3\]

Answer

Let \(\Delta\) be the determinant. Apply \(C_2\to C_2-C_1,\ C_3\to C_3-C_1\) and use \(p^2-q^2=(p-q)(p+q)\):

\[\Delta=\begin{vmatrix}(b+c)^2&(a-b-c)(a+b+c)&(a-b-c)(a+b+c)\\b^2&(c+a-b)(a+b+c)&0\\c^2&0&(a+b-c)(a+b+c)\end{vmatrix}\]

Take \((a+b+c)\) common from \(C_2\) and from \(C_3\):

\[\Delta=(a+b+c)^2\begin{vmatrix}(b+c)^2&a-b-c&a-b-c\\b^2&c+a-b&0\\c^2&0&a+b-c\end{vmatrix}\]

Apply \(R_1\to R_1-R_2-R_3\): the entries become \((b+c)^2-b^2-c^2=2bc\), \((a-b-c)-(c+a-b)=-2c\), \((a-b-c)-(a+b-c)=-2b\):

\[\Delta=(a+b+c)^2\begin{vmatrix}2bc&-2c&-2b\\b^2&c+a-b&0\\c^2&0&a+b-c\end{vmatrix}\]

Multiply \(C_2\) by \(b\) and \(C_3\) by \(c\), dividing outside by \(bc\) (for \(b,c\ne0\)):

\[\Delta=\frac{(a+b+c)^2}{bc}\begin{vmatrix}2bc&-2bc&-2bc\\b^2&b(c+a-b)&0\\c^2&0&c(a+b-c)\end{vmatrix}\]

Apply \(C_2\to C_2+C_1,\ C_3\to C_3+C_1\):

\[\Delta=\frac{(a+b+c)^2}{bc}\begin{vmatrix}2bc&0&0\\b^2&b(c+a)&b^2\\c^2&c^2&c(a+b)\end{vmatrix}\]

Expand along \(R_1\):

\[\begin{aligned}\Delta&=\frac{(a+b+c)^2}{bc}\cdot2bc\big[bc(c+a)(a+b)-b^2c^2\big]\\&=2bc\,(a+b+c)^2\big[(c+a)(a+b)-bc\big]\\&=2bc\,(a+b+c)^2(a^2+ab+ac)=2abc\,(a+b+c)^3.\end{aligned}\]

\(\Delta=2abc(a+b+c)^3\)  Hence proved.

(Both sides are polynomials in \(a,b,c\), so the identity proved for \(b,c\ne0\) holds for all values.)

Concepts to recall

  • Subtracting columns turns \(p^2-q^2\) entries into factors \((p-q)(p+q)\) that can be taken out.
  • \(R_1\to R_1-R_2-R_3\) and \(C_j\to C_j+C_1\) do not change the value; multiplying a column by \(k\) multiplies \(\Delta\) by \(k\).
  • Aim for a row with two zeros, then expand.
Q2(b)Matrices and Determinants[5]

Compute \(A^{-1}\) for the matrix \(A=\begin{bmatrix}0&1&2\\1&2&3\\3&1&1\end{bmatrix}\). Hence solve the system of equations

\(y+2z+8=0,\ x+2y+3z+14=0,\ 3x+y+z+8=0\).

Answer

\(|A|=0(2-3)-1(1-9)+2(1-6)=0+8-10=-2\ne0\).

Cofactors: \(A_{11}=-1,\ A_{12}=8,\ A_{13}=-5;\ A_{21}=1,\ A_{22}=-6,\ A_{23}=3;\ A_{31}=-1,\ A_{32}=2,\ A_{33}=-1.\)

\[\operatorname{adj}A=\begin{bmatrix}-1&1&-1\\8&-6&2\\-5&3&-1\end{bmatrix},\qquad A^{-1}=-\frac12\begin{bmatrix}-1&1&-1\\8&-6&2\\-5&3&-1\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac12&-\tfrac12&\tfrac12\\-4&3&-1\\\tfrac52&-\tfrac32&\tfrac12\end{bmatrix}.\]

The equations are \(y+2z=-8,\ x+2y+3z=-14,\ 3x+y+z=-8\), i.e. \(AX=B\) with \(B=\begin{bmatrix}-8\\-14\\-8\end{bmatrix}\). Then \(X=A^{-1}B\):

\[X=-\frac12\begin{bmatrix}8-14+8\\-64+84-16\\40-42+8\end{bmatrix}=-\frac12\begin{bmatrix}2\\4\\6\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}-1\\-2\\-3\end{bmatrix}.\]

\(x=-1,\ y=-2,\ z=-3\)

Check: \(-2-6+8=0\) ✓; \(-1-4-9+14=0\) ✓; \(-3-2-3+8=0\) ✓.

Concepts to recall

  • Move the constants to the right before writing \(AX=B\) (here \(B\) is negative).
  • \(A^{-1}=\frac{\operatorname{adj}A}{|A|}\); \(X=A^{-1}B\).
  • Substitute back into the original equations.
Q3(a)Inverse Trigonometric Functions[5]

Prove that \(\sin^{-1}\dfrac45+\cos^{-1}\dfrac{2}{\sqrt5}=\cot^{-1}\dfrac{2}{11}\).

Answer

Let \(\alpha=\sin^{-1}\frac45\): \(\sin\alpha=\frac45,\ \cos\alpha=\frac35\), so \(\alpha=\tan^{-1}\frac43\).

Let \(\beta=\cos^{-1}\frac2{\sqrt5}\): \(\cos\beta=\frac2{\sqrt5},\ \sin\beta=\frac1{\sqrt5}\), so \(\beta=\tan^{-1}\frac12\).

Since \(\frac43\cdot\frac12=\frac23<1\),

\[\alpha+\beta=\tan^{-1}\frac{\frac43+\frac12}{1-\frac43\cdot\frac12}=\tan^{-1}\frac{11/6}{1/3}=\tan^{-1}\frac{11}{2}.\]

As \(\frac{11}{2}>0\), \(\tan^{-1}\frac{11}{2}=\cot^{-1}\frac{2}{11}\).

\(\sin^{-1}\frac45+\cos^{-1}\frac{2}{\sqrt5}=\cot^{-1}\frac{2}{11}\)  Hence proved.

Concepts to recall

  • Convert every term to \(\tan^{-1}\) using a right triangle.
  • \(\tan^{-1}a+\tan^{-1}b=\tan^{-1}\frac{a+b}{1-ab}\) for \(ab<1\).
  • \(\tan^{-1}x=\cot^{-1}\frac1x\) for \(x>0\).
Q5(a)Differentiation (second order)[5]

If \(y=(\cos^{-1}x)^2\), prove that \((1-x^2)\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}-x\dfrac{dy}{dx}=2\). Hence find the value of \(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}\) at \(x=0\).

[No mark is printed against 5(a); it is taken as 5, like every other Section A part.]

Answer

\[y_1=\frac{dy}{dx}=2\cos^{-1}x\cdot\left(-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\right)\ \Rightarrow\ \sqrt{1-x^2}\,y_1=-2\cos^{-1}x.\]

Squaring: \((1-x^2)y_1^2=4(\cos^{-1}x)^2=4y\).

Differentiate with respect to \(x\):

\[(1-x^2)\,2y_1y_2-2x\,y_1^2=4y_1.\]

Divide by \(2y_1\) (\(y_1\ne0\)):

\((1-x^2)\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}-x\dfrac{dy}{dx}=2\)  Hence proved.

At \(x=0\): \((1-0)y_2-0\cdot y_1=2\), so \(\left.\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}\right|_{x=0}=2\)

Concepts to recall

  • \(\frac{d}{dx}\cos^{-1}x=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\).
  • Clear the root by squaring before the second differentiation.
  • At \(x=0\) the \(x\,y_1\) term vanishes, so \(y_1(0)\) is not needed.
Q5(b)Applications of Derivatives (maxima and minima)[5]

An open box with a square base is to be made out of a given quantity of sheet of area \(a^2\). Show that the maximum volume of the box is \(a^3\).

Answer

[The paper's stated answer is a misprint: the maximum volume works out to \(\dfrac{a^3}{6\sqrt3}\), the standard result for this problem. It cannot be \(a^3\): a box made from sheet of area \(a^2\) has every edge shorter than \(a\), so its volume is less than \(a^3\).]

Let the side of the square base be \(x\) and the height \(h\). The box is open, so the sheet used is base + four sides:

\[x^2+4xh=a^2\ \Rightarrow\ h=\frac{a^2-x^2}{4x}.\]

\[V=x^2h=\frac{x(a^2-x^2)}{4}=\frac{a^2x-x^3}{4},\qquad 0<x<a.\]

\[\frac{dV}{dx}=\frac{a^2-3x^2}{4}=0\ \Rightarrow\ x=\frac{a}{\sqrt3}.\qquad \frac{d^2V}{dx^2}=-\frac{3x}{2}<0,\]

so V is maximum at \(x=\frac{a}{\sqrt3}\), where \(h=\dfrac{a^2-a^2/3}{4a/\sqrt3}=\dfrac{a}{2\sqrt3}=\dfrac x2\).

\[V_{\max}=x^2h=\frac{a^2}{3}\cdot\frac{a}{2\sqrt3}=\frac{a^3}{6\sqrt3}.\]

Maximum volume \(=\dfrac{a^3}{6\sqrt3}\) (when the height is half the side of the base)

Concepts to recall

  • Use the fixed quantity (area of sheet) to eliminate one variable.
  • Open box: surface \(=x^2+4xh\) (no lid).
  • Second-derivative test for the maximum; state the dimensions too.
Q6(a)Definite Integrals (properties)[5]

Evaluate the integral: \(\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi}\frac{x\tan x}{\sec x+\tan x}\,dx\).

Answer

Let \(I=\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi}\frac{x\tan x}{\sec x+\tan x}dx\). Using \(\int_0^af(x)dx=\int_0^af(a-x)dx\) with \(\tan(\pi-x)=-\tan x,\ \sec(\pi-x)=-\sec x\):

\[I=\int_0^{\pi}\frac{(\pi-x)(-\tan x)}{-\sec x-\tan x}dx=\int_0^{\pi}\frac{(\pi-x)\tan x}{\sec x+\tan x}dx.\]

Adding the two forms of \(I\):

\[2I=\pi\int_0^{\pi}\frac{\tan x}{\sec x+\tan x}dx=\pi\int_0^{\pi}\frac{\sin x}{1+\sin x}dx=\pi\int_0^{\pi}\left(1-\frac{1}{1+\sin x}\right)dx.\]

Now \(\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi}\frac{dx}{1+\sin x}=2\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{dx}{1+\sin x}\) (since \(\sin(\pi-x)=\sin x\)), and

\[\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{dx}{1+\sin x}=\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{dx}{1+\cos x}=\int_0^{\pi/2}\tfrac12\sec^2\tfrac x2\,dx=\Big[\tan\tfrac x2\Big]_0^{\pi/2}=1.\]

(using \(\int_0^{a}f(x)dx=\int_0^af(a-x)dx\) with \(a=\frac\pi2\)). So \(\int_0^{\pi}\frac{dx}{1+\sin x}=2\) and

\[2I=\pi(\pi-2)\ \Rightarrow\ I=\frac{\pi(\pi-2)}{2}.\]

\(I=\dfrac{\pi(\pi-2)}{2}=\dfrac{\pi^2}{2}-\pi\)

Concepts to recall

  • \(\int_0^af(x)dx=\int_0^af(a-x)dx\) — removes the factor \(x\) when \(f(a-x)=f(x)\).
  • \(\int_0^{2a}f(x)dx=2\int_0^af(x)dx\) if \(f(2a-x)=f(x)\).
  • \(1+\cos x=2\cos^2\frac x2\).
Q6(b)Application of Integrals (area)[5]

Find the area bounded by the curve \(x=8+2y-y^2\), the y-axis and the lines \(y=-1,\ y=3\).

Answer

\(x=8+2y-y^2=9-(y-1)^2\) is a parabola opening to the left with vertex \((9,1)\). It meets \(y=-1\) and \(y=3\) at \(x=5\), so \(x>0\) for \(-1\le y\le3\): the region lies to the right of the y-axis.

\[\text{Area}=\int_{-1}^{3}x\,dy=\int_{-1}^{3}(8+2y-y^2)\,dy=\Big[8y+y^2-\frac{y^3}{3}\Big]_{-1}^{3}\]

\[=(24+9-9)-\left(-8+1+\frac13\right)=24+\frac{20}{3}=\frac{92}{3}.\]

Area \(=\dfrac{92}{3}\) square units \((\approx30.67)\)

Concepts to recall

  • Area between a curve \(x=g(y)\), the y-axis and \(y=c,\ y=d\): \(\int_c^d|g(y)|\,dy\).
  • Complete the square to find the vertex and check the sign of \(x\) on the interval.
Q8(a)Probability (independent events)[5]

Two persons throw a die, alternately till one of them gets a multiple of 3 and wins the game. Find their respective probability of winning.

Answer

On each throw \(p=P(3\text{ or }6)=\frac26=\frac13\), \(q=\frac23\); throws are independent.

The first thrower wins on the 1st, 3rd, 5th, … throw:

\[P_1=p+q^2p+q^4p+\cdots=\frac{p}{1-q^2}=\frac{1/3}{1-4/9}=\frac{1/3}{5/9}=\frac35.\]

The second thrower wins on the 2nd, 4th, … throw:

\[P_2=qp+q^3p+\cdots=\frac{qp}{1-q^2}=\frac{2/9}{5/9}=\frac25.\]

First thrower \(\tfrac35\), second thrower \(\tfrac25\)  (they add to 1: the game ends with probability 1).

Concepts to recall

  • Multiples of 3 on a die: 3 and 6.
  • Alternate trials: first player \(\frac{p}{1-q^2}\), second \(\frac{qp}{1-q^2}\).
  • Infinite G.P. \(a+ar+\cdots=\frac{a}{1-r}\).
Q8(b)Probability (independent events)[5]

A bag contains 4 white and 2 black balls. Another contains 3 white and 5 black balls. If one ball is drawn from each bag, find the probability that i) both are white ii) both are black iii) one is white and one is black.

Answer

Bag I: \(P(W_1)=\frac46=\frac23,\ P(B_1)=\frac26=\frac13\). Bag II: \(P(W_2)=\frac38,\ P(B_2)=\frac58\). Draws from different bags are independent.

(i) \(P(W_1\cap W_2)=\frac23\cdot\frac38=\frac14\).

(ii) \(P(B_1\cap B_2)=\frac13\cdot\frac58=\frac{5}{24}\).

(iii) \(P(W_1B_2)+P(B_1W_2)=\frac23\cdot\frac58+\frac13\cdot\frac38=\frac{10}{24}+\frac{3}{24}=\frac{13}{24}\).

Check: \(\frac{6}{24}+\frac{5}{24}+\frac{13}{24}=1\).

(i) \(\tfrac14\)   (ii) \(\tfrac5{24}\)   (iii) \(\tfrac{13}{24}\)

Concepts to recall

  • Independent events: \(P(A\cap B)=P(A)P(B)\).
  • 'One white and one black' has two mutually exclusive orders — add them.
Q9(b)Differential Equations[5]

Solve the differential equation \((1-x^2)\dfrac{dy}{dx}-xy=x\), given \(y=2\) when \(x=0\).

Answer

\((1-x^2)\dfrac{dy}{dx}=x(y+1)\), so the variables separate (for \(|x|<1\), which contains \(x=0\)):

\[\frac{dy}{y+1}=\frac{x\,dx}{1-x^2}.\]

Integrate: \(\log|y+1|=-\frac12\log(1-x^2)+\log C\ \Rightarrow\ y+1=\dfrac{C}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\).

At \(x=0,\ y=2\): \(3=C\).

\(y=\dfrac{3}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}-1\)

(It can equally be solved as a linear equation \(\frac{dy}{dx}-\frac{x}{1-x^2}y=\frac{x}{1-x^2}\) with I.F. \(\sqrt{1-x^2}\): \(y\sqrt{1-x^2}=-\sqrt{1-x^2}+C\), the same result.)

Concepts to recall

  • Factor the right side before reaching for the linear method — here it separates.
  • \(\int\frac{x\,dx}{1-x^2}=-\frac12\log|1-x^2|\).
  • Use the initial condition to fix C.

Q11(a)Three-dimensional Geometry (shortest distance)[5]

The vector equation of two straight lines are \(\vec r_1=\hat i+2\hat j+\hat k+\lambda(\hat i-\hat j+\hat k)\), \(\vec r_2=2\hat i-\hat j-\hat k+\mu(2\hat i+\hat j+\hat k)\). Find the shortest distance between the two lines.

Answer

\(\vec a_1=\hat i+2\hat j+\hat k,\ \vec b_1=\hat i-\hat j+\hat k\); \(\vec a_2=2\hat i-\hat j-\hat k,\ \vec b_2=2\hat i+\hat j+\hat k\). The directions are not parallel.

\[\vec b_1\times\vec b_2=\begin{vmatrix}\hat i&\hat j&\hat k\\1&-1&1\\2&1&1\end{vmatrix}=(-1-1)\hat i-(1-2)\hat j+(1+2)\hat k=-2\hat i+\hat j+3\hat k,\]

\(|\vec b_1\times\vec b_2|=\sqrt{4+1+9}=\sqrt{14}\); \(\vec a_2-\vec a_1=\hat i-3\hat j-2\hat k\).

\[d=\frac{|(\vec a_2-\vec a_1)\cdot(\vec b_1\times\vec b_2)|}{|\vec b_1\times\vec b_2|}=\frac{|-2-3-6|}{\sqrt{14}}=\frac{11}{\sqrt{14}}.\]

Shortest distance \(=\dfrac{11}{\sqrt{14}}=\dfrac{11\sqrt{14}}{14}\approx2.94\) units  (non-zero, so the lines are skew).

Concepts to recall

  • Skew lines: \(d=\dfrac{|(\vec a_2-\vec a_1)\cdot(\vec b_1\times\vec b_2)|}{|\vec b_1\times\vec b_2|}\).
  • \(d=0\) means the lines intersect.
Q11(b)Three-dimensional Geometry (planes)[5]

Find the equation of the plane passing through the line of intersection of the planes \(2x-y=0\) and \(3z-y=0\) and perpendicular to the plane \(4x+5y-3z=0\).

Answer

Normals: \(\vec n_1=2\hat i-\hat j\) and \(\vec n_2=-\hat j+3\hat k\). The line of intersection is perpendicular to both, so its direction is

\[\vec n_1\times\vec n_2=\begin{vmatrix}\hat i&\hat j&\hat k\\2&-1&0\\0&-1&3\end{vmatrix}=-3\hat i-6\hat j-2\hat k\ \parallel\ 3\hat i+6\hat j+2\hat k.\]

Both planes pass through the origin, so the line passes through O.

The required plane contains this line, and is perpendicular to \(4x+5y-3z=0\), so it contains the direction \(4\hat i+5\hat j-3\hat k\). Its normal is

\[\vec n=(3\hat i+6\hat j+2\hat k)\times(4\hat i+5\hat j-3\hat k)=\begin{vmatrix}\hat i&\hat j&\hat k\\3&6&2\\4&5&-3\end{vmatrix}=-28\hat i+17\hat j-9\hat k.\]

Through O with normal \(28\hat i-17\hat j+9\hat k\):

\(28x-17y+9z=0\)  (vector form \(\vec r\cdot(28\hat i-17\hat j+9\hat k)=0\))

Check: \(28(4)-17(5)+9(-3)=112-85-27=0\) ✓ (perpendicular); a point of the line, \((3,6,2)\): \(84-102+18=0\) ✓.

(Equivalently, \(2x-y+\lambda(3z-y)=0\) with \(8-5(1+\lambda)-9\lambda=0\) gives \(\lambda=\frac{3}{14}\) and the same plane.)

Concepts to recall

  • Line of intersection of two planes has direction \(\vec n_1\times\vec n_2\).
  • Plane ⟂ to a given plane contains that plane's normal.
  • Normal = cross product of two directions lying in the plane.
Q12(a)Probability (conditional probability)[5]

In an examination, 30% of the students have failed in Mathematics, 20% have failed in Chemistry and 10% have failed in both the subjects. A student is selected at random.

i) What is the probability that the student has failed in Mathematics if it is known that he has failed in Chemistry?
ii) What is the probability that the student has failed either in Mathematics or in Chemistry?

Answer

Let M: failed in Mathematics, C: failed in Chemistry. \(P(M)=0.3,\ P(C)=0.2,\ P(M\cap C)=0.1\).

(i) \[P(M\mid C)=\frac{P(M\cap C)}{P(C)}=\frac{0.1}{0.2}=\frac12.\]

(ii) \[P(M\cup C)=P(M)+P(C)-P(M\cap C)=0.3+0.2-0.1=0.4.\]

(i) \(\tfrac12\)   (ii) \(0.4\)

(If 'either … or' is read as exactly one of the two, the probability is \(P(M\cup C)-P(M\cap C)=0.3\).)

Concepts to recall

  • \(P(A\mid B)=\dfrac{P(A\cap B)}{P(B)}\).
  • Addition theorem: \(P(A\cup B)=P(A)+P(B)-P(A\cap B)\).
Q12(b)Probability (Bayes' theorem)[5]

Three urns are given. Urn 1 contains 6 red and 4 black balls, Urn 2 contains 2 red and 6 black balls and Urn 3 contains 1 red and 8 black balls. An urn is chosen at random and a ball is drawn. The ball drawn is black. Find the probability that the ball is drawn either from Urn 2 or from Urn 3.

Answer

Let \(E_i\): urn \(i\) is chosen, \(P(E_i)=\frac13\); B: the ball is black.

\(P(B|E_1)=\frac4{10},\ P(B|E_2)=\frac68,\ P(B|E_3)=\frac89\).

\[P(B)=\frac13\left(\frac25+\frac34+\frac89\right)=\frac13\cdot\frac{72+135+160}{180}=\frac{367}{540}.\]

By Bayes' theorem, since \(E_2,E_3\) are mutually exclusive,

\[P(E_2\cup E_3\mid B)=\frac{\frac13\cdot\frac34+\frac13\cdot\frac89}{\frac13\left(\frac25+\frac34+\frac89\right)}=\frac{135+160}{72+135+160}=\frac{295}{367}.\]

\(P(\text{Urn 2 or Urn 3}\mid\text{black})=\dfrac{295}{367}\approx0.804\)

(Equivalently \(1-P(E_1|B)=1-\frac{72}{367}\).)

Concepts to recall

  • Bayes: \(P(E_i|B)=\dfrac{P(E_i)P(B|E_i)}{\sum P(E_j)P(B|E_j)}\).
  • For 'urn 2 or urn 3', add the two posterior probabilities (or take 1 − P(urn 1 | B)).
  • Use a common denominator (180) to keep the fractions exact.

Q13(b)Applications of Derivatives (rate measure; increasing and decreasing functions)[5]

The average cost function for a commodity is given by \(AC=x+5+\dfrac{36}{x}\) in terms of output \(x\).

Find: (i) the total cost function and marginal cost function.
(ii) the outputs for which AC increases.

Answer

(i) Total cost \(=\) output \(\times\) average cost:

\[C(x)=x\cdot AC=x\left(x+5+\frac{36}{x}\right)=x^2+5x+36.\]

Marginal cost is the rate of change of total cost with output:

\[MC=\frac{dC}{dx}=2x+5.\]

\(C(x)=x^2+5x+36,\quad MC=2x+5\)

(ii) \[\frac{d(AC)}{dx}=1-\frac{36}{x^2}=\frac{x^2-36}{x^2}=\frac{(x-6)(x+6)}{x^2}.\]

For \(x>0\) (output) the sign is that of \(x-6\): \(\frac{d(AC)}{dx}>0\) for \(x>6\) and \(<0\) for \(0<x<6\).

AC increases for \(x>6\)  (it decreases for \(0<x<6\); its minimum is \(AC(6)=17\)).

Concepts to recall

  • \(C(x)=x\cdot AC(x)\); marginal cost \(MC=\dfrac{dC}{dx}\) (rate of change of cost).
  • \(f\) is increasing where \(f'(x)>0\), decreasing where \(f'(x)<0\).
  • Output \(x\) is positive, so \(x=-6\) is discarded.
Q15(b)Linear Programming[5]

A manufacturer produces nuts and bolts for industrial machinery. It takes 1 hour of work on machine A and 3 hours on machine B to produce a package of nuts while it takes 3 hours on machine A and 1 hour on machine B to produce a package of bolts. He earns a profit of Rs 2.50 per package of nuts and Re 1.00 per package of bolts. How many packages of each should he produce each day so as to maximize his profit, if he operates his machines for at most 12 hours a day? Formulate this mathematically and then solve it.

Answer

Formulation. Let \(x\) packages of nuts and \(y\) packages of bolts be made per day.

Maximise \(Z=2.5x+y\) subject to

\[x+3y\le12\ \text{(machine A)},\qquad 3x+y\le12\ \text{(machine B)},\qquad x\ge0,\ y\ge0.\]

Graph. \(x+3y=12\) meets the axes at \((12,0),(0,4)\); \(3x+y=12\) at \((4,0),(0,12)\). O satisfies both, so the feasible region is on the origin side of each line. Solving the two equations: \(x=3,\ y=3\).

The feasible region is the bounded quadrilateral OABC.

Corner\(Z=2.5x+y\)
O(0, 0)0
A(4, 0)10
B(3, 3)10.5
C(0, 4)4

3 packages of nuts and 3 packages of bolts a day; maximum profit Rs 10.50

Concepts to recall

  • 'At most 12 hours' gives \(\le\) constraints, one per machine.
  • Bounded region: the optimum is at a corner — tabulate Z at every corner.
  • Corner B from solving the two boundary equations simultaneously.
