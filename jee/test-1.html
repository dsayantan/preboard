Question 1Physics · Section A · Multiple choice · +4 / −1 Electrostatics

Two identical parallel-plate capacitors are connected in parallel across a 60 V battery. The battery is then disconnected and the space between the plates of one capacitor is completely filled with a dielectric of dielectric constant \(K = 5\). The potential difference across the other capacitor now is

Solution

Before: total charge \(Q = (C + C)\times 60 = 120C\). The battery is removed, so this charge is conserved.

After: the two capacitors are still in parallel, \(C_{\text{eq}} = C + 5C = 6C\).

Common potential \(V = \dfrac{Q}{C_{\text{eq}}} = \dfrac{120C}{6C} = 20\) V (the same across both).

\(60\) V assumes the battery is still connected; \(12\) V divides 60 V by \(K\) as if the dielectric acted alone; \(10\) V forgets that the charge was stored on two capacitors.

Answer: \(20\) V.

Question 2Physics · Section A · Multiple choice · +4 / −1 Electrostatics

A pendulum bob of mass 40 mg carrying a charge of 15 nC hangs at rest in a uniform horizontal electric field of \(2\times10^{4}\) N C\(^{-1}\). Taking \(g = 10\) m s\(^{-2}\), the tension in the thread is

Solution

Weight \(mg = 40\times10^{-6}\times10 = 4\times10^{-4}\) N, vertically down.

Electric force \(qE = 15\times10^{-9}\times2\times10^{4} = 3\times10^{-4}\) N, horizontal.

In equilibrium the tension balances the resultant of these two perpendicular forces: \(T = \sqrt{(mg)^2 + (qE)^2} = 10^{-4}\sqrt{4^2+3^2} = 5\times10^{-4}\) N (thread at \(\tan^{-1}(3/4) = 37^\circ\) to the vertical).

Adding the forces as scalars gives \(7\times10^{-4}\) N, subtracting gives \(1\times10^{-4}\) N, and \(4\times10^{-4}\) N ignores the electric force.

Answer: \(5\times10^{-4}\) N.

Question 3Physics · Section A · Multiple choice · +4 / −1 Electrostatics

A parallel-plate capacitor is charged by a battery, which is then disconnected. The separation between the plates is now doubled. Which statement is correct?

Solution

\(C = \varepsilon_0A/d\): doubling \(d\) halves \(C\).

The battery is disconnected, so \(Q\) is constant. \(V = Q/C\) doubles.

\(U = \dfrac{Q^2}{2C}\): with \(Q\) fixed and \(C\) halved, \(U\) doubles (the extra energy is the work done in pulling the plates apart against their attraction).

Why the others are wrong: "\(V\) unchanged, \(U\) halves" is the result when the battery stays connected (\(V\) fixed, \(U = \tfrac12CV^2\)); "\(U\) unchanged" ignores that \(U\) depends on \(C\); "capacitance doubles" has \(C\propto d\) instead of \(1/d\).

Answer: capacitance halves, potential difference doubles, stored energy doubles.

An ammeter is made from a galvanometer of coil resistance 90 Ω with a shunt of 10 Ω. It is connected in series with a 15 Ω resistor across an ideal 12 V battery. The ammeter reads

Solution

Resistance of the ammeter = coil ∥ shunt \(= \dfrac{90\times10}{90+10} = 9\) Ω.

Total resistance \(= 15 + 9 = 24\) Ω, so \(I = \dfrac{12}{24} = 0.50\) A. The ammeter reads the whole current (coil + shunt together).

\(0.80\) A treats the ammeter as ideal (12/15); \(0.48\) A uses the shunt alone (12/25); \(0.11\) A uses the coil alone (12/105).

Answer: \(0.50\) A.

Question 5Physics · Section A · Multiple choice · +4 / −1 Current Electricity

Free electrons drift with speed \(v\) in a metal wire carrying current \(I\). In a second wire of the same material but of half the radius, carrying current \(2I\), the drift speed is

Solution

\(I = neAv_d = ne\pi r^2 v_d\Rightarrow v_d = \dfrac{I}{ne\pi r^2}\); \(n\) is the same (same material).

\(\dfrac{v_d'}{v} = \dfrac{2I}{I}\times\dfrac{r^2}{(r/2)^2} = 2\times4 = 8\).

\(4v\) forgets the doubled current, \(2v\) forgets the smaller area, and \(v/2\) uses \(v_d\propto r^2\).

Answer: \(8v\).

Question 6Physics · Section A · Multiple choice · +4 / −1 Magnetic Effects of Current and Magnetism

An \(\alpha\)-particle and a proton, having the same kinetic energy, move at right angles to a uniform magnetic field. The ratio of the radii of their circular paths, \(r_\alpha : r_p\), is

Solution

\(qvB = \dfrac{mv^2}{r}\Rightarrow r = \dfrac{mv}{qB} = \dfrac{\sqrt{2mK}}{qB}\).

With \(m_\alpha = 4m_p\), \(q_\alpha = 2e\), same \(K\): \(\dfrac{r_\alpha}{r_p} = \dfrac{\sqrt{4m_p}}{\sqrt{m_p}}\cdot\dfrac{e}{2e} = \dfrac{2}{2} = 1\).

\(1:2\) is the answer for equal momenta (\(r\propto1/q\)); \(2:1\) is for equal speeds (\(r\propto m/q\)); \(\sqrt2:1\) is for acceleration through the same potential difference (\(r\propto\sqrt{m/q}\)).

Answer: \(1:1\).

Question 7Physics · Section A · Multiple choice · +4 / −1 Magnetic Effects of Current and Magnetism

A very long wire carrying current \(I\) is bent as shown: a straight part along a line through O, a semicircle of radius \(r\) centred at O, and a straight part running off to infinity from the foot of the perpendicular from O. The magnitude of the magnetic field at O is

Solution

Straight part whose line passes through O: \(d\vec l\parallel\hat r\), so it gives \(B_1 = 0\).

Semicircle: half of a full loop's field, \(B_2 = \dfrac12\cdot\dfrac{\mu_0I}{2r} = \dfrac{\mu_0I}{4r}\), out of the page.

Semi-infinite wire starting at the foot of the perpendicular (distance \(r\)): \(B_3 = \dfrac{\mu_0I}{4\pi r}\), also out of the page (current to the right, O above it).

\(B = \dfrac{\mu_0I}{4r} + \dfrac{\mu_0I}{4\pi r} = \dfrac{\mu_0I}{4\pi r}(\pi+1)\).

Why not the others: \((\pi-1)\) takes the two contributions as opposite, but both are out of the page; \(\mu_0I/4r\) leaves out the straight wire; \((\pi+2)\) treats the semi-infinite wire as an infinite one (\(\mu_0I/2\pi r\)).

Answer: \(\dfrac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi r}(\pi+1)\).

For a prism of angle \(60^\circ\) the angle of minimum deviation is \(30^\circ\). The refractive index of the prism material is

The electron in a hydrogen atom jumps from the third excited state to the second excited state. The emitted photon belongs to the

Question 19Physics · Section A · Multiple choice · +4 / −1 Electronic Devices

When a p-n junction is forward biased, the width of the depletion region and the height of the potential barrier respectively

Solution

The applied forward voltage opposes the built-in field. Majority carriers are pushed towards the junction and neutralise some of the immobile ions, so the depletion layer narrows and the barrier falls from \(V_0\) to \(V_0 - V\).

"Both increase" is the reverse-bias behaviour. The barrier height cannot stay unchanged while the width shrinks — it is the potential across that layer of ions, so the two fall together. Width increasing with barrier decreasing is inconsistent for the same reason.

Answer: both decrease.

Question 27Chemistry · Section A · Multiple choice · +4 / −1 Solutions

Question 30Chemistry · Section A · Multiple choice · +4 / −1 Chemical Kinetics

A reaction is 50% complete in 20 min and 75% complete in 40 min. The time needed for 99.9% completion is about

Solution

50% in 20 min and the next half of what is left (to 75%) in another 20 min: the half-life is independent of concentration, so the reaction is first order, \(t_{1/2} = 20\) min, \(k = 0.693/20\) min\(^{-1}\).
