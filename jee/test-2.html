Question 1Physics · Section A · Multiple choice · +4 / −1 Electrostatics

Charges \(+q\), \(-2q\) and \(+q\) are placed at the vertices of an equilateral triangle of side \(a\). The magnitude of the electric dipole moment of the system is

Solution

Split \(-2q\) into two charges \(-q\). The system is then two dipoles, each of moment \(qa\), pointing from the \(-2q\) vertex to the two \(+q\) vertices, with \(60^\circ\) between them.

\(p = \sqrt{p_1^2 + p_2^2 + 2p_1p_2\cos60^\circ} = qa\sqrt{1+1+1} = \sqrt3\,qa\), along the bisector from the \(-2q\) vertex.

\(2qa\) adds the two moments as if parallel; \(qa\) counts one dipole; "zero" confuses zero net charge with zero dipole moment — the charges are not arranged symmetrically about a point, so the two dipoles do not cancel.

Answer: \(\sqrt3\,qa\).

Question 2Physics · Section A · Multiple choice · +4 / −1 Electrostatics

A \(4\ \mu\)F capacitor charged to 300 V is disconnected from the supply and connected across an uncharged \(2\ \mu\)F capacitor. The energy lost in the process is

Solution

Charge \(Q = 4\times10^{-6}\times300 = 1.2\times10^{-3}\) C is shared: common potential \(V = \dfrac{Q}{C_1 + C_2} = \dfrac{1.2\times10^{-3}}{6\times10^{-6}} = 200\) V.

\(U_i = \tfrac12(4\times10^{-6})(300)^2 = 0.18\) J; \(U_f = \tfrac12(6\times10^{-6})(200)^2 = 0.12\) J.

Loss \(= 0.18 - 0.12 = 0.06\) J \(\left(= \dfrac{C_1C_2}{2(C_1+C_2)}V_1^2\right)\), dissipated as heat and radiation.

\(0.18\) J and \(0.12\) J are the initial and final energies; \(0.03\) J halves the loss formula once too often.

Answer: \(0.06\) J.

Question 4Physics · Section A · Multiple choice · +4 / −1 Current Electricity

On a potentiometer wire a cell of emf 1.5 V is balanced at 75 cm, and another cell at 110 cm. The emf of the second cell is

Solution

At balance no current is drawn from the cell, so \(\varepsilon = kl\) with the same potential gradient \(k\) for both: \(\dfrac{\varepsilon_2}{\varepsilon_1} = \dfrac{l_2}{l_1}\).

\(\varepsilon_2 = 1.5\times\dfrac{110}{75} = 2.2\) V.

\(1.02\) V inverts the ratio; \(1.1\) V assumes 1 V per metre of wire; \(1.65\) V multiplies 1.5 V by the length 1.10 m instead of by the ratio of the lengths.

Answer: \(2.2\) V.

Question 5Physics · Section A · Multiple choice · +4 / −1 Current Electricity

In a metre bridge a 3 Ω resistor is in the left gap and an unknown resistance \(X\) in the right gap. The null point is 60 cm from the left end. To shift the null point to 50 cm one must connect

Solution

Balance: \(\dfrac{3}{X} = \dfrac{60}{100-60}\Rightarrow X = 3\times\dfrac{40}{60} = 2\ \Omega\).

Null point at 50 cm needs equal arms, so the right gap must hold 3 Ω. Raising 2 Ω to 3 Ω needs \(1\ \Omega\) in series (a parallel resistor can only lower it).

"\(1\ \Omega\) in parallel" would give 0.67 Ω; "\(9\ \Omega\) in parallel" comes from taking \(X = 3\times60/40 = 4.5\ \Omega\) (the ratio inverted); "\(2\ \Omega\) in series" gives 4 Ω.

Answer: \(1\ \Omega\) in series with \(X\).

Question 6Physics · Section A · Multiple choice · +4 / −1 Magnetic Effects of Current and Magnetism

A wire bent into a single circular loop gives a magnetic field \(B\) at its centre when a current \(I\) flows. The same wire is rebent into a circular coil of 2 turns and carries the same current. The field at the centre becomes

Solution

Same length of wire: \(2\pi R = 2(2\pi R')\Rightarrow R' = R/2\).

\(B = \dfrac{\mu_0NI}{2R}\Rightarrow B' = \dfrac{\mu_0(2)I}{2(R/2)} = 4\cdot\dfrac{\mu_0I}{2R} = 4B\) (in general \(B\propto N^2\) for a fixed length).

\(2B\) counts the extra turn but forgets the smaller radius; \(B\) is what happens if both \(N\) and \(R\) are doubled; \(B/2\) doubles the radius and forgets the extra turn.

Answer: \(4B\).

Question 7Physics · Section A · Multiple choice · +4 / −1 Magnetic Effects of Current and Magnetism

Electrons moving to the right with speed \(2\times10^{6}\) m s\(^{-1}\) pass undeviated through crossed fields: an electric field of \(5\times10^{4}\) V m\(^{-1}\) pointing vertically downward in the plane of the page, and a magnetic field \(\vec B\). Then \(\vec B\) is

Solution

Undeviated: \(eE = evB\Rightarrow B = \dfrac{E}{v} = \dfrac{5\times10^{4}}{2\times10^{6}} = 0.025\) T.

Direction: the electric force on an electron is opposite to \(\vec E\), i.e. upward, so the magnetic force \(-e(\vec v\times\vec B)\) must be downward, i.e. \(\vec v\times\vec B\) upward. With \(\vec v\) to the right, \(\hat x\times(-\hat z) = +\hat y\): \(\vec B\) is into the page.

"Out of the page" treats the electron as a positive charge; \(40\) T is \(v/E\); \(0.05\) T is \(2E/v\).

Answer: \(0.025\) T, into the page.

Question 10Physics · Section A · Multiple choice · +4 / −1 Electromagnetic Induction and Alternating Currents

A 50 Hz a.c. source is connected to a resistor of resistance \(P\) in series with a component B of reactance \(Y\). The applied voltage leads the current by \(\pi/4\). Then

Solution

Voltage leading the current means the circuit is inductive, so B is an inductor.

\(\tan\varphi = \dfrac{Y}{P} = \tan\dfrac{\pi}{4} = 1\Rightarrow Y = P\).

A capacitor would make the current lead the voltage, so both "capacitor" options are wrong; \(Y = 2P\) would give \(\varphi = \tan^{-1}2 = 63.4^\circ\), not \(45^\circ\).

Answer: B is an inductor and \(Y = P\).

Which sequence lists the radiations in order of increasing wavelength?

A thin biconvex air lens with both radii of curvature 20 cm is formed inside a block of glass of refractive index 1.5. The focal length of this air lens is

Question 19Physics · Section A · Multiple choice · +4 / −1 Electronic Devices

Pure silicon is doped with a small amount of arsenic (group 15). The resulting semiconductor is

Solution

Arsenic is pentavalent: four of its valence electrons form covalent bonds with silicon and the fifth is loosely bound and easily freed. Arsenic is a donor, so the material is n-type and electrons are the majority carriers (holes, made only thermally, are the minority).

p-type needs a trivalent (group 13) acceptor; "n-type with holes as majority" contradicts what n-type means; a doped crystal is extrinsic, not intrinsic.

Answer: n-type, with electrons as majority carriers.

Question 20Physics · Section A · Multiple choice · +4 / −1 Electronic Devices

A full-wave rectifier is fed from the 50 Hz mains. The fundamental frequency of the ripple in its output is

Solution

The full-wave rectifier passes both half-cycles with the same polarity, giving one output pulse every half-cycle: \(2\times50 = 100\) pulses per second, so the ripple frequency is 100 Hz.

\(50\) Hz is the half-wave rectifier's ripple; \(25\) Hz halves instead of doubling; a rectifier alone gives pulsating, not steady, d.c. — a filter is needed to smooth it.

Answer: \(100\) Hz.

Question 27Chemistry · Section A · Multiple choice · +4 / −1 Solutions

Which of the following mixtures forms a minimum-boiling azeotrope?

Solution

A minimum-boiling azeotrope has a maximum vapour pressure: large positive deviation from Raoult's law, A–B attractions weaker than A–A and B–B. Ethanol–water (95.6% ethanol, b.p. 351 K) is the standard example: ethanol breaks up the hydrogen-bonded network of water.
