Question 1Physics · Section A · Multiple choice · +4 / −1 Electrostatics

\(n\) identical capacitors, each of capacitance \(C\), are first connected in parallel and then in series, each time across the same source of voltage \(V\). The ratio of the energy stored in the parallel arrangement to that in the series arrangement is

Solution

Parallel: \(C_p = nC\); series: \(C_s = C/n\).

Same \(V\): \(U = \tfrac12C_{\text{eq}}V^2\), so \(\dfrac{U_p}{U_s} = \dfrac{nC}{C/n} = n^2\).

\(n\) compares only one of the two capacitances with \(C\); \(1\) assumes the energy depends only on \(V\); \(1/n^2\) is the ratio upside-down.

Answer: \(n^2\).

Question 2Physics · Section A · Multiple choice · +4 / −1 Electrostatics

Capacitors of \(2\ \mu\)F and \(6\ \mu\)F are connected in series across a 160 V supply. The potential difference across the \(2\ \mu\)F capacitor is

Solution

\(C_s = \dfrac{2\times6}{2+6} = 1.5\ \mu\)F; \(Q = 1.5\times160 = 240\ \mu\)C, the same on each capacitor in series.

\(V_2 = \dfrac{Q}{C} = \dfrac{240}{2} = 120\) V (and 40 V across the \(6\ \mu\)F).

\(40\) V is the p.d. across the \(6\ \mu\)F (dividing in proportion to \(C\) instead of \(1/C\)); \(80\) V splits the supply equally; \(160\) V treats the capacitors as in parallel.

Answer: \(120\) V.

An electric dipole of moment \(2\times10^{-8}\) C m is in equilibrium (aligned) in a uniform electric field of \(5\times10^{3}\) N C\(^{-1}\). The work needed to rotate it through \(120^\circ\) is

\(W = 10^{-4}(1 - \cos120^\circ) = 10^{-4}(1 + 0.5) = 1.5\times10^{-4}\) J.

\(0.5\times10^{-4}\) J takes \(\cos120^\circ = +0.5\); \(0.87\times10^{-4}\) J uses \(pE\sin\theta\) (that is the torque, in N m); \(2.0\times10^{-4}\) J is the work for \(180^\circ\).

Answer: \(1.5\times10^{-4}\) J.

Question 4Physics · Section A · Multiple choice · +4 / −1 Current Electricity

Resistors of 6 Ω and 9 Ω are connected in parallel, and a total current of 5 A enters the combination. The current through the 9 Ω resistor is

Solution

\(3\) A is the current in the 6 Ω resistor (or dividing in proportion to \(R\)); \(2.5\) A splits equally; \(5\) A forgets the division.

Question 5Physics · Section A · Multiple choice · +4 / −1 Current Electricity

A metallic resistor connected to a battery warms up as current flows. Which of the following does not change as it heats up?

Solution

In a metal the number of free electrons per unit volume is fixed by the material (one or two per atom) and is practically independent of temperature.

Heating makes the ions vibrate more, so collisions are more frequent and the relaxation time \(\tau\) falls: resistivity \(\rho = \dfrac{m}{ne^2\tau}\) rises, so the resistance rises, the current falls and the drift speed \(v_d = \dfrac{eE}{m}\tau\) falls. All three of those options change.

Answer: the number density of free electrons.

Question 6Physics · Section A · Multiple choice · +4 / −1 Magnetic Effects of Current and Magnetism

An electron and a proton, each starting from rest, are accelerated through the same potential difference and then enter a uniform magnetic field at right angles to it. The ratio of the radii of their paths, \(r_e : r_p\), is

Solution

After acceleration \(K = eV\) for both, so \(p = \sqrt{2meV}\).

\(r = \dfrac{p}{eB} = \dfrac{\sqrt{2meV}}{eB}\propto\sqrt m\) (same \(|q|\)), hence \(\dfrac{r_e}{r_p} = \sqrt{\dfrac{m_e}{m_p}}\approx\dfrac{1}{43}\).

\(m_e/m_p\) is the answer for equal speeds; \(1\) is the answer for equal momenta; \(\sqrt{m_p/m_e}\) inverts the ratio — the lighter electron curves more tightly, not less.

Answer: \(\sqrt{m_e/m_p}\).

For a short bar magnet, the magnetic field at a distance \(d\) on its axis is \(B_1\) and at a distance \(2d\) on its equatorial line is \(B_2\). The ratio \(B_1/B_2\) is

Question 11Physics · Section A · Multiple choice · +4 / −1 Electromagnetic Waves

Welders wear special dark glass goggles or face shields mainly to protect the eyes from the intense

Solution

An electric welding arc is at several thousand kelvin and emits strongly in the ultraviolet (besides very bright visible light). UV damages the cornea and the skin ("arc eye"), and the special glass absorbs it.

The arc does emit infrared (heat), but that is not the radiation the goggles are designed against — infrared trapping is what a greenhouse glass does. A welding arc has far too little energy per electron to produce X-rays, and microwaves are not emitted by hot sources in significant amount.

Answer: ultraviolet radiation from the arc.

Question 20Physics · Section A · Multiple choice · +4 / −1 Electronic Devices

A centre-tap full-wave rectifier using two junction diodes is fed from a 50 Hz supply. Which statement is correct?

Solution

The two ends of the secondary are in antiphase about the centre tap. In one half-cycle one diode is forward biased and the other reverse biased; in the next half-cycle the roles swap. Both send current the same way through the load, so there is one output pulse per half-cycle: 100 pulses per second.

The diodes never conduct together (one is always reverse biased); a 50 Hz output is the half-wave result; a diode conducts only while forward biased, i.e. for half of each cycle.

Answer: each diode conducts in alternate half-cycles and the output pulses at 100 Hz.

Question 32Chemistry · Section A · Multiple choice · +4 / −1 d- and f-Block Elements

Assertion (A): Actinoids form complexes more readily than lanthanoids.
Reason (R): The 5f orbitals extend further from the nucleus than the 4f orbitals and can take part in bonding.
