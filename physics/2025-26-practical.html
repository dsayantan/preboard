Question 1

Q1Practical — Ray Optics (convex lens by u–v / no-parallax method)[9]

This experiment determines the focal length of the given Convex lens by no parallax method.

You are provided with: (a) A lens holder (b) A Convex lens (c) Two optical pins (d) An optical bench

  1. Arrange the object pin O, the image pin I and the lens L on an optical bench or a tabletop as shown in Figure 1 below. Adjust the height of the object pin O and that of the image pin I so that their tips lie on the principal axis of the lens.
  2. Place the object pin O at the 0·0 cm mark and the lens L at the ___ cm mark so that the object distance u = 20 cm (i.e. the distance between the lens and the object pin).
  3. Look at the tip of the object pin O through the lens from a distance so that you see an inverted image (I′) of the object pin.
  4. Now, adjust the position of the image pin I in such a way, that there is no parallax between I and I′. Ensure that tip to tip parallax is removed.
  5. At no parallax, note the position of the image pin I and record the image distance v = LI (i.e. the distance between the lens and the image pin) in cm, correct up to one decimal place.
  6. Repeat the experiment for five more values of u, i.e. u = 25 cm, 30 cm, 35 cm, 40 cm, and 45 cm. Each time, remove the parallax and find the value of v.
  7. For each value of u, calculate \(x = u\cdot v\) & \(y = u + v\) and record their values up to two decimal places.
  8. Tabulate all Six sets of u, v, x, and y with their units.
  9. Now plot a graph of y vs x.
  10. Draw the best fit line. (It must be thin and uniform.)
  11. Find its slope S, using \(S = \dfrac{\text{change in } y}{\text{change in } x} = \dfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}\)
  12. Record the value of S, rounded up to three significant figures.
  13. Determine: \(F = \dfrac{1}{S}\) for each set and record its value correct up to one decimal place with Unit.

Show the image position when the parallax has been removed, in any one of the readings in (vi) above, to the Visiting Examiner.

[The printed slope formula reads "change∈y / change∈x" — an equation-editor slip for "change in y / change in x". Step (xiii) says "for each set", but F = 1/S comes from the single slope of the graph, so there is one value of F.]

Answer

The readings below are sample readings, generated for a convex lens of focal length about 15 cm and rounded to the least count; your own readings will differ.

Theory (for the practical file; not required in the answer booklet). With all distances taken as positive magnitudes for a real image, \(\dfrac1u + \dfrac1v = \dfrac1f\) ⇒ \(f = \dfrac{uv}{u+v}\) ⇒ \(u + v = \dfrac{1}{f}\,(uv)\), i.e. \(y = \dfrac{1}{f}\,x\). So the graph of y against x is a straight line of slope \(S = 1/f\), and \(F = 1/S\) is the focal length.

(ii) Object pin at 0.0 cm, so the lens is placed at the 20.0 cm mark for \(u = 20.0\) cm.

Observations (bench scale least count 0.1 cm):

S. No.u (cm)image pin at (cm)v (cm)\(x = uv\) (cm²)\(y = u + v\) (cm)
120.080.460.41208.0080.40
225.062.337.3932.5062.30
330.060.230.2906.0060.20
435.061.126.1913.5061.10
540.064.124.1964.0064.10
645.067.422.41008.0067.40

Graph of y against x: origin shifted to (880, 58) so that the points spread over more than 60% of the paper; scale x: 1 small division = 10 cm², y: 1 small division = 0.25 cm. The best-fit line is drawn with the points evenly on either side.

Slope from two points on the line that are not plotted points, P(920, 61.37) and Q(1200, 79.91):

\(S = \dfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1} = \dfrac{79.91 - 61.37}{1200 - 920} = \dfrac{18.54}{280}\) ⇒ \(S = 0.0662\ \text{cm}^{-1}\) (3 s.f.)

\(F = \dfrac{1}{S} = \dfrac{1}{0.0662}\) ⇒ \(F = 15.1\) cm

Result: the focal length of the given convex lens is 15.1 cm.

Precautions (for the file): tips of O, I and the lens centre at one height on the principal axis; parallax removed tip to tip, keeping the eye at least 25 cm from the image pin; distances measured between the index marks of the stands (index correction if any).

Concepts to recall

  • \(1/u + 1/v = 1/f\) (magnitudes, real image) ⇒ \(u+v = uv/f\).
  • Slope from two non-plotted points far apart on the line; S to 3 s.f., F to 1 d.p. with unit.
  • Real image exists only for \(u \gt f\); at \(u = 2f\), \(v = 2f\).

Q2Practical — Current Electricity (resistance of a wire from V and I)[6]

This experiment determines the resistance of the given wire.

You are provided with: a) A 100 cm long uniform metallic wire AB stretched along a metre scale and provided with terminals at both ends. b) A resistance box R.B., c) A voltmeter of range 0–3 V d) An ammeter of range 0–1 A e) DC source E f) A plug key 'K', g) A jockey 'J' and h) A few connecting wires.

  1. Determine and record the least count of the given with proper units in your answer booklet. I. voltmeter II. ammeter with proper units in your answer booklet.
  2. Set up a circuit as shown in Figure 2 below. Make sure that all connections are tight.
  3. Take out 1 Ω plug from the resistance box R.B. so that R = 1 Ω. Ensure that all other plugs are tightly closed. Place the jockey J at a point C on the wire AB, such that AC = x = 80 cm. The reading of the voltmeter as well as the ammeter must be within its range. Read and record the readings of the voltmeter and the ammeter, i.e. V and I, with proper units.
  4. Repeat the experiment to obtain five more sets of readings of R, V and I by taking resistance R = 2 Ω, 3 Ω, 4 Ω, 5 Ω and 6 Ω. Ensure that the jockey is always kept at the same position C such that AC = x cm in all six sets of readings.
  5. Show any one of the readings in (iv) above, to the Visiting Examiner.
  6. Determine the value of resistance r using: \(r = V/I\) for each set, correct up to one decimal place.
  7. Now, tabulate all the six sets of values of R, V, I and r with proper units.
  8. Find r0, the mean of all the six values of r and record its value in your answer booklet.

Answer

The readings below are sample readings, generated for a wire of about 0.04 Ω cm\(^{-1}\) (4 Ω for the whole 100 cm) driven by a 3 V source, and rounded to the meters' least counts; your own readings will differ.

(i) Least counts (each meter has its range divided into equal small divisions; least count = range ÷ number of divisions; the values below are those of the school's meters, as given in the Lab Manual):
I. Voltmeter (0–3 V, 60 divisions): \(\text{LC} = 3/60 =\) 0.05 V
II. Ammeter (0–1 A, 50 divisions): \(\text{LC} = 1/50 =\) 0.02 A

Theory (for the file): the ammeter measures the current I through the wire; the voltmeter, joined between A and the jockey at C, measures the p.d. V across the length AC = 80 cm. By Ohm's law the resistance of that 80 cm is \(r = V/I\). Changing R in the resistance box changes I (and V in proportion), so each set gives an independent value of the same r.

Observations (AC = x = 80.0 cm in every set):

e.g. set 1: \(r = \dfrac{1.85}{0.58} = 3.19 \approx 3.2\ \Omega\); set 5: \(\dfrac{1.05}{0.32} = 3.28 \approx 3.3\ \Omega\).

(viii) \(r_0 = \dfrac{3.2 + 3.2 + 3.2 + 3.2 + 3.3 + 3.2}{6} = \dfrac{19.3}{6} = 3.22\) ⇒ \(r_0 = 3.2\ \Omega\)

Result: resistance of 80 cm of the wire = 3.2 Ω (≈ 0.04 Ω per cm).

Precautions (for the file): meters connected with + to the higher-potential side (current enters the wire at A); key closed only while reading, so the wire does not heat up; jockey pressed gently and always at the same point C; readings within range and recorded to the least count.

Concepts to recall

  • Least count = range ÷ number of divisions (school meters: 0.05 V, 0.02 A).
  • Ohm's law \(r = V/I\); a series R only changes the current, not r.
  • Every reading must be a whole number of least counts.
