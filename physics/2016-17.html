Q1(A)(i)Electrostatics[1]

Three equal charges are placed on the three corners of a square. If the force between \(q_1\) and \(q_2\) is \(F_{12}\) and that between \(q_1\) and \(q_3\) is \(F_{13}\), then the ratio of magnitudes \((F_{12}/F_{13})\) is

(a) \(\tfrac12\)   (b) 2   (c) \(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\)   (d) \(\sqrt2\)

Answer

(b) 2.

The intended arrangement: \(q_2\) at a corner adjacent to \(q_1\) (distance \(a\)), \(q_3\) at the diagonally opposite corner (distance \(\sqrt2a\)).

\(F\propto 1/r^2\): \(\dfrac{F_{12}}{F_{13}} = \dfrac{(\sqrt2a)^2}{a^2} = 2\).

[The question does not say which corners; if \(q_2\) and \(q_3\) were both adjacent to \(q_1\) the ratio would be 1, which is not an option.]

Concepts to recall

  • Coulomb: \(F = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\dfrac{q_1q_2}{r^2}\).
  • Diagonal of a square = \(\sqrt2a\).
Q1(A)(ii)Current Electricity[1]

Which of the following quantities does not change when a resistor connected to a battery is heated due to current?

(a) Drift speed   (b) Resistivity   (c) Resistance   (d) Number of free electrons

Answer

(d) Number of free electrons.

Heating increases the collision frequency (smaller relaxation time \(\tau\)), so resistivity and resistance rise and the current and drift speed fall. The number of free electrons in a metal is fixed by the material and does not change.

Concepts to recall

  • \(\rho = m/ne^2\tau\); in metals \(\tau\) decreases with temperature, \(n\) is constant.
Q1(A)(iii)Optics — Wave Optics[1]

If two waves represented by \(y_1 = 4\sin\omega t\) and \(y_2 = 3\sin(\omega t+\pi/3)\) interfere at a point, the amplitude of the resultant wave will be about

(a) 7   (b) 5   (c) 6   (d) 3.5

Answer

(c) 6.

\(A = \sqrt{a_1^2 + a_2^2 + 2a_1a_2\cos\phi} = \sqrt{16 + 9 + 2(4)(3)\cos60^\circ} = \sqrt{37} \approx 6.1\).

Concepts to recall

  • Superposition of two SHMs of the same frequency: \(A^2 = a_1^2+a_2^2+2a_1a_2\cos\phi\).
Q1(A)(iv)Dual Nature of Radiation and Matter[1]

If the work function of potassium is 2 eV, then its photoelectric threshold wavelength is

(a) 310 nm   (b) 620 nm   (c) 6200 nm   (d) 3100 nm

Answer

(b) 620 nm.

\(\lambda_0 = \dfrac{hc}{\phi_0} = \dfrac{6.6\times10^{-34}\times3\times10^{8}}{2\times1.6\times10^{-19}} = 6.19\times10^{-7}\) m ≈ 620 nm (or \(1240/2\) nm).

  • \(\phi_0 = hc/\lambda_0\); shortcut \(\lambda(\text{nm}) \approx 1240/E(\text{eV})\).
Q1(A)(v)Electronic Devices[1]

In a n-type semiconductor, which of the following statement is true?

(a) Electrons are majority carriers and trivalent atoms are dopants.
(b) Electrons are minority carriers and pentavalent atoms are dopants.
(c) Holes are minority carriers and pentavalent atoms are dopants.
(d) Holes are majority carriers and trivalent atoms are dopants.

Answer

(c) Holes are minority carriers and pentavalent atoms are dopants. A pentavalent donor gives free electrons (majority carriers); holes, produced only thermally, are the minority.

Q1(B)(iii)Current Electricity[1]

In the given figure find the current through the 20 V battery.

Answer

The ideal 20 V cell is connected directly between the top junction T and the bottom junction M, with + at T: \(V_T - V_M = 20\) V.

Left branch (16 V, + at top, with 2 Ω): \(V_T - V_M = 16 - 2I_1\) for current \(I_1\) upward ⇒ \(I_1 = (16 - 20)/2 = -2\) A, i.e. 2 A downward.

Middle branch (16 V with + at the bottom, and 4 Ω): \(V_T - V_M = -16 - 4I_2\) ⇒ \(I_2 = (-16-20)/4 = -9\) A, i.e. 9 A downward.

Q1(B)(vi)Electromagnetic Induction and Alternating Currents[1]

A metallic square loop \(ABCD\) is moving in its own plane with velocity \(v\) in a uniform magnetic field perpendicular to its plane as shown in the figure. Mention the arms in which an electric field is induced.

Answer

A motional electric field (emf \(Blv\)) appears in a conductor only when \(\vec v\times\vec B\) has a component along it. For AB and DC, \(\vec v\) is along their length, so \(\vec v\times\vec B\) is perpendicular to them: no emf.

An electric field is induced in the arms AD and BC (perpendicular to \(\vec v\)). The two emfs are equal and oppose each other round the loop (flux is constant), so no current flows.

Concepts to recall

  • Motional emf \(\varepsilon = Blv\), along \(\vec v\times\vec B\) (on + charges).
  • Net induced current needs a change of flux through the loop.

Q2(a)Electrostatics[2]

A pendulum of mass 80 mg and carrying a charge of 20 nC is at rest in a horizontal uniform electric field of \(2\times10^4\) N/C. Find the tension in the thread of the pendulum

Answer

At rest the bob is in equilibrium under \(mg\) (down), \(qE\) (horizontal) and \(T\) along the thread.

\(mg = 80\times10^{-6}\times9.8 = 7.84\times10^{-4}\) N; \(qE = 20\times10^{-9}\times2\times10^{4} = 4.0\times10^{-4}\) N.

\(T = \sqrt{(mg)^2 + (qE)^2} = 10^{-4}\sqrt{7.84^2 + 4^2}\) = \(8.8\times10^{-4}\) N, the thread making \(\theta = \tan^{-1}(qE/mg) = 27^\circ\) with the vertical.

Concepts to recall

  • Three forces in equilibrium: \(T\) balances the resultant of \(mg\) and \(qE\).
  • 80 mg \(= 80\times10^{-6}\) kg.
Q2(b)Electrostatics[3]

An electric dipole of dipole moment \(\vec p\) is placed in a uniform electric field \(\vec E\) with its axis inclined to the field. Write an expression for the torque \(\vec\tau\) experienced by the dipole in vector form. Show diagrammatically how the dipole should be kept in the electric field so that the torque acting on it is (i) Maximum (ii) Zero

Answer

\(\vec\tau = \vec p\times\vec E\), magnitude \(\tau = pE\sin\theta\), \(\theta\) = angle between \(\vec p\) and \(\vec E\).

(i) Maximum, \(\tau = pE\), when \(\theta = 90^\circ\): dipole axis perpendicular to the field.

(ii) Zero when \(\theta = 0^\circ\) (stable) or \(180^\circ\) (unstable): axis along the field.

Concepts to recall

  • The forces \(+q\vec E\), \(-q\vec E\) form a couple of arm \(2l\sin\theta\).
  • \(\tau = pE\sin\theta\); \(U = -pE\cos\theta\).

Q3(b)Electrostatics[3]

Derive an expression for the energy stored in a charged capacitor.

Answer

Charge a capacitor \(C\) gradually. When its charge is \(q\), its p.d. is \(v = q/C\). The work needed to bring a further charge \(dq\) from the negative plate to the positive plate is \(dW = v\,dq = \dfrac{q}{C}dq\).

Total work in charging from 0 to \(Q\): \(W = \displaystyle\int_0^Q \frac{q}{C}\,dq = \frac{Q^2}{2C}\).

This work is stored as electric potential energy. Using \(Q = CV\):

\(U = \dfrac{Q^2}{2C} = \dfrac12CV^2 = \dfrac12QV\) — the area under the \(v\)–\(q\) graph.

Concepts to recall

  • \(dW = v\,dq\); p.d. grows linearly with charge, so average p.d. is \(V/2\).
  • Energy density \(u = \tfrac12\varepsilon_0E^2\).

Q4(b)Current Electricity[3]

Draw a neat labeled diagram of meter bridge circuit use to determine the unknown resistance \(X\).

Answer

A uniform wire AC, 1 m long, is stretched along a metre scale between thick copper strips. The unknown resistance \(X\) is put in the left gap and a resistance box \(R\) in the right gap. A galvanometer G is connected between the middle strip B and a jockey J sliding on the wire; a cell with key K is joined across A and C.

The jockey is moved to the null point D (no deflection), at distance \(l\) from A. Then the bridge is balanced: \(\dfrac{X}{R} = \dfrac{R_{AD}}{R_{DC}} = \dfrac{l}{100-l}\)

\(X = R\,\dfrac{l}{100-l}\)

Concepts to recall

  • Metre bridge = Wheatstone bridge with two arms formed by the wire (\(R\propto l\)).
  • Best accuracy when the null point is near the middle.

Q5(b)Electromagnetic Induction and Alternating Currents[3]

Plot a labelled graph showing variation in impedance Z of a L-C-R circuit with frequency f of alternating emf applied to it. What is the minimum value of this impedance? Define 'power factor' of an L-C-R circuit.

Answer

\(Z = \sqrt{R^2 + (2\pi fL - 1/2\pi fC)^2}\): large at low \(f\) (capacitive), falls to a minimum at the resonant frequency \(f_0 = \dfrac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{LC}}\), then rises at high \(f\) (inductive).

Minimum impedance (at resonance, \(X_L = X_C\)): \(Z_{\min} = R\)

Power factor is the ratio of the true (average) power to the apparent power \(V_{\text{rms}}I_{\text{rms}}\): \(\cos\varphi = \dfrac{P}{V_{\text{rms}}I_{\text{rms}}} = \dfrac{R}{Z}\), where \(\varphi\) is the phase difference between voltage and current.

Concepts to recall

  • Resonance: \(X_L = X_C\), \(f_0 = 1/2\pi\sqrt{LC}\), \(Z = R\), \(\cos\varphi = 1\).
Q6(a)Electromagnetic Induction and Alternating Currents[2]

What are eddy currents? Write a practical application of eddy current.

Answer

Eddy currents are the circulating currents induced in the body of a bulk conductor when the magnetic flux through it changes (or it moves in a magnetic field). They flow in closed loops and, by Lenz's law, oppose the change producing them.

Applications (any one): electromagnetic (eddy-current) brakes in trains; induction furnace; dead-beat moving-coil galvanometer (damping); speedometers; induction cooktops.

Concepts to recall

  • Reduced in transformer cores by lamination (insulated thin sheets).

Q7(a)Magnetic Effects of Current and Magnetism[2]

Derive an expression for torque on a rectangular current loop having a steady current \(i\) and placed in a uniform magnetic field \(B\).

Answer

Let the rectangular loop PQRS (sides \(l\) and \(b\), \(N\) turns) carry current \(i\) and be free to turn about an axis perpendicular to \(\vec B\); let the normal \(\hat n\) to its plane make angle \(\theta\) with \(\vec B\).

Sides of length \(b\): the forces on them are equal, opposite and collinear — they cancel.

Sides PS and QR (length \(l\), perpendicular to \(\vec B\)): \(F = ilB\) each, equal and opposite but along different lines ⇒ a couple. The perpendicular distance between their lines of action is \(b\sin\theta\).

\(\tau = F\times b\sin\theta = ilB\,b\sin\theta = iAB\sin\theta\) (\(A = lb\)). For \(N\) turns:

\(\tau = NiAB\sin\theta\), i.e. \(\vec\tau = \vec m\times\vec B\) with \(\vec m = Ni\vec A\)

Maximum (\(NiAB\)) when the plane of the loop is parallel to \(\vec B\) (\(\theta = 90^\circ\)); zero when the plane is perpendicular to \(\vec B\).

Concepts to recall

  • \(\vec F = i\,\vec l\times\vec B\); net force on a closed loop in a uniform field is zero.
  • θ is between the normal (\(\vec m\)) and \(\vec B\).

Q8(a)Optics — Ray Optics[3]

Starting with an expression for refraction at a single spherical surface, obtain an expression for lens maker's formula for a bi-convex lens.

Answer

For refraction at a spherical surface from medium \(\mu_1\) to \(\mu_2\): \(\dfrac{\mu_2}{v} - \dfrac{\mu_1}{u} = \dfrac{\mu_2-\mu_1}{R}\).

Thin biconvex lens of index \(\mu_2\) in a medium \(\mu_1\); radii \(R_1\) (first surface) and \(R_2\) (second). A point object O on the axis.

First surface alone forms image \(I_1\) at \(v_1\): \(\dfrac{\mu_2}{v_1} - \dfrac{\mu_1}{u} = \dfrac{\mu_2-\mu_1}{R_1}\) … (1)

\(I_1\) is the (virtual) object for the second surface (lens thin, so distance \(v_1\) from it); light goes from \(\mu_2\) to \(\mu_1\), giving the final image I at \(v\): \(\dfrac{\mu_1}{v} - \dfrac{\mu_2}{v_1} = \dfrac{\mu_1-\mu_2}{R_2}\) … (2)

Add (1) and (2): \(\mu_1\left(\dfrac1v - \dfrac1u\right) = (\mu_2-\mu_1)\left(\dfrac1{R_1}-\dfrac1{R_2}\right)\).

For an object at infinity \(v = f\): with \(\mu = \mu_2/\mu_1\),

\(\dfrac1f = (\mu - 1)\left(\dfrac1{R_1} - \dfrac1{R_2}\right)\) and hence \(\dfrac1v - \dfrac1u = \dfrac1f\).

For a biconvex lens \(R_1 > 0\), \(R_2 < 0\), so \(\dfrac1f = (\mu-1)\left(\dfrac1{|R_1|}+\dfrac1{|R_2|}\right) > 0\).

Concepts to recall

  • Image of the first surface acts as the object of the second.
  • Cartesian signs: \(R_1 = +\), \(R_2 = -\) for a biconvex lens.
Q8(b)Optics — Ray Optics[2]

A narrow beam of light is incident normally on one face of an equilateral glass prism of refractive index 1.45. When the prism is immersed in a suitable liquid, the ray makes a grazing emergence along the other face. Find the refractive index of this liquid.

Answer

Normal incidence on the first face ⇒ \(r_1 = 0\), so \(r_2 = A - r_1 = 60^\circ\).

Grazing emergence ⇒ 60° is the critical angle for glass→liquid: \(\sin C = \dfrac{\mu_l}{\mu_g}\).

\(\mu_l = 1.45\sin60^\circ = 1.45\times0.866\) = 1.26

Concepts to recall

  • Prism: \(r_1 + r_2 = A\).
  • Critical angle (denser → rarer): \(\sin C = \mu_{\text{rarer}}/\mu_{\text{denser}}\).
Q9(a)Optics — Ray Optics[3]

Draw a neat labeled diagram showing the formation of image by a compound microscope for normal adjustment condition.

Answer

Object AB is placed just beyond \(F_o\) of the objective (short focal length). The objective forms a real, inverted, magnified image A′B′ inside the focal length of the eyepiece. The eyepiece acts as a simple magnifier and forms the final virtual, inverted (w.r.t. AB), highly magnified image A″B″ at the least distance of distinct vision \(D = 25\) cm.

\(M = \dfrac{v_o}{u_o}\left(1 + \dfrac{D}{f_e}\right)\).

Figure drawn to scale for \(f_o = 2\) cm, \(f_e = 5\) cm, \(u_o = 2.4\) cm (heights not to scale). For a telescope, normal adjustment always means the final image at infinity. For the compound microscope the ISC 2027 syllabus asks for the ray diagram with the final image at \(D\) (only the expression for the image at infinity), and this school's 2015-16 paper calls the image-at-\(D\) set-up "normal use", so that is the diagram drawn. If the examiner means the image at infinity, A′B′ is placed at \(F_e\) instead, the emergent rays are parallel and \(M = \dfrac{v_o}{u_o}\cdot\dfrac{D}{f_e}\).

Concepts to recall

  • Objective: real magnified image; eyepiece: magnifier.
  • Rays: one parallel to the axis (through the focus after the lens), one through the optical centre (undeviated).
Q10(a)Optics — Wave Optics[3]

i) What is the shape of the wave front diverging from a point source of light?
ii) In Young's double slit experiment, red light of wavelength 6000 Å is used and the nth bright fringe is obtained at a point \(P\) on the screen. Keeping the same setting, the source is replaced by the green light of 5000 Å and now \((n+1)^{th}\) bright fringe is obtained at the same point \(P\). Calculate the value of \(n\).

Answer

(i) Spherical (diverging).

(ii) Position of the \(n\)th bright fringe: \(y_n = \dfrac{n\lambda D}{d}\). Same point P, same \(D, d\): \(n\lambda_1 = (n+1)\lambda_2\).

\(6000n = 5000(n+1)\Rightarrow 1000n = 5000\) ⇒ \(n = 5\)

Concepts to recall

  • Bright fringe: path difference \(d\sin\theta = n\lambda\), \(y_n = n\lambda D/d\).
Q10(b)Optics — Wave Optics[2]

Can two sodium vapour lamps act as coherent sources? Explain in brief.

Answer

No. Each lamp's light comes from many atoms emitting independently in short wave trains (~\(10^{-8}\) s) with random phases. The phase difference between two independent lamps changes randomly millions of times a second, so it is not constant: they are not coherent and no sustained interference pattern is seen.

Concepts to recall

  • Coherent sources: same frequency and a constant phase difference.
Q11(a)Optics — Ray Optics[3]

A point source of monochromatic light S is kept at the centre of the bottom of a cylinder. Radius of the circular base of the cylinder is 15 cm. The cylinder contains water (Refractive Index = 4/3) to a height of 7 cm. Find the area of the water surface through which the light emerges in the air.

Answer

Light escapes only where it meets the surface at less than the critical angle \(C\): \(\sin C = \dfrac1\mu = \dfrac34\), \(\tan C = \dfrac{3}{\sqrt{16-9}} = \dfrac{3}{\sqrt7}\).

Radius of the circle of escape: \(r = h\tan C = 7\times\dfrac{3}{\sqrt7} = 3\sqrt7 = 7.94\) cm (less than 15 cm, so the whole circle lies on the water surface).

Area \(= \pi r^2 = \pi\times63\) = 198 cm² (\(1.98\times10^{-2}\) m²).

Concepts to recall

  • Total internal reflection beyond \(C\), \(\sin C = 1/\mu\).
  • Radius of the bright circle \(= h\tan C = h/\sqrt{\mu^2-1}\).
Q11(b)Optics — Ray Optics[2]

A ray of light strikes a glass plate at an angle of \(60^0\). If the reflected and the refracted rays are perpendicular to each other, find the refractive index of the glass.

Answer

Angle of reflection = 60°. Reflected and refracted rays perpendicular: \(60^\circ + 90^\circ + r = 180^\circ\Rightarrow r = 30^\circ\).

Snell's law: \(\mu = \dfrac{\sin 60^\circ}{\sin 30^\circ} = \sqrt3\) = 1.73

Equivalently \(\mu = \tan i\) — this is the polarising (Brewster) angle, but Snell's law alone answers the question.

Concepts to recall

  • Angles on a straight line at the point of incidence sum to 180°.
  • \(\mu = \sin i/\sin r\).

Q12(a)Atoms and Nuclei — Atoms[3]

Obtain an expression for the kinetic energy of the electron in Bohr model of the hydrogen atom \((Z = 1)\). Start from equating the electrostatic force with the centripetal force required and then use the quantization condition for angular momentum.

Answer

Coulomb force = centripetal force: \(\dfrac{mv^2}{r} = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\dfrac{e^2}{r^2}\) ⇒ \(mv^2 = \dfrac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r}\), so \(K = \tfrac12mv^2 = \dfrac{e^2}{8\pi\varepsilon_0 r}\) … (i)

Quantisation: \(mvr = \dfrac{nh}{2\pi}\). Dividing \(mv^2 r = \dfrac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\) by it: \(v = \dfrac{e^2}{2\varepsilon_0 nh}\) … (ii); then \(r = \dfrac{nh}{2\pi m v} = \dfrac{\varepsilon_0n^2h^2}{\pi me^2}\).

Put \(r\) in (i):

\(K_n = \dfrac{me^4}{8\varepsilon_0^2n^2h^2}\) \(= \dfrac{13.6}{n^2}\) eV (and \(U = -2K\), \(E = -K\)).

Concepts to recall

  • \(mvr = nh/2\pi\); \(r_n\propto n^2\), \(v_n\propto 1/n\).
  • \(K = -E = -U/2\).
Q12(b)Dual Nature of Radiation and Matter[2]

An electron is accelerated through a potential difference of 300 V. What is its de Broglie wavelength associated with the electron in Å unit?

Answer

\(\lambda = \dfrac{h}{\sqrt{2meV}} = \dfrac{6.6\times10^{-34}}{\sqrt{2\times9.1\times10^{-31}\times1.6\times10^{-19}\times300}}\)

\(= \dfrac{6.6\times10^{-34}}{9.35\times10^{-24}} = 7.06\times10^{-11}\) m = 0.71 Å (shortcut \(12.27/\sqrt{300}\) Å = 0.708 Å).

Concepts to recall

  • \(eV = p^2/2m\) ⇒ \(\lambda = h/\sqrt{2meV}\).
Q13(b)Atoms and Nuclei — Nuclei[2]

Calculate the energy released in the nuclear reaction \({}_1\mathrm{H}^2 + {}_1\mathrm{H}^2\to{}_2\mathrm{He}^4 + \text{energy}\). (Mass of \({}_1\mathrm{H}^2 = 2.014102\) u, mass of \({}_2\mathrm{He}^4 = 4.002604\) u)

Q14(a)Electronic Devices[3]

Explain with the help of a circuit diagram how a Zener diode works as a voltage regulator. Draw its \(I-V\) characteristics.

Answer

Circuit: the Zener diode is connected in reverse bias across the load \(R_L\); the unregulated d.c. input is applied through a series resistor \(R_s\).

Working: once the input exceeds the breakdown voltage \(V_Z\), the Zener conducts in breakdown, where its voltage stays almost constant at \(V_Z\) over a wide range of current. If the input voltage rises, the current through \(R_s\) increases; the extra current flows through the Zener, the extra voltage drops across \(R_s\), and the output stays at \(V_Z\). If the input falls (or the load current changes) the Zener current adjusts the other way. So \(V_0 = V_Z\) is regulated.

I–V characteristic: forward bias as an ordinary diode; in reverse bias a tiny current until \(V_Z\), then a sharp rise of current at almost constant voltage.

Concepts to recall

  • Zener: heavily doped, sharp reverse breakdown, used in reverse bias.
  • \(V_{\text{in}} = IR_s + V_Z\); \(I = I_Z + I_L\).
