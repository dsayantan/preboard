Q1(A)(i)Electrostatics[1]

A short electric dipole consisting of charges \(+q\) & \(-q\) is placed at the center \(O\) of a large cube of side length \(L\). The electric flux emanating through the cube is:

(a) \(\dfrac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 L}\)   (b) \(\dfrac{q}{2\pi\varepsilon_0 L}\)   (c) Zero   (d) \(\dfrac{q}{3\pi\varepsilon_0 L}\)

Answer

(c) Zero.

Gauss's theorem: \(\phi_E = q_{\text{enclosed}}/\varepsilon_0\). The cube encloses \(+q\) and \(-q\), net charge zero, so \(\phi_E = 0\) (as many field lines enter the cube as leave it).

Note also that options (a), (b), (d) have the dimensions of potential (V), not flux (V m).

Concepts to recall

  • Gauss's theorem: \(\oint \vec E\cdot d\vec A = q_{\text{enc}}/\varepsilon_0\).
  • Only the net enclosed charge matters; size and shape of the surface do not.
Q1(A)(ii)Current Electricity[1]

The equivalent resistance between points \(a\) and \(f\) of the electric network given below is.

(a) 24 Ω   (b) 110 Ω   (c) 140 Ω   (d) 200 Ω

Answer

(c) 140 Ω.

Points \(c\) and \(d\) are free ends, so no current flows in the 20 Ω and 40 Ω resistors; they play no part.

The current from \(a\) to \(f\) must go \(a\to b\to e\to f\): 10 Ω, 50 Ω and 80 Ω in series.

\(R_{af} = 10 + 50 + 80 = \) 140 Ω

Concepts to recall

  • A resistor with one end free carries no current.
  • Series: \(R = R_1 + R_2 + \dots\)
Q1(A)(iii)Optics — Ray Optics[1]

To a bird in air a fish in water \((\mu = 4/3)\) appears to be at 30 cm from the surface. The real depth of the fish is:

(a) 22.5 cm   (b) 30 cm   (c) 40 cm   (d) 45 cm

Answer

(c) 40 cm.

Viewing from the rarer medium (air) into the denser medium (water): \(\mu = \dfrac{\text{real depth}}{\text{apparent depth}}\).

Real depth \(= \mu \times 30 = \tfrac43\times 30 = \) 40 cm

Concepts to recall

  • \(\mu = \text{real depth}/\text{apparent depth}\) when viewed from air.
  • An object in a denser medium appears raised (nearer the surface).

If the radius of second electron orbit in hydrogen atom be r then the radius of the third orbit will be:

(a) \(3r\)   (b) \(2.25r\)   (c) \(9r\)   (d) \(\dfrac{r}{3}\)

(b) 2.25 r.

Q1(A)(v)Atoms and Nuclei — Nuclei[1]

Binding energy of a nucleus is of the order of:

(a) eV   (b) keV   (c) MeV   (d) joule

Answer

(c) MeV. Binding energy per nucleon is about 7–9 MeV, so a nucleus is bound by a few MeV to about 2000 MeV — the MeV is the natural unit (atomic electrons are bound by eV).

Concepts to recall

Write an expression for magnetic flux density \(B\) at a point on the end-on position or an axial position of a magnetic dipole. (No derivation is expected)

Q1(B)(iii)Electrostatics[1]

When two capacitors, one \(3\,\mu\)F and other of \(6\,\mu\)F are connected in series and the combination is charged to a potential difference of 120 V then what is the potential difference across the \(3\,\mu\)F capacitor?

Q1(B)(vii)Magnetic Effects of Current and Magnetism[1]

A very long straight wire carries a current \(I\). At the instant when a charge \(+Q\) at a point P at a distance \(a\) has velocity \(\vec v\) (shown in figure), find the magnitude and direction of force on the charge.

Answer

Field of the wire at P: \(B = \dfrac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi a}\); by the right-hand rule (current upward, P to its right) \(\vec B\) is into the page.

\(\vec v\) (away from the wire) ⟂ \(\vec B\): \(F = QvB\sin 90^\circ\).

Q1(B)(viii)Electromagnetic Induction and Alternating Currents[1]

A closed conducting loop moves normal to the electric field between the plates of a large capacitor. Is there any current induced in the loop when it is: a) wholly inside the capacitor b) partially outside the plates of the capacitor? The electric field is normal to the plane of the loop.

Answer

No current in either case. A current is induced only by a change of magnetic flux through the loop (Faraday). A static electric field between charged plates produces no magnetic field, so the magnetic flux through the loop stays zero whether the loop is wholly inside (a) or partly outside (b) the plates.

Concepts to recall

  • Faraday: \(\varepsilon = -d\phi_B/dt\) — magnetic flux only.

Draw the graph showing the variation of capacitive reactance \(X_C\) of a capacitor with frequency \(f\) of an a.c circuit.

Answer

\(X_C = \dfrac{1}{2\pi f C}\), so \(X_C \propto 1/f\): a rectangular hyperbola, very large at low \(f\) (infinite for d.c.) and tending to zero at high \(f\).

Q1(B)(x)Optics — Wave Optics[1]

State one condition for obtaining sustained interference of light.

Answer

The two sources must be coherent — they must emit waves of the same frequency with a constant (time-independent) phase difference (in practice, both derived from a single source).

Other acceptable conditions: equal (or nearly equal) amplitudes; narrow sources close to each other; monochromatic light.

Concepts to recall

  • Sustained interference needs a constant phase difference → coherent sources.
Q1(B)(xi)Optics — Ray Optics[1]

Define angular magnification of an optical instrument.

Answer

Angular magnification (magnifying power) is the ratio of the angle subtended at the eye by the final image, \(\beta\), to the angle subtended at the eye by the object seen directly (placed at the least distance of distinct vision for a microscope, at its actual position for a telescope), \(\alpha\): \(M = \beta/\alpha\)

Concepts to recall

  • Simple microscope: \(M = 1 + D/f\) (image at D), \(D/f\) (image at ∞).

A particle is dropped from a height \(H\). Write down the expression for the de Broglie wavelength of the particle as a function of height.

\(\lambda = \dfrac{h}{p} = \dfrac{h}{m\sqrt{2gH}}\)

Q1(B)(xv)Electronic Devices[1]

What are extrinsic semiconductors?

Answer

An extrinsic semiconductor is a pure (intrinsic) semiconductor such as Si or Ge doped with a small, controlled amount of impurity to increase its conductivity: a pentavalent impurity (P, As) gives an n-type semiconductor (electrons majority carriers); a trivalent impurity (B, Al, In) gives a p-type semiconductor (holes majority carriers).

Concepts to recall

  • Donor (group 15) → n-type; acceptor (group 13) → p-type.

Q2(a)Electrostatics[2]

An electric dipole of dipole moment \(\vec p\) is placed in a uniform electric field \(\vec E\) with its axis inclined to the field. Write an expression for the torque \(\vec\tau\) experienced by the dipole in vector form. Show diagrammatically how the dipole should be kept in the electric field so that the torque acting on it is: i) maximum   ii) zero

Answer

\(\vec\tau = \vec p\times\vec E\), magnitude \(\tau = pE\sin\theta\), \(\theta\) = angle between \(\vec p\) and \(\vec E\).

(i) Maximum, \(\tau = pE\), when \(\theta = 90^\circ\): dipole axis perpendicular to the field.

(ii) Zero when \(\theta = 0^\circ\) (stable) or \(180^\circ\) (unstable): axis along the field.

Concepts to recall

  • Net force on a dipole in a uniform field is zero; the two forces form a couple.
  • Arm of the couple \(= 2l\sin\theta\) ⇒ \(\tau = qE\cdot2l\sin\theta = pE\sin\theta\).
Q2(b)Electrostatics[3]

A capacitor of \(4\,\mu\)F is connected to 400 V supply. It is then disconnected and connected to an uncharged capacitor of \(2\,\mu\)F. Calculate: i) Common potential after the capacitors are connected together. ii) Loss of energy in the form of heat and radiation.

Answer

Charge on the first: \(Q = C_1V_1 = 4\times10^{-6}\times400 = 1.6\times10^{-3}\) C. Charge is conserved on sharing.

(i) \(V = \dfrac{C_1V_1}{C_1+C_2} = \dfrac{1.6\times10^{-3}}{6\times10^{-6}}\) = 266.7 V

(ii) \(U_i = \tfrac12 C_1V_1^2 = \tfrac12(4\times10^{-6})(400)^2 = 0.320\) J

\(U_f = \tfrac12(C_1+C_2)V^2 = \tfrac12(6\times10^{-6})(266.7)^2 = 0.2133\) J

Loss \(= U_i - U_f\) = 0.107 J (check: \(\dfrac{C_1C_2}{2(C_1+C_2)}V_1^2 = \dfrac{8\times10^{-12}}{12\times10^{-6}}\times160000 = 0.107\) J).

Concepts to recall

  • Charge is conserved when capacitors share charge; energy is not.
  • \(U = \tfrac12CV^2 = Q^2/2C\); loss \(= \dfrac{C_1C_2(V_1-V_2)^2}{2(C_1+C_2)}\).
Q3(a)Electrostatics[2]

Derive using Gauss' theorem an expression for the electric field intensity at a distance \(r\) from an infinitely long line of charge having a uniform charge density \(\lambda\) coulomb/m.

Answer

By symmetry \(\vec E\) is radial (perpendicular to the line) and has the same magnitude at all points at distance \(r\). Choose a coaxial Gaussian cylinder of radius \(r\) and length \(l\).

Flux through the flat caps is zero (\(\vec E \perp d\vec A\)). On the curved surface \(\vec E \parallel d\vec A\) and \(E\) is constant: \(\phi = E(2\pi r l)\).

Charge enclosed \(= \lambda l\). Gauss: \(E(2\pi r l) = \dfrac{\lambda l}{\varepsilon_0}\)

\(E = \dfrac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0 r}\), radially outward for \(\lambda>0\).

Concepts to recall

  • Gaussian surface: matches the symmetry; \(E\) constant and ∥ or ⟂ to \(d\vec A\) on each part.
  • Line: \(E\propto 1/r\); sheet: \(\sigma/2\varepsilon_0\) (constant); shell: \(q/4\pi\varepsilon_0 r^2\) outside, 0 inside.
Q3(b)Electrostatics[3]

Two positive charges of \(10\,\mu\)C each are situated 10 cm apart from each other. What is the increase in the electrostatic potential energy of the system when two charges are brought closer by 7 cm?

Answer

\(U = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\dfrac{q_1q_2}{r}\), \(q_1q_2 = (10^{-5})^2 = 10^{-10}\) C².

\(r_1 = 0.10\) m: \(U_1 = 9\times10^{9}\times10^{-10}/0.10 = 9.0\) J.

\(r_2 = 0.10 - 0.07 = 0.03\) m: \(U_2 = 0.9/0.03 = 30.0\) J.

Increase \(\Delta U = U_2 - U_1\) = 21 J (work done against the repulsion).

Concepts to recall

  • \(U = q_1q_2/4\pi\varepsilon_0 r\); like charges: \(U\) rises as they approach.
Q4(a)Current Electricity[2]

Why is a potentiometer preferred to a voltmeter in measuring the emf of a cell?

Answer

A voltmeter draws a current from the cell, so it reads the terminal p.d. \(V = \varepsilon - Ir\), which is less than the emf.

A potentiometer uses a null method: at the balance point no current is drawn from the cell, so there is no drop across its internal resistance and the balancing p.d. equals the emf exactly. (It is also more sensitive, since a long wire gives a small potential gradient.)

Concepts to recall

  • \(V = \varepsilon - Ir\) when current flows; \(V = \varepsilon\) only when \(I = 0\).
  • Potentiometer principle: \(V = Kl\) for a wire of uniform cross-section and steady current.

For what value of unknown resistance \(x\), the p.d between B and D is zero in the arrangement shown in figure.

Q5(b)Magnetic Effects of Current and Magnetism[3]

What is hysteresis loss? How does the magnetic permeability \(\mu\) differ for dia, para and ferromagnetic materials?

Answer

Hysteresis loss: when a ferromagnetic material is taken through a complete cycle of magnetisation, the magnetisation lags behind the magnetising field and energy is dissipated as heat in the material. The energy lost per unit volume per cycle equals the area of the B–H loop.

Permeability (\(\mu_r = \mu/\mu_0 = 1+\chi_m\)):

  • Diamagnetic: \(\mu_r\) slightly less than 1 (\(\chi_m\) small, negative), e.g. Bi, Cu.
  • Paramagnetic: \(\mu_r\) slightly greater than 1 (\(\chi_m\) small, positive), e.g. Al, Pt.
  • Ferromagnetic: \(\mu_r \gg 1\) (hundreds to thousands) and depends on \(H\), e.g. Fe, Ni, Co.

Concepts to recall

  • Retentivity: \(B\) left when \(H = 0\); coercivity: reverse \(H\) needed to make \(B = 0\).
  • Transformer cores: soft iron — narrow loop (low hysteresis loss).

An \(\alpha\) particle and a proton are moving in the plane of the paper in a region of uniform magnetic field \((\vec B)\), directed normal to the plane of the paper. If the two particles have equal linear momenta, what will be the ratio of the radii of their trajectories in the field?

Q6(b)Electromagnetic Induction and Alternating Currents[3]

A 0.5 m long metal rod PQ completes the circuit, as shown in the figure. The area of the circuit is perpendicular to the magnetic field of flux density 0.15 T. If the resistance of the total circuit is 3 Ω, calculate the force needed to move the rod in the direction as indicated in figure, with a constant speed 2 m/s.

Q7(a)Electromagnetic Induction and Alternating Currents[2]

What is the power consumed by an ideal inductor even though both V and I are non-zero? Explain your answer in brief.

Answer

Zero. In an ideal (resistanceless) inductor the current lags the voltage by \(\pi/2\), so the power factor \(\cos\varphi = \cos 90^\circ = 0\) and \(P = V_{\text{rms}}I_{\text{rms}}\cos\varphi = 0\).

Energy stored in the magnetic field during one quarter cycle is returned to the source in the next quarter, so the average power over a cycle is zero (wattless current).

Concepts to recall

  • \(P_{\text{av}} = V_{\text{rms}}I_{\text{rms}}\cos\varphi\), \(\cos\varphi = R/Z\).
Q7(b)Electromagnetic Induction and Alternating Currents[3]

An LCR series circuit with 100 Ω resistance is connected to an ac source of 200 V and angular frequency 300 rad/s. When only the capacitance is removed, the current lags behind the voltage by \(60^0\). When only the inductance is removed, the current leads the voltage by \(60^0\). Calculate the current and power dissipated in LCR circuit.

Answer

LR circuit: \(\tan 60^\circ = X_L/R\Rightarrow X_L = 100\sqrt3\) Ω.

CR circuit: \(\tan 60^\circ = X_C/R\Rightarrow X_C = 100\sqrt3\) Ω.

In LCR: \(X_L = X_C\) — resonance, \(Z = \sqrt{R^2+(X_L-X_C)^2} = R = 100\) Ω.

\(I = V/Z = 200/100\) = 2 A

\(P = I^2R = 4\times100\) = 400 W (power factor 1).

Concepts to recall

  • \(\tan\varphi = (X_L - X_C)/R\); \(Z = \sqrt{R^2 + (X_L-X_C)^2}\).
  • At resonance \(Z = R\), current and power are maximum.

Q8(a)Optics — Wave Optics[2]

Can two sodium vapour lamps act as coherent sources? Explain in brief.

Answer

No. Light from a lamp is emitted by millions of atoms independently, in short wave trains (about \(10^{-8}\) s long) with random phases. The phase difference between light from two independent lamps therefore changes randomly and rapidly, so it is not constant: the lamps are incoherent and any interference pattern shifts too fast to be seen (the screen is uniformly illuminated).

Concepts to recall

  • Coherent: same frequency, constant phase difference — obtained from one source (YDSE slits).
Q8(b)Optics — Wave Optics[3]

i) The ratio of the intensities at the maxima and minima in an interference pattern is 25:9. What will be the ratio of the widths of the two slits in the Young's experiment?
ii) What type of wave front is generated from a line source of light?

Answer

(i) \(\dfrac{I_{\max}}{I_{\min}} = \dfrac{(a_1+a_2)^2}{(a_1-a_2)^2} = \dfrac{25}{9}\Rightarrow \dfrac{a_1+a_2}{a_1-a_2} = \dfrac53\Rightarrow a_1 = 4a_2\).

Intensity from a slit ∝ its width ∝ \(a^2\): \(\dfrac{w_1}{w_2} = \dfrac{a_1^2}{a_2^2}\) = 16 : 1

(ii) A line source gives a cylindrical wavefront.

Concepts to recall

  • \(I\propto a^2\); \(I_{\max} = (a_1+a_2)^2\), \(I_{\min} = (a_1-a_2)^2\).
  • Point source → spherical; line source → cylindrical; distant source → plane wavefront.
Q9(b)Optics — Wave Optics[3]

Fraunhofer's diffraction from a single slit of width \(1.24\times10^{-6}\) m is observed with light of wavelength 6200 Å. Calculate the angular width of the central maximum.

Answer

First minima at \(a\sin\theta = \lambda\): \(\sin\theta = \dfrac{6.2\times10^{-7}}{1.24\times10^{-6}} = 0.5\Rightarrow\theta = 30^\circ\).

Angular width of central maximum \(= 2\theta\) = 60° (\(\pi/3\) rad).

Here \(\lambda/a = 0.5\) is not small, so use \(\sin\theta\), not the approximation \(2\lambda/a = 1\) rad (57.3°).

Concepts to recall

  • Single slit minima: \(a\sin\theta_n = n\lambda\); central maximum spans \(-\theta_1\) to \(+\theta_1\).
  • Small angles: angular width \(2\lambda/a\).
Q10(a)Optics — Ray Optics[3]

Deduce the relation \(\dfrac{\mu_2}{v} - \dfrac{\mu_1}{u} = \dfrac{\mu_2-\mu_1}{R}\) when refraction takes place from rarer to denser medium \((\mu_2 > \mu_1)\) in case of a convex spherical surface and the image formed is real.

Answer

Let a convex surface (pole P, centre C, radius R) separate media \(\mu_1\) (object side) and \(\mu_2\). A ray from the point object O meets the surface at N and is refracted to form the real image I. CN is the normal at N; NM ⟂ axis. \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\) are the angles made with the axis by ON, IN and CN.

Exterior angles: in \(\triangle\)ONC, \(i = \alpha + \gamma\); in \(\triangle\)NCI, \(\gamma = r + \beta\Rightarrow r = \gamma - \beta\).

Snell's law for small (paraxial) angles: \(\mu_1 i = \mu_2 r\Rightarrow \mu_1(\alpha+\gamma) = \mu_2(\gamma-\beta)\).

For paraxial rays M ≈ P: \(\alpha \approx \dfrac{NM}{PO}\), \(\beta\approx\dfrac{NM}{PI}\), \(\gamma\approx\dfrac{NM}{PC}\). Hence \(\dfrac{\mu_1}{PO} + \dfrac{\mu_2}{PI} = \dfrac{\mu_2-\mu_1}{PC}\).

Sign convention (distances from P, positive in the direction of the incident light): \(PO = -u\), \(PI = +v\), \(PC = +R\):

\(\dfrac{\mu_2}{v} - \dfrac{\mu_1}{u} = \dfrac{\mu_2-\mu_1}{R}\)

Concepts to recall

  • Snell's law \(\mu_1\sin i = \mu_2\sin r\); paraxial: \(\sin\theta\approx\tan\theta\approx\theta\).
  • Exterior angle of a triangle = sum of the interior opposite angles.
Q10(b)Optics — Ray Optics[2]

A point object is placed at a distance of 12 cm on the axis of a convex lens of focal length 10 cm. On the other side of the lens; a convex mirror is placed at a distance of 10 cm from the lens such that the image formed by the combination coincides with the object itself. What is the focal length of the convex mirror?

Answer

Lens: \(\dfrac1v - \dfrac1u = \dfrac1f\) with \(u = -12\), \(f = +10\): \(\dfrac1v = \dfrac1{10} - \dfrac1{12} = \dfrac1{60}\Rightarrow v = +60\) cm.

The image would form 60 − 10 = 50 cm behind the mirror. For the light to retrace its path (image coinciding with the object), the rays must strike the mirror normally, i.e. head towards its centre of curvature. So \(R = 50\) cm.

\(f = R/2\) = 25 cm

Concepts to recall

  • Rays aimed at a mirror's centre of curvature strike it normally and retrace their path.
  • Thin lens \(\frac1v-\frac1u=\frac1f\); mirror \(f = R/2\).
Q11(b)Optics — Ray Optics[3]

i) State how the resolving power of the astronomical telescope can be increased.
ii) A compound microscope consists of an objective of focal length 2 cm and an eyepiece of focal length 5 cm. When an object is placed 2.4 cm from the objective, final image is virtual and 25 cm from the eyepiece. Determine the magnifying power of the compound microscope in this set up, i.e in normal use.

Answer

(i) Resolving power of a telescope \(= \dfrac{D}{1.22\lambda}\): increase the diameter (aperture) of the objective — which is why large telescopes use big mirrors (reflecting telescopes). (Using shorter wavelength also increases it.)

ISC 2027 names "resolving power of compound microscope" and the advantages of the reflecting telescope (large aperture); the telescope's resolving power is the same idea, so this part is kept.

(ii) Objective: \(u_o = -2.4\), \(f_o = 2\): \(\dfrac1{v_o} = \dfrac12 - \dfrac1{2.4} = \dfrac1{12}\Rightarrow v_o = 12\) cm; \(m_o = \dfrac{v_o}{u_o}\), \(|m_o| = \dfrac{12}{2.4} = 5\).

Eyepiece with final image at \(D = 25\) cm: \(m_e = 1 + \dfrac{D}{f_e} = 1 + \dfrac{25}{5} = 6\) (the intermediate image is \(u_e = 25\times5/30 = 4.17\) cm from it).

\(M = m_o\,m_e = 5\times6\) = 30

Concepts to recall

  • Compound microscope (image at D): \(M = \dfrac{v_o}{u_o}\left(1+\dfrac{D}{f_e}\right)\).
  • Telescope resolving power ∝ aperture/λ.

Q12(a)Dual Nature of Radiation and Matter[3]

In the experiment of photoelectric effect, the graph of maximum kinetic energy \(E_k\) of the emitted photoelectrons versus the frequency \(\nu\) of the incident light is a straight line AB as shown in the figure below, find:

Q13(a)Atoms and Nuclei — Atoms[3]

On the basis of Bohr's theory, derive an expression for the radius of the nth orbit of an electron of hydrogen atom.

Answer

Postulates used: (1) the electron moves in a circular orbit, the Coulomb attraction of the nucleus providing the centripetal force; (2) only those orbits are allowed for which the angular momentum is an integral multiple of \(h/2\pi\).

(1) \(\dfrac{mv^2}{r} = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\dfrac{e^2}{r^2}\Rightarrow mv^2r = \dfrac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\) … (i)

(2) \(mvr = \dfrac{nh}{2\pi}\Rightarrow v = \dfrac{nh}{2\pi mr}\) … (ii)

Substitute (ii) in (i): \(m\dfrac{n^2h^2}{4\pi^2m^2r^2}\,r = \dfrac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\Rightarrow \dfrac{n^2h^2}{4\pi^2 m r} = \dfrac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\)

\(r_n = \dfrac{\varepsilon_0 n^2h^2}{\pi m e^2}\) \(\;\propto n^2\); for \(n = 1\), \(r_1 = 0.53\) Å (Bohr radius).

Concepts to recall

  • Bohr quantisation: \(mvr = nh/2\pi\).
  • Coulomb force = centripetal force.
Q14(b)Atoms and Nuclei — Nuclei[3]

In a star, three alpha particle fuse together to form \({}_6\mathrm{C}^{12}\) nucleus. Compute the energy evolved. The masses of \(\alpha\)-particle and \({}_6\mathrm{C}^{12}\) 4.002604u and 12.000000u respectively.

Answer

\(3\,{}^4_2\mathrm{He}\to{}^{12}_6\mathrm{C} + Q\)

Mass defect \(\Delta m = 3(4.002604) - 12.000000 = 12.007812 - 12.000000 = 0.007812\) u.

\(Q = \Delta m\times931\) MeV \(= 0.007812\times931\) = 7.27 MeV (\(\approx 1.16\times10^{-12}\) J).

Concepts to recall

  • Energy released \(= (\text{mass of reactants} - \text{mass of products})\times931\) MeV/u.
  • Fusion of light nuclei releases energy (BE/A increases).
Q15(a)(i)Electronic Devices[2]

Draw a labelled diagram of a full wave rectifier. Show how output voltage varies with time, if input voltage is a sinusoidal voltage.

[The paper prints [3+2] for Q15: 3 marks for (a)(i) and (ii) together, split not printed. Taken here as 2 for the diagram with waveforms and 1 for "what is modulation".]

Answer

Centre-tap full-wave rectifier: the a.c. input is applied to the primary of a transformer whose secondary has a centre tap T. The ends S₁, S₂ feed diodes D₁ and D₂, whose cathodes are joined; the load \(R_L\) is connected between this junction and T.

Positive half cycle: S₁ is positive w.r.t. T, D₁ is forward biased and conducts, D₂ is reverse biased. Negative half cycle: S₂ is positive, D₂ conducts, D₁ is off.

In both halves the current through \(R_L\) is in the same direction, so the output is a unidirectional (pulsating d.c.) voltage with a hump in every half cycle — frequency twice the input frequency.

Concepts to recall

  • Forward-biased diode conducts; reverse-biased does not.
  • Half-wave: output only in alternate half cycles; full-wave: in both.
