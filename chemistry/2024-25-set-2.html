Q1(A)(ii)Coordination Compounds[1]

(ii) In the complex [Co(NH3)5(NO2)]Cl, the coordination number and the oxidation number of the central metal ion are ______ and ______.

Answer

Six donor atoms (5 N of NH3 + 1 N of \(\ce{NO2-}\)) surround Co: coordination number six.

One \(\ce{Cl-}\) outside the bracket ⇒ the complex ion is \(\ce{[Co(NH3)5(NO2)]+}\): \(x + 5(0) + (-1) = +1 \Rightarrow x = +2\).

six; +2

The familiar compound is [Co(NH3)5(NO2)]Cl2, in which cobalt is +3. With the formula as printed (one Cl) the oxidation number is +2; both +2 and +3 are in the word list, so write the value the printed formula gives and show the working.

Concepts to recall

  • Coordination number = number of donor atoms bonded to the metal (not the number of ligands, for polydentate ligands).
  • Charge of complex ion = oxidation state of metal + sum of ligand charges.

Q1(B)(i)Organic Compounds containing Nitrogen[1]

(B) Select and write the correct alternative from the choices given below: [7×1]

(i) Decreasing order of basicity of the three isomers of nitro aniline is :

(a) p-nitroaniline > o- nitroaniline > m- nitroaniline
(b) p-nitroaniline > m- nitroaniline > o- nitroaniline
(c) m-nitroaniline > p- nitroaniline > o- nitroaniline
(d) m-nitroaniline > o- nitroaniline > p- nitroaniline

Answer

–NO2 withdraws electrons from the ring and so from the N lone pair. At o- and p- positions it acts by both −R (resonance) and −I; at m- by −I only, so m-nitroaniline is the strongest base. The o-isomer is weakest (strongest −I at short range, plus intramolecular H-bonding and steric hindrance to protonation — the ortho effect).

(c) m-nitroaniline > p-nitroaniline > o-nitroaniline

Concepts to recall

  • Electron-withdrawing groups lower the basicity of aniline; the effect is largest at o- and p- (resonance) and smallest at m- (inductive only).

(ii) If two liquids A and B form minimum boiling azeotrope at some specific composition, then

(a) A–B interactions are stronger than those between A–A or B–B.

(vi) Given below are two statements marked Assertion and Reason. Read the two statements carefully and select the correct option.

Assertion: Tertiary alkyl halides are most reactive towards SN1 reaction.
Reason: In SN1 reaction rate of reaction depends only on the concentration of alkyl halide.

Q1(B)(vii)d- and f-Block Elements[1]

(vii) Given below are two statements marked Assertion and Reason. Read the two statements carefully and select the correct option.

Assertion: Actinoids form relatively more stable complexes as compared to lanthanoids.
Reason: Actinoids can utilise their 5f orbitals along with 6d orbitals in bonding but lanthanoids do not use their 4f orbital for bonding

(a) Both assertion and reason are true, and reason is the correct explanation of assertion.
(b) Both assertion and reason are true, but reason is not the correct explanation for assertion.
(c) Assertion is true, but reason is false.
(d) Assertion is false, but reason is true.

Answer

Actinoids have a greater tendency to form complexes. The 5f orbitals are less deeply buried than the 4f, extend beyond the 6s/6p shells and can take part in bonding (with 6d), so actinoid–ligand bonds have more covalent character; the 4f orbitals of lanthanoids are shielded and do not take part. The reason explains the assertion.

(a) Both true, and reason is the correct explanation

Concepts to recall

  • 4f: deeply buried, little part in bonding. 5f: more exposed, used in bonding → more oxidation states and more complexes for actinoids.
Q1(C)Chemical Kinetics[3]

C. Read the passage given below carefully and answer the questions that follow: [3]

In a reaction, the rates of disappearance of different reactants or rates of formation of different products may not be equal but rate of reaction at any instant of time has the same value expected in terms of any reactant or product. Further, the rate of reaction may not depend upon the stoichiometric coefficients of the balanced chemical equation. The exact powers of molar concentrations of reactants on which rate depend are found experimentally and expressed in terms of ‘order of reaction’. Each reaction has a characteristics rate constant depends upon temperature. The units of the rate constant depend upon the order of reaction.

(i) The rate constant of a reaction is found to be 3 x 10-3 mol-2L2sec-1. What is the order of the reaction?

(ii) If the half-life of a first order reaction is 50 minutes, what percentages of reactant will be left after 250 minutes?

(iii) Rate of a reaction can be expressed by following rate expression, Rate = k[A]2[B]. If concentration of A is increased by 3 times and concentration of B is increased by 2 times, by how many times rate of reaction increases?

Answer

(i) Units of k for order n: (mol L−1)1−n s−1. Here mol−2 L2 s−1 = (mol L−1)−2 s−1 ⇒ \(1 - n = -2\).

n = 3: third order

(ii) For a first-order reaction the half-life is independent of concentration. Number of half-lives \(= \dfrac{250}{50} = 5\).

\[\frac{[\mathrm A]}{[\mathrm A]_0} = \left(\frac12\right)^5 = \frac1{32} = 0.03125\]

3.125 % of the reactant is left

(iii) \(\text{Rate}_2 = k(3[\mathrm A])^2(2[\mathrm B]) = 9\times2\,k[\mathrm A]^2[\mathrm B]\)

The rate increases 18 times

Concepts to recall

  • Units of k: (mol L−1)1−n time−1.
  • First order: after n half-lives, fraction left = (1/2)n.
  • Rate = k[A]x[B]y: scaling [A] by a and [B] by b scales the rate by axby.

Q2Electrochemistry[2]

Sourav, a student of class 12 prepared a 0.01 M H2SO4 solution and found that its conductivity and equivalent conductivities are 1.65 × 10-4 S cm-1 and 16.5 S cm2 eqv-1. Are these data justified? Explain.

Answer

\(\ce{H2SO4}\) is dibasic: 1 mol = 2 g-equivalents, so normality N = 2 × 0.01 = 0.02 N.

\[\Lambda_\text{eq} = \frac{1000\,\kappa}{N} = \frac{1000\times1.65\times10^{-4}}{0.02} = 8.25\ \text{S cm}^2\ \text{eq}^{-1}\]

\[\Lambda_m = \frac{1000\,\kappa}{M} = \frac{1000\times1.65\times10^{-4}}{0.01} = 16.5\ \text{S cm}^2\ \text{mol}^{-1}\]

Not justified: with κ = 1.65 × 10−4 S cm−1 the equivalent conductivity is 8.25 S cm2 eq−1. 16.5 is the molar conductivity (Λm = 2Λeq for H2SO4).

Sourav has divided by the molarity instead of the normality.

Concepts to recall

  • \(\Lambda_\text{eq} = 1000\kappa/N\), \(\Lambda_m = 1000\kappa/M\).
  • For \(\ce{H2SO4}\), N = 2M, so \(\Lambda_m = 2\Lambda_\text{eq}\).
Q3Aldehydes, Ketones and Carboxylic Acids[2]

i) A compound having the molecular formula C3H6O forms a crystalline white ppt. with sodium bisulphite and reduces Fehling’s solution. Suggest the structural formula and IUPAC name of this compound.

ii) CH3CHO is more reactive than CH3COCH3 towards reaction with HCN. Explain

Answer

(i) A bisulphite adduct shows a carbonyl compound; reducing Fehling's solution shows an aldehyde (acetone, the other C3H6O carbonyl, does not reduce Fehling's).

\(\ce{CH3CH2CHO}\), propanal

\(\ce{CH3CH2CHO + NaHSO3 -> CH3CH2CH(OH)SO3Na}\) (white crystalline adduct)

\(\ce{CH3CH2CHO + 2Cu^2+ + 5OH- -> CH3CH2COO- + Cu2O v + 3H2O}\) (red ppt.)

(ii) Addition of HCN is a nucleophilic addition: \(\ce{CN-}\) attacks the carbonyl carbon.

  • Electronic: the two CH3 groups of acetone push electrons (+I) to the carbonyl carbon and reduce its positive character more than the one CH3 of ethanal does.
  • Steric: the two CH3 groups crowd the carbonyl carbon of acetone and hinder the approach of \(\ce{CN-}\); in ethanal one side carries only H.

Hence ethanal is more reactive than propanone.

Concepts to recall

  • NaHSO3 adduct: aldehydes and methyl ketones. Fehling's/Tollens': aldehydes only.
  • Reactivity to nucleophilic addition: HCHO > RCHO > R2CO (+I and steric effects).

Q5Chemical Kinetics[2]

The rate constant of a first order reaction at T K is given by the following expression

\[\log k = \frac{-3163}{T} + 11.9\]

K is expressed in min-1. What is the activation energy and half-life period of the reaction at 20 oC?

Answer

Compare with the Arrhenius equation in log form: \(\log k = \log A - \dfrac{E_a}{2.303RT}\).

\[\frac{E_a}{2.303R} = 3163 \;\Rightarrow\; E_a = 3163\times2.303\times8.314 = 6.06\times10^{4}\ \text{J mol}^{-1}\]

\(E_a \approx 60.6\) kJ mol−1

At 20 °C, T = 293 K: \(\log k = -\dfrac{3163}{293} + 11.9 = -10.795 + 11.9 = 1.105\) ⇒ \(k = 12.7\) min−1.

\[t_{1/2} = \frac{0.693}{k} = \frac{0.693}{12.7} = 0.0544\ \text{min}\]

\(t_{1/2} \approx 5.44\times10^{-2}\) min (about 3.3 s)

Concepts to recall

  • \(\log k = \log A - \dfrac{E_a}{2.303RT}\): the coefficient of 1/T is \(-E_a/2.303R\).
  • First order: \(t_{1/2} = 0.693/k\).

Q7Coordination Compounds[2]

During chemistry class, a teacher wrote [Fe(CN)6]3- as a coordination complex ion on the board. The students were asked to find out the hybridisation and magnetic character of the complex. Pari, a student, wrote the answer sp3d2 and diamagnetic whereas another student Suhail wrote d2sp3and paramagnetic. Evaluate Pari’s and Suhail’s responses.

Answer

Fe in \(\ce{[Fe(CN)6]^3-}\): \(x + 6(-1) = -3 \Rightarrow x = +3\); \(\ce{Fe^3+}\) = [Ar]3d5.

\(\ce{CN-}\) is a strong-field ligand: it forces pairing, so the five electrons occupy three 3d orbitals (2, 2, 1), leaving two inner 3d orbitals free. Two 3d + one 4s + three 4p → d2sp3 (inner-orbital, octahedral). One electron remains unpaired.

Suhail is correct: d2sp3, paramagnetic (1 unpaired e−, μ = √3 ≈ 1.73 BM). Pari is wrong on both counts.

sp3d2 would need a weak-field ligand (outer-orbital, 5 unpaired electrons), and with an odd number of electrons (d5) the complex can never be diamagnetic.

Concepts to recall

  • Strong-field ligands (CN−, CO) → pairing → inner-orbital d2sp3.
  • μ = √(n(n+2)) BM; an odd-electron ion is always paramagnetic.

Q11Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers[2]

Write the chemical test to distinguish between the following pairs of compounds.

(a) Methanol and ethanol
(b) Phenol and propan-2-ol

Answer

(a) Iodoform test — warm with I2 and NaOH: ethanol (has CH3CH(OH)–) gives a yellow precipitate of iodoform; methanol gives none.

  • Iodoform test: positive for CH3CH(OH)– and CH3CO– (ethanol is the only 1° alcohol that gives it).

Q13Solutions[3]

The van 't Hoff factor is a measure of the effect of a solute upon colligative properties such as osmotic pressure, relative lowering in vapour pressure, the elevation of boiling point and freezing point depression. It is the ratio between the actual concentration of particles produced when the substance is dissolved, and the concentration of a substance as calculated from its mass.

Consider the two cases given below and answer the questions that follow.

(A) The freezing point depression of a solution containing 2 g benzoic acid in 25 g of benzene is 1.62 K. (Kf for benzene is 4.9 K kg mol-1)
(B) A 0.15 M aqueous solution of KCl exerts an osmotic pressure of 6.8 atm at 310 K.

Answer the following questions.

(i) Calculate the van’t Hoff factor in each case.
(ii) Inferring the value of van’t Hoff factor in both the cases, find out which solution undergoes association and which solution undergoes dissociation.
(iii) Given below is the increasing order of elevation in boiling point of water observed for the following solutions.
0.1 molal glucose < 0.1 molal NaCl < 0.1 molal K2SO4.
Provide a reason to explain if the above ordered arrangement is correct or not

Answer

(i) Case (A) — benzoic acid, \(\ce{C6H5COOH}\), M = 122 g mol−1.

\[\Delta T_f(\text{calc}) = \frac{K_f\times w\times1000}{M\times W} = \frac{4.9\times2\times1000}{122\times25} = 3.21\ \text{K}\]

\[i = \frac{\Delta T_f(\text{obs})}{\Delta T_f(\text{calc})} = \frac{1.62}{3.21} = 0.504\]

i(A) ≈ 0.50

Case (B) — \(\pi = iCRT\):

\[i = \frac{\pi}{CRT} = \frac{6.8}{0.15\times0.0821\times310} = \frac{6.8}{3.818} = 1.78\]

i(B) ≈ 1.78

(ii) i < 1 means fewer particles than formula units: benzoic acid in benzene associates (dimerises through hydrogen bonds, \(\ce{2C6H5COOH <=> (C6H5COOH)2}\)). i > 1 means more particles: KCl in water dissociates (\(\ce{KCl -> K+ + Cl-}\), 78 % dissociated).

(iii) \(\Delta T_b = iK_bm\). At the same molality (0.1 m) and the same solvent, \(\Delta T_b \propto i\): glucose i = 1 (non-electrolyte), NaCl i ≈ 2, K2SO4 i ≈ 3. So \(\Delta T_b\): glucose < NaCl < K2SO4.

The order is correct

Concepts to recall

  • i = observed colligative property / calculated = calculated M / observed M.
  • i < 1: association; i > 1: dissociation.
  • \(\Delta T_b = iK_bm\), \(\Delta T_f = iK_fm\), \(\pi = iCRT\).

Q15Coordination Compounds[3]

Q21(i)Solutions[5]

i) The properties of the solutions which depend only on the number of solute particles but not on the nature of the solute are called colligative properties. Relative lowering in vapour pressure, boiling point elevation, freezing point depression and osmotic pressure are colligative properties of a solution which are widely used for the determination of molar mass of a compound.

a) Which colligative property is preferred for the molar mass determination of macromolecules?

b) What would be the molar mass of compound if 6.21 g of it is dissolved in 24.0 g of CHCl3 from a solution that has a boiling point of 68.04ºC. The boiling point of pure chloroform is 61.7ºC and the boiling point elevation constant Kb for chloroform is 3.63ºC/m

c) Why the freezing point depression of 0.4 M NaCl is nearly twice than that 0.4 M glucose solution?

Answer

(a) Osmotic pressure

Macromolecules (polymers, proteins) have very large molar masses, so a solution of reasonable concentration contains very few moles of solute: \(\Delta T_b\) and \(\Delta T_f\) would be far too small to measure, but the osmotic pressure is measurable. It is also measured at room temperature, so heat-sensitive biomolecules are not damaged.

(b) \(\Delta T_b = 68.04 - 61.7 = 6.34\) °C.

\[M = \frac{K_b\times w\times1000}{\Delta T_b\times W} = \frac{3.63\times6.21\times1000}{6.34\times24.0} = 148\ \text{g mol}^{-1}\]

M ≈ 148 g mol−1

(c) Depression of freezing point is a colligative property: \(\Delta T_f = iK_fm\), proportional to the number of solute particles. NaCl is a strong electrolyte, \(\ce{NaCl -> Na+ + Cl-}\), so 0.4 M NaCl gives about 0.8 M of particles (i ≈ 2); glucose does not dissociate (i = 1). Hence \(\Delta T_f\)(NaCl) ≈ 2 × \(\Delta T_f\)(glucose). (It is slightly less than twice because of interionic attraction.)

Concepts to recall

  • \(M_2 = \dfrac{K_b w_2 \times 1000}{\Delta T_b w_1}\).
  • Osmotic pressure: best for macromolecules (large, measurable at room temperature).
  • \(\Delta T_f = iK_fm\); i = number of particles per formula unit.
