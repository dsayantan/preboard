Q1(A)(ii)Chemical Kinetics[1]

ii. The half-life period of a ------ order reaction is ------ of the concentration of the reactant.

Answer

The half-life period of a first order reaction is independent of the concentration of the reactant.

\(t_{1/2} = \dfrac{0.693}{k}\) — no concentration term appears.

first; independent

Concepts to recall

  • First order: \(t_{1/2} = 0.693/k\), independent of \([\mathrm{A}]_0\).
  • Zero order: \(t_{1/2} = [\mathrm{A}]_0/2k\), directly proportional to \([\mathrm{A}]_0\).

Q1(B)(vii)Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers[1]

(vii) Assertion: m-Nitrophenol is more acidic than ortho and para nitrophenols.
Reason: –NO2 groups at o- and p- position withdraws electrons of the O-H bond by stronger –R effect while the –NO2 group at m- position withdraws electrons of the O-H bond by weaker –I effect.

(a) Both assertion and reason are true and the reason is the correct explanation of assertion.
(b) Both assertion and reason are true but reason is not the correct explanation for assertion.
(c) Assertion is true but reason is false.
(d) Assertion is false but reason is true.

Answer

At o- and p- positions \(\ce{-NO2}\) withdraws electrons by both –R and –I effects and delocalises the negative charge of the phenoxide ion onto its own oxygens; at the m-position only the weaker –I effect acts.   • Electron-withdrawing groups at o/p stabilise phenoxide by resonance (–R) → stronger acid than the m-isomer, which has only –I.
Q1(C)Electrochemistry[3]

(C) Read the passage given below carefully and answer the questions that follow:

In the chemistry lab, Kabir sets up an electrochemical cell as shown below: At room temperature, she found that the initial voltmeter reading was +0.16v.

(i) The standard electrode potential for the Cu2+/Cu electrode is given by

Cu2+(aq) + 2e– → Cu(s); Eo = + 0.34 V

Calculate the electrode potential of the electrode on the left-hand side of the above electrochemical cell.

(ii) Explain the importance of the salt bridge between the two solutions.

(iii) Give the cell representation.

Answer

(i) The right-hand electrode dips in 1.0 mol dm−3 \(\ce{Cu^2+}\), i.e. it is under standard conditions: \(E_\text{right} = E^\circ = +0.34\) V. With the voltmeter reading taken as \(E_\text{cell} = E_\text{right} - E_\text{left}\):

\[E_\text{left} = E_\text{right} - E_\text{cell} = 0.34 - 0.16 = 0.18\ \text{V}\]

\(E_\text{left} = +0.18\) V

The diagram has copper in both beakers, so this is a Cu | Cu2+ concentration cell, not a cell measured against the standard hydrogen electrode: the 0.16 V reading is the difference of the two copper electrode potentials, not the potential of the left electrode itself. The answer above is the one the question's data lead to. Note, however, that the data are not self-consistent: the Nernst equation for 0.15 mol dm−3 \(\ce{Cu^2+}\) gives \(E_\text{left} = 0.34 + \frac{0.0591}{2}\log 0.15 = +0.316\) V, i.e. an emf of only 0.024 V. A reading of 0.16 V would need \([\ce{Cu^2+}] \approx 4\times10^{-6}\) mol dm−3 on the left. The measured reading is used as given.

(ii) Importance of the salt bridge:

  1. It completes the internal circuit: ions move through it, so current can flow between the two solutions without the solutions mixing.
  2. It maintains electrical neutrality of the two half-cells: as the anode solution gains cations (or the cathode solution loses them), anions of the bridge electrolyte migrate to the anode side and cations to the cathode side. Without it, charge would build up and the cell would stop within moments.
  3. It minimises the liquid-junction potential (an inert electrolyte such as KCl or \(\ce{KNO3}\) in agar, whose ions have nearly equal mobilities).

(iii) Oxidation at the left (dilute) electrode, reduction at the right:

\(\ce{Cu(s) | Cu^2+(aq, 0.15 M) || Cu^2+(aq, 1.0 M) | Cu(s)}\)

Concepts to recall

  • \(E_\text{cell} = E_\text{cathode} - E_\text{anode}\) (reduction potentials; cathode written on the right).
  • Nernst: \(E = E^\circ - \frac{0.0591}{n}\log\frac{1}{[\ce{M^{n+}}]}\) at 298 K.
  • Cell notation: anode | anode solution || cathode solution | cathode.

Q2Chemical Kinetics[2]

Carbon dating is used by archaeologists to date trees, plants, and animal remains as well as human artefacts made from wood and leather. If an archaeologist found that the percentage of carbon-14 in the remains of an animal was 10% of what carbon-14 was in the animal's body when the animal died, find the age of this sample.

(Given the half-life of carbon-14 = 5730 years)

Answer

Radioactive decay is a first-order process.

\[k = \frac{0.693}{t_{1/2}} = \frac{0.693}{5730\ \text{y}} = 1.209\times10^{-4}\ \text{y}^{-1}\]

\[t = \frac{2.303}{k}\log\frac{N_0}{N} = \frac{2.303}{1.209\times10^{-4}}\log\frac{100}{10} = \frac{2.303\times 5730}{0.693}\times 1\]

\[t = 1.904\times10^{4}\ \text{y}\]

Age \(\approx 1.90\times10^{4}\) years (about 19 000 years)

Concepts to recall

  • First order: \(k = \frac{2.303}{t}\log\frac{[\mathrm{A}]_0}{[\mathrm{A}]}\); \(t_{1/2} = 0.693/k\).
  • 10% remaining means \(N_0/N = 10\), \(\log 10 = 1\).

Q4Coordination Compounds[2]

Discuss the hybridisation, shape and charge on metal in [Ni(CO)4] complex. Also comment on its magnetic properties.

Answer

Ni (Z = 28): [Ar] 3d84s2. CO is neutral, so the oxidation state (charge) of Ni is 0.

Q5Solutions[2]

A 2 molal solution of sodium chloride in water causes an elevation in the boiling point by 1.88K. What is the value of van't Hoff factor? What does it signify? [Kb = 0.52 K kg mol−1]

Answer

\[\Delta T_b = iK_bm \;\Rightarrow\; i = \frac{\Delta T_b}{K_b m} = \frac{1.88}{0.52\times2} = 1.81\]

\(i = 1.81\)

Significance: \(i > 1\) means the solute dissociates in water (\(\ce{NaCl -> Na+ + Cl-}\)), so there are more particles than formula units. Degree of dissociation \(\alpha = \frac{i-1}{n-1} = \frac{1.81-1}{2-1} = 0.81\), i.e. about 81% dissociated (not 100%, because of interionic attraction at this concentration).

Concepts to recall

  • \(\Delta T_b = iK_bm\).
  • For dissociation into \(n\) ions: \(\alpha = (i-1)/(n-1)\).

Q8(ii) ORd- and f-Block Elements[2]

OR

ii. a. Comment on the nature of bonding in La2O3 and Lu2O3.
b. Zr and Hf exhibit similar properties. Explain [At no of Zr = 40, Hf = 72]

Answer

(a) Because of the lanthanoid contraction, \(\ce{Lu^3+}\) is much smaller than \(\ce{La^3+}\), so it has a greater polarising power. Hence \(\ce{La2O3}\) is the more ionic (and more basic) oxide, while \(\ce{Lu2O3}\) has greater covalent character (and is less basic).

(b) Hf (5d series) follows the lanthanoids. The lanthanoid contraction (poor shielding by the 4f electrons) cancels the expected increase in size from Zr to Hf, so the two have almost identical atomic radii (Zr 160 pm, Hf 159 pm) and ionic radii, with the same outer configuration (\(n\)d2\((n+1)\)s2). Same size + same valence configuration ⇒ very similar properties (they occur together and are hard to separate).

Concepts to recall

  • Lanthanoid contraction: steady decrease in size La → Lu due to poor shielding by 4f electrons.
  • Consequences: basicity of oxides/hydroxides decreases La → Lu; 4d and 5d elements of a group have similar radii (Zr/Hf, Nb/Ta, Mo/W).

Q10Chemical Kinetics[2]

The rate constant of a first order reaction becomes six times when the temperature is increased from 350K to 410 K. Calculate activation energy [Ea] for the reaction.

Answer

Arrhenius equation in two-temperature form:

\[\log\frac{k_2}{k_1} = \frac{E_a}{2.303R}\left(\frac{T_2-T_1}{T_1T_2}\right)\]

\[\log 6 = \frac{E_a}{2.303\times8.314}\left(\frac{410-350}{350\times410}\right)\]

\[0.7782 = \frac{E_a}{19.147}\times4.181\times10^{-4}\]

\[E_a = \frac{0.7782\times19.147}{4.181\times10^{-4}} = 3.563\times10^{4}\ \text{J mol}^{-1}\]

\(E_a \approx 35.6\) kJ mol−1

Concepts to recall

  • \(\log\frac{k_2}{k_1} = \frac{E_a}{2.303R}\cdot\frac{T_2-T_1}{T_1T_2}\), R = 8.314 J K−1 mol−1.
