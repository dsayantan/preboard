Q1(a)(ii)Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers[1]

Fill in the blanks with appropriate words given below: [Greater, lesser, nitric acid, nitrous acid, common ion effect, salt hydrolysis, decreased, increased, does not change, acidic, basic, neutral, PCl5, SOCl2, methyl cyanide, primary amine, vapour, vapourpressure, positive, negative]

ii) Ethanol is ________ to litmus while phenol is ________ to litmus.

Answer

Ethanol is neutral to litmus while phenol is acidic to litmus.

Phenol ionises slightly in water, \(\ce{C6H5OH + H2O <=> C6H5O- + H3O+}\), because the phenoxide ion is stabilised by resonance; ethanol (\(\mathrm{p}K_a\approx16\)) does not ionise appreciably.

Concepts to recall

  • Phenoxide is resonance-stabilised; alkoxide is destabilised by the +I alkyl group.

Q1(c)(i)Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers[1]

Explain the following facts in brief:

(i) Unlike most phenolic compounds, 2,4,6-trinitrophenol is soluble in sodium bicarbonate solution.

Answer

The three –NO2 groups at the o- and p-positions withdraw electrons (−I and −R) and delocalise the negative charge of the picrate ion, so 2,4,6-trinitrophenol (picric acid, pKa ≈ 0.4) is a stronger acid than carbonic acid (pKa 6.4). It therefore decomposes \(\ce{NaHCO3}\) with effervescence and dissolves; ordinary phenol (pKa 10) is weaker than carbonic acid and cannot.

\(\ce{C6H2(NO2)3OH + NaHCO3 -> C6H2(NO2)3ONa + H2O + CO2 ^}\)

Concepts to recall

  • Electron-withdrawing groups at o/p increase the acidity of phenol; electron-releasing groups decrease it.
  • An acid stronger than \(\ce{H2CO3}\) liberates \(\ce{CO2}\) from bicarbonate.
Q1(c)(ii)Electrochemistry[1]

Explain the following facts in brief:

(ii) Specific conductance of a solution decreases with dilution.

Answer

Specific conductance is the conductance of 1 cm3 of solution. On dilution the number of ions present per cm3 decreases (although the degree of ionisation rises), so fewer ions carry current in that unit volume and \(\kappa\) falls. (Molar conductance, by contrast, increases.)

Concepts to recall

  • \(\kappa\) depends on ions per unit volume; \(\Lambda_m = \kappa\times1000/M\) increases with dilution.
Q1(c)(iii)d- and f-Block Elements[1]

Explain the following facts in brief:

(iii) Transition metals form complex compounds.

Answer

  • Their ions are small and highly charged (high charge density), so they strongly attract ligands.
  • They have vacant (n−1)d (and ns, np) orbitals of suitable energy which can accept lone pairs from ligands to form coordinate bonds.

Concepts to recall

  • Complex formation, colour, paramagnetism, variable valency and catalysis all stem from the partly filled d subshell.
Q1(c)(v)Practical: qualitative analysis (Group IV)[1]

Explain the following facts in brief:

(v) Zinc sulphide is precipitated from an ammoniacal solution in presence of hydrogen sulphide.

Answer

\(\ce{H2S}\) is a weak acid: \(\ce{H2S <=> 2H+ + S^2-}\). In ammoniacal (basic) solution the \(\ce{OH-}\) ions remove \(\ce{H+}\), so the ionisation of \(\ce{H2S}\) increases and [\(\ce{S^2-}\)] becomes high. The ionic product [\(\ce{Zn^2+}\)][\(\ce{S^2-}\)] then exceeds the solubility product of ZnS and a white precipitate forms. In acid solution (Group II) [\(\ce{S^2-}\)] is suppressed by \(\ce{H+}\), so ZnS is not precipitated — which is why \(\ce{Zn^2+}\) is a Group IV cation.

\(\ce{ZnCl2 + H2S + 2NH4OH -> ZnS v + 2NH4Cl + 2H2O}\) (white ppt)

Concepts to recall

  • Group II: \(\ce{H2S}\) in dil. HCl (low [S2−]) → CuS, PbS.
  • Group IV: \(\ce{H2S}\) in \(\ce{NH4Cl + NH4OH}\) (high [S2−]) → ZnS (white), MnS (buff), NiS, CoS (black).

Q2(a)(i)Solutions[2]

A solution containing 8 gm of compound per 100 ml solution was found to have an osmotic pressure of 500 cm of Hg at 27°C. calculate the molecular mass of the compound.

Answer

\(\pi = \dfrac{500}{76}\ \text{atm} = 6.579\ \text{atm}\); \(T = 300\ \text{K}\); \(w = 8\ \text{g}\) in \(V = 0.100\ \text{L}\).

\[\pi V = \frac{w}{M}RT \;\Rightarrow\; M = \frac{wRT}{\pi V} = \frac{8\times0.0821\times300}{6.579\times0.100} = 299.5\ \text{g mol}^{-1}\]

Molecular mass ≈ 299.5 g mol−1 (about 300)

Concepts to recall

  • van't Hoff: \(\pi = CRT\), C in mol L−1, R = 0.0821 L atm K−1 mol−1.
  • 76 cm Hg = 1 atm; T in kelvin.
Q2(a)(ii)Solutions[1]

What happens to the boiling point of water when small amount of sodium chloride is dissolved in it?

Answer

The boiling point rises (elevation of boiling point): the non-volatile solute lowers the vapour pressure, so a higher temperature is needed to make it equal to the external pressure. Since NaCl dissociates into \(\ce{Na+}\) and \(\ce{Cl-}\) (i ≈ 2), the elevation is nearly twice that of the same molality of a non-electrolyte: \(\Delta T_b = iK_bm\).

Concepts to recall

  • \(\Delta T_b = iK_b m\); van't Hoff factor i > 1 for dissociating solutes.
Q2(c)(i)Electrochemistry[2]

If one Faraday were to be 48250C instead of 96500C, what will be the charge of an electron?

Answer

One faraday is the charge on one mole of electrons: \(F = N_A e\). With Avogadro's number unchanged,

\[e = \frac{F}{N_A} = \frac{48250}{6.022\times10^{23}} = 8.01\times10^{-20}\ \text{C}\]

e = 8.01 × 10−20 C (half the actual 1.602 × 10−19 C)

Concepts to recall

  • \(F = N_A e = 6.022\times10^{23}\times1.602\times10^{-19} = 96500\) C mol−1.
Q3(a)(i)Electrochemistry[2]

The values of λ∞m for HCl, NaCl and CH3COONa are 426.1, 126.5 and 91 ohm−1 cm2 mol−1 respectively. Calculate the value of λ∞m for acetic acid.

Answer

By Kohlrausch's law of independent migration of ions:

\[\Lambda^\infty_m(\ce{CH3COOH}) = \Lambda^\infty_m(\ce{CH3COONa}) + \Lambda^\infty_m(\ce{HCl}) - \Lambda^\infty_m(\ce{NaCl})\]

\[= 91.0 + 426.1 - 126.5 = 390.6\ \Omega^{-1}\,\text{cm}^2\,\text{mol}^{-1}\]

\(\Lambda^\infty_m(\ce{CH3COOH}) = 390.6\ \text{S cm}^2\,\text{mol}^{-1}\)

Concepts to recall

  • Kohlrausch: \(\Lambda^\infty_m = \nu_+\lambda^\infty_+ + \nu_-\lambda^\infty_-\).
  • \(\Lambda^\infty_m\) of a weak electrolyte cannot be found by extrapolation; combine strong electrolytes so that the unwanted ions cancel.
Q3(a)(ii)Chemical Kinetics[3]

The temperature coefficient for the hydrolysis of ethyl acetate by sodium hydroxide solution is 1.75. Calculate the energy of activation for the reaction.

Answer

The temperature coefficient is \(k_{T+10}/k_T\). Take \(T_1 = 298\) K, \(T_2 = 308\) K, so \(k_2/k_1 = 1.75\).

\[\log\frac{k_2}{k_1} = \frac{E_a}{2.303R}\left(\frac{T_2 - T_1}{T_1T_2}\right)\]

\[E_a = \frac{2.303\,R\,T_1T_2}{T_2 - T_1}\log\frac{k_2}{k_1} = \frac{2.303\times1.987\times298\times308}{10}\times\log1.75\]

\(\log 1.75 = 0.2430\):

\[E_a = 2.303\times1.987\times9178.4\times0.2430 = 1.021\times10^{4}\ \text{cal mol}^{-1}\]

Ea ≈ 10.2 kcal mol−1 (= 42.7 kJ mol−1)

The temperature range is not stated; 298–308 K is assumed, as is usual for a temperature coefficient.

Concepts to recall

  • Temperature coefficient = \(k_{T+10}/k_T\), usually 2–3.
  • Arrhenius two-temperature form: \(\log\dfrac{k_2}{k_1} = \dfrac{E_a}{2.303R}\cdot\dfrac{T_2-T_1}{T_1T_2}\).
  • R = 1.987 cal = 8.314 J K−1 mol−1.
Q3(c)Electrochemistry[1]

AgNO3 solution cannot be stored in a copper vessel – Explain.

Answer

\(E^\circ(\ce{Cu^2+/Cu}) = +0.34\) V is lower than \(E^\circ(\ce{Ag+/Ag}) = +0.80\) V, so copper is a stronger reducing agent and displaces silver:

\(\ce{Cu + 2AgNO3 -> Cu(NO3)2 + 2Ag}\); \(E^\circ_{cell} = 0.80 - 0.34 = +0.46\) V > 0, so the reaction is spontaneous and the vessel would be eaten away.

Concepts to recall

  • A metal displaces from solution any metal with a higher reduction potential.

Q4(a)Electrochemistry[3]

The standard electrode potential of Pb/Pb2+ and Pt/I−/I2 are −0.126 v and 0.536 v respectively. When a galvanic cell is constructed using 0.1 M concentration of the ions. Pt is found to be the cathode. What is the emf of the cell?

[The values are standard reduction potentials: E°(Pb2+/Pb) = −0.126 V, E°(I2/I−) = +0.536 V.]

Answer

Cell: \(\ce{Pb | Pb^2+ (0.1 M) || I- (0.1 M) | I2 | Pt}\)

Anode: \(\ce{Pb -> Pb^2+ + 2e-}\); cathode: \(\ce{I2 + 2e- -> 2I-}\); cell reaction \(\ce{Pb + I2 -> Pb^2+ + 2I-}\), n = 2.

\(E^\circ_{cell} = E^\circ_{cathode} - E^\circ_{anode} = 0.536 - (-0.126) = 0.662\ \text{V}\)

\[E_{cell} = E^\circ_{cell} - \frac{0.0591}{2}\log\left([\ce{Pb^2+}][\ce{I-}]^2\right) = 0.662 - 0.02955\log(0.1\times0.1^2)\]

\[= 0.662 - 0.02955\times(-3) = 0.662 + 0.0887 = 0.751\ \text{V}\]

Ecell = 0.751 V

Concepts to recall

  • \(E_{cell} = E^\circ_{cell} - \dfrac{0.0591}{n}\log Q\) at 298 K; pure solids and \(\ce{I2}\) (s) are omitted from Q.
  • \(E^\circ_{cell} = E^\circ_{\text{right}} - E^\circ_{\text{left}}\) (reduction potentials).
Q4(b)(i)Chemical Kinetics[2]

What is Arrhenius equation? How can it be used graphically to calculate the energy of activation for a reaction?

Answer

Arrhenius equation: \(k = A\,e^{-E_a/RT}\), where k is the rate constant, A the frequency (pre-exponential) factor, \(E_a\) the activation energy, R the gas constant and T the absolute temperature.

Taking logarithms: \[\log k = \log A - \frac{E_a}{2.303R}\cdot\frac{1}{T}\]

This has the form y = c + mx. Measure k at several temperatures and plot log k against 1/T: a straight line is obtained.

slope = \(-\dfrac{E_a}{2.303R}\) ⇒ \(E_a = -2.303R\times\text{slope}\); intercept = log A

Concepts to recall

  • Higher \(E_a\) ⇒ steeper line ⇒ rate more sensitive to temperature.
  • Using ln k instead: slope = \(-E_a/R\).

Q5(b)Coordination Compounds[1]

Using C.F.T determine the number of unpaired electrons for FeCl4− complex

Answer

\(\ce{[FeCl4]-}\): Fe is +3 (\(x - 4 = -1\)), 3d5; four ligands ⇒ tetrahedral. In a tetrahedral field the d orbitals split into a lower e pair and an upper t2 set, with \(\Delta_t \approx \tfrac49\Delta_o\) — always smaller than the pairing energy, especially with weak-field \(\ce{Cl-}\):

5 unpaired electrons (e2 t23), high spin; μ = 5.92 BM

Concepts to recall

  • Octahedral: t2g below, eg above; tetrahedral: the reverse, e below, t2 above.

Q5(c)Coordination Compounds[2]

Explain why tetrahedral nickel complexes are paramagnetic but square planar Ni(II) complexes are diamagnetic.

Answer

\(\ce{Ni^2+}\) is 3d8 with two unpaired electrons.

  • Tetrahedral (e.g. \(\ce{[NiCl4]^2-}\), weak-field \(\ce{Cl-}\)): the 3d electrons are not disturbed; the ligand pairs enter the 4s and three 4p orbitals (sp3). Two unpaired electrons remain ⇒ paramagnetic (μ ≈ 2.83 BM).
  • Square planar (e.g. \(\ce{[Ni(CN)4]^2-}\), strong-field \(\ce{CN-}\)): the two odd electrons pair up, leaving one 3d orbital empty; it hybridises with 4s and two 4p orbitals (dsp2). No unpaired electron ⇒ diamagnetic.

(In CFT terms: in a square-planar field the \(d_{x^2-y^2}\) orbital is raised so high that all eight electrons pair in the four lower orbitals.)

Concepts to recall

  • sp3 → tetrahedral; dsp2 (uses an inner (n−1)d orbital) → square planar.
  • \(\mu = \sqrt{n(n+2)}\): n = 2 gives 2.83 BM.
Q6(a)(ii)d- and f-Block Elements[2]

Explain the following facts: (ii) In a given transition series, there is no significant change in the atomic radii of elements with increase in atomic number.

Answer

  • Across a transition series the nuclear charge rises by one unit at each element, and the added electron goes into the inner (n−1)d subshell.
  • These d electrons screen the outer ns electrons from the nucleus. The increase in nuclear charge is therefore almost balanced by the increase in screening, so the effective nuclear charge on the outer electrons hardly changes.
  • Hence the atomic radii remain nearly constant (a small decrease at the start; a slight increase near the end of the series because of repulsion among the paired d electrons).

Concepts to recall

  • Zeff = Z − screening constant; d electrons screen poorly but enough to offset the +1 per step.

Q9(a)Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers[3]

Give balanced equations for the following name reactions:

(i) Kolbe-Schmidt reaction. (ii) Hellvolard's Zelinsky reaction (iii) Clemmensen's reduction reaction.

Answer

(i) Kolbe–Schmidt: sodium phenoxide and \(\ce{CO2}\) at about 400 K and 4–7 atm, then acid:

\(\ce{C6H5ONa + CO2 -> C6H4(OH)COONa}\) (sodium salicylate)

\(\ce{C6H4(OH)COONa + HCl -> C6H4(OH)COOH + NaCl}\) (salicylic acid)

(ii) Hell–Volhard–Zelinsky: α-halogenation with red P:

\(\ce{CH3COOH + Cl2 ->[red P] ClCH2COOH + HCl}\)

(iii) Clemmensen reduction: C=O → CH2 with zinc amalgam and conc. HCl:

\(\ce{CH3COCH3 + 4[H] ->[Zn-Hg/conc. HCl] CH3CH2CH3 + H2O}\)

Concepts to recall

  • Kolbe: phenoxide + \(\ce{CO2}\) → o-hydroxy acid.
  • HVZ: needs α-H. Clemmensen (acid) vs Wolff–Kishner (base) both give the alkane.
Q9(b)Organic Compounds containing Nitrogen[4]

An organic compound[A] with molecular formula C3H6O which reduces Tollen's reagent gives compound [B] on treatment with hydroxylamine. [B] on reduction with LiAlH4 gives compound [C]. Compound [C] on warming with chloroform and potassium hydroxide gives compound [D] with unpleasant smell. [D] on reduction with Na/C2H5OH gives [E] which gives insoluble compound with benzene sulphonyl chloride and NaOH. Give the sequence of reactions and suggest structures of[A] to [E]

Answer

\(\ce{C3H6O}\) reducing Tollens' reagent is an aldehyde: propanal (acetone does not reduce it).

\(\ce{CH3CH2CHO + NH2OH -> CH3CH2CH=NOH + H2O}\)

\(\ce{CH3CH2CH=NOH + 4[H] ->[LiAlH4] CH3CH2CH2NH2 + H2O}\)

\(\ce{CH3CH2CH2NH2 + CHCl3 + 3KOH ->[\Delta] CH3CH2CH2NC + 3KCl + 3H2O}\) (carbylamine; offensive smell)

\(\ce{CH3CH2CH2NC + 4[H] ->[Na/C2H5OH] CH3CH2CH2NHCH3}\)

E is a secondary amine; its sulphonamide has no H on N, so it is insoluble in NaOH:

\(\ce{CH3CH2CH2NHCH3 + C6H5SO2Cl -> C6H5SO2N(CH3)CH2CH2CH3 + HCl}\)

APropanal, \(\ce{CH3CH2CHO}\)
BPropanal oxime, \(\ce{CH3CH2CH=NOH}\)
CPropan-1-amine, \(\ce{CH3CH2CH2NH2}\)
DPropyl isocyanide, \(\ce{CH3CH2CH2NC}\)
EN-Methylpropan-1-amine, \(\ce{CH3CH2CH2NHCH3}\)

Concepts to recall

  • Oximes are reduced to 1° amines; isocyanides are reduced to N-methyl 2° amines.
  • Carbylamine test: only 1° amines, foul-smelling isocyanide.
  • Hinsberg: 2° amine → sulphonamide insoluble in alkali.
Q9(c)Aldehydes, Ketones and Carboxylic Acids[3]

Give one chemical test to distinguish between the following pairs.

i) Formic acid and oxalic acid .   ii) Glucose and fructose

Answer

(i) Tollens' reagent: formic acid (it has an H–C=O group) gives a silver mirror on warming; oxalic acid does not.

\(\ce{HCOOH + Ag2O -> 2Ag v + CO2 + H2O}\)

(ii) Bromine water: glucose decolourises it (oxidised to gluconic acid); fructose, a ketose, does not.

\(\ce{CH2OH(CHOH)4CHO + Br2 + H2O -> CH2OH(CHOH)4COOH + 2HBr}\)

Concepts to recall

  • Formic acid is a reducing acid (Tollens', Fehling's +ve).
  • Bromine water oxidises aldoses only.
Q10(a)Aldehydes, Ketones and Carboxylic Acids[4]

Write balanced reactions for the following:

(i) Acetamide is heated with phosphorous pentoxide followed by hydrolysis.

(ii) Benzoic acid is treated with a mixture of conc.H2SO4 and conc.HNO3.

(iii) Ethyl magnesium chloride is treated with formaldehyde and the product is hydrolysed in presence of an acid.

(iv) Diethyl ether reacts with excess of HI.

Answer

(i) Dehydration to methyl cyanide, then acid hydrolysis to acetic acid:

\(\ce{CH3CONH2 ->[P2O5][\Delta] CH3CN + H2O}\); \(\ce{CH3CN + 2H2O + HCl -> CH3COOH + NH4Cl}\)

(ii) –COOH is meta-directing: m-nitrobenzoic acid is formed.

\(\ce{C6H5COOH + HNO3 ->[conc. H2SO4] C6H4(NO2)COOH + H2O}\)

(iii) A primary alcohol with one carbon more (propan-1-ol):

\(\ce{C2H5MgCl + HCHO ->[dry ether] C2H5CH2OMgCl}\); \(\ce{C2H5CH2OMgCl + H2O ->[H+] CH3CH2CH2OH + Mg(OH)Cl}\)

(iv) With excess HI both C–O bonds are cleaved:

\(\ce{C2H5OC2H5 + 2HI ->[\Delta] 2C2H5I + H2O}\)

Concepts to recall

  • RMgX + HCHO → 1° alcohol; + RCHO → 2°; + ketone → 3°.
  • Ether + 1 HI (cold) → ROH + RI; excess HI (hot) → 2 RI.
Q10(b)Biomolecules[2]

On electrolysis in acidic solution amino acids migrate towards cathode, while in alkaline solution these migrate towards anode. Explain

Answer

In water an amino acid exists as a zwitter ion. In acid the –COO− is protonated and the molecule becomes a cation, \(\ce{H3N+-CHR-COOH}\), which moves to the cathode. In alkali the –NH3+ loses a proton and the molecule becomes an anion, \(\ce{H2N-CHR-COO-}\), which moves to the anode. At the isoelectric point it does not migrate.

Concepts to recall

  • Isoelectric point: pH at which the amino acid is electrically neutral (zwitter ion).
