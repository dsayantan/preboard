Q1Titrations (KMnO₄ / ammonium iron(II) sulphate)[8]

You are provided with two solutions as follows:

(a) C-10 is a solution prepared by dissolving 1.15 gms. of potassium manganate (VII), \(\ce{KMnO4}\) per litre.

(b) C-11 is a solution prepared by dissolving 17.2 gms. of impure sample of hydrated ammonium iron (II) sulphate crystals, \(\ce{(NH4)2SO4.FeSO4.6H2O}\) per litre.

PROCEDURE: Rinse and fill the burette with the solution C-10 of potassium manganate (VII). Pipette out 20 ml or 25 ml of ammonium iron(II) sulphate solution C-11 into a clean conical flask. To this, add 20 ml of C-12, solution of dilute \(\ce{H2SO4}\) specially provided for titration. Titrate this solution by running C-10 from the burette till one drop of this solution gives a permanent light pink colour to solution C-11 in the conical flask. Ensure that the pink colour obtained does not disappear on shaking the content of the conical flask.

Repeat the above procedure of titration to get at least two concordant readings.

Tabulate your readings. State (i) The capacity of the pipette used. (ii) The titre value you intend to use in your calculations.

Show the titre value to your visiting examiner.

The equation for the above reaction is as follows:

\[\ce{2KMnO4 + 3H2SO4 -> K2SO4 + 2MnSO4 + 3H2O + 5[O]}\] \[\ce{10FeSO4 + 5H2SO4 + 5[O] -> 5Fe2(SO4)3 + 5H2O}\]

[Relative atomic masses K = 39, Fe = 56, N = 14, S = 32, H = 1, O = 16, Mn = 55]

Calculate the following: (i) The molarity of potassium manganate (VII) solution C-10. (ii) The molarity of hydrated ammonium iron (II) sulphate solution C-11. (iii) The concentration of hydrated ammonium iron (II) sulphate in gms/litre (iv) The percentage purity of hydrated ammonium iron (II) sulphate in the impure sample.

Answer

The burette readings below are sample readings, generated for a sample of about 92% purity and read to the burette least count (0.05 mL, recorded to two decimal places). The calculation is the part to learn.

Pipette used: 20.0 mL of C-11. No indicator: \(\ce{KMnO4}\) is self-indicating (colourless → permanent light pink). No warming — the \(\ce{Fe^2+}\) reaction is fast at room temperature.

S. No.Initial burette reading (mL)Final burette reading (mL)Volume of C-10 used (mL)
1 (rough)0.0022.5022.50
20.0022.2022.20
31.0023.2022.20

(i) Capacity of pipette = 20.0 mL   (ii) Titre value (concordant) = 22.20 mL

Adding the two equations:

\[\ce{2KMnO4 + 10FeSO4 + 8H2SO4 -> K2SO4 + 2MnSO4 + 5Fe2(SO4)3 + 8H2O}\] \[\ce{MnO4- + 5Fe^2+ + 8H+ -> Mn^2+ + 5Fe^3+ + 4H2O}\]

1 mol \(\ce{MnO4-}\) ≡ 5 mol \(\ce{Fe^2+}\).

(i) \(M(\ce{KMnO4}) = 158\) g mol⁻¹: \(M_1 = \dfrac{1.15}{158} = 0.007278\ \text{M}\)

(ii)

\[\frac{M_1V_1}{M_2V_2} = \frac{1}{5}\;\Rightarrow\; M_2 = \frac{5\times0.007278\times22.20}{20.0} = 0.04040\ \text{M}\]

(iii) \(M(\ce{(NH4)2SO4.FeSO4.6H2O}) = 2(18) + 96 + 56 + 96 + 6(18) = 392\) g mol⁻¹

\[\text{concentration} = 0.04040\times392 = 15.84\ \text{g L}^{-1}\]

(iv) \(\%\ \text{purity} = \dfrac{15.84}{17.2}\times100 = 92.1\%\)

M(C-10) = 0.007278 M; M(C-11) = 0.04040 M; 15.84 g L⁻¹; purity ≈ 92.1%

Concepts to recall

  • MnO₄⁻ + 5Fe²⁺ + 8H⁺ → Mn²⁺ + 5Fe³⁺ + 4H₂O: mole ratio 1 : 5.
  • Dilute \(\ce{H2SO4}\) (not HCl, which \(\ce{KMnO4}\) oxidises) prevents hydrolysis of \(\ce{Fe^2+}\) and supplies \(\ce{H+}\).
  • Mohr's salt \(M_r\) = 392; % purity = found ÷ taken × 100.
Q2(a)Characteristic tests of carbohydrates[2½]

[Question 2 carries 5 marks for (a) and (b) together; shown here as 2½ each.] You are provided with two organic compounds C-13 and C-14.

Perform the experiments given below on each of the two compounds. Record the changes taking place at every step of the experiment.

Note the smell of the substance formed, if significant, the colour of the solution obtained, the colour of the precipitate produced and any other observation you may have. State the identity of each compound on the basis of the experiments and the observation changes.

(a) Substance C-13

(i) Take about 2ml of C-13 in a test tube and to this add two drops of alcoholic alpha-napthol solution followed by conc sulphuric acid along the side of the test tube carefully.

(ii) Take about 2ml C-13 in a test tube add about 1ml of Fehling's solution. Warm the contents in a water bath for about five minutes.

(iii) Take about 1–2ml of C-13 in a test-tube to this add 0.5gm of solid lead acetate boil the contents in a water bath and add 5ml of \(\ce{NH4OH}\) solution. Heat the mixture again to boiling in the water bath.

Answer

ExperimentObservationInference
(i) Molisch's test: C-13 + alcoholic α-naphthol, conc. \(\ce{H2SO4}\) poured down the side Violet (purple) ring at the junction of the two layersCarbohydrate present
(ii) Fehling's test: C-13 + Fehling's solution, warm on water bathBlue colour discharged; brick-red precipitateReducing sugar (free aldehyde/α-hydroxy ketone group)
(iii) C-13 + solid lead acetate, boil; add \(\ce{NH4OH}\), boil againA coloured (pinkish / orange-red) precipitate on boilingReducing sugar (supports the carbohydrate)
\[\ce{RCHO + 2Cu^2+ + 5OH- -> RCOO- + Cu2O v + 3H2O}\quad(\ce{Cu2O}\text{ brick red})\]

C-13 is a carbohydrate — a reducing sugar, glucose (\(\ce{C6H12O6}\))

ISC 2027 asks only that the substance be identified as a carbohydrate; glucose is the carbohydrate set in the syllabus. The decisive tests are (i) Molisch and (ii) Fehling. The colour of the lead acetate/ammonia precipitate in (iii) is described differently in different manuals and could not be confirmed against a standard source, so the identification does not rest on it. A standard confirmable test in its place: Tollens' reagent, warm on a water bath — silver mirror (reducing sugar); a protein (milk powder) would instead give the biuret violet colour with NaOH + dilute \(\ce{CuSO4}\).

Concepts to recall

  • Molisch's test (α-naphthol + conc. \(\ce{H2SO4}\)): violet ring — all carbohydrates.
  • Fehling's / Tollens' / Benedict's: positive for reducing sugars (glucose, fructose, maltose, lactose); sucrose negative.
  • Carbohydrate vs protein: Molisch violet ring (carbohydrate) vs biuret violet (protein).

(b) Substance C-14

(i) Take 2 ml of C-14 in a test tube. Add few crystals of iodine and dissolve. Now add NaOH solution with costant shaking until the brown colour disappears. Heat gently in a water bath and cool.

(ii) Take 2 ml of C-14 in a test tube and add 0.5 ml of caustic soda solution followed by 2–3 drops of freshly prepared sodium nitroprusside solution. Allow it to stand for some time. Warm the contents gently in a water bath and cool.

(iii) Take about 2ml of saturated solution of \(\ce{NaHSO3}\) in a test tube. To this add a few drops of C-14

Answer

ExperimentObservationInference

(ii) C-14 + NaOH + sodium nitroprussideRed / wine-red colourationMethyl ketone — acetone
(iii) Saturated \(\ce{NaHSO3}\) + C-14, shakeWhite crystalline precipitate; warmth Carbonyl compound (aldehyde or methyl ketone)

Concepts to recall
