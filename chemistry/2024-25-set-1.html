Q1(A)(i)Electrochemistry[1]

(A) Fill in the blanks by choosing the appropriate word(s) from those given in the bracket: [specific, acetaldehyde, formaldehyde, acetic acid, methyl isocyanide, tetrahedral, octahedral, square planar, calcium bicarbonate, para, iodoform, equivalent, acetone, dia, one, two, three] [4×1=4]

(i) As the concentration of an electrolytic solution increases its ________ conductance increases but _________ conductance decreases.

Answer

As the concentration of an electrolytic solution increases its specific conductance increases but equivalent conductance decreases.

Specific conductance \(\kappa\) is the conductance of 1 cm3 of solution: more concentrated ⇒ more ions per cm3 ⇒ \(\kappa\) rises. Equivalent (and molar) conductance \(\Lambda_{eq} = \dfrac{1000\,\kappa}{N}\) refers to the ions from one gram-equivalent; at higher concentration interionic attraction (strong electrolytes) or a smaller degree of dissociation (weak electrolytes) lowers it.

specific; equivalent

Concepts to recall

  • \(\kappa = \dfrac{1}{R}\cdot\dfrac{l}{A}\) (S cm−1); \(\Lambda_m = \dfrac{1000\,\kappa}{M}\), \(\Lambda_{eq} = \dfrac{1000\,\kappa}{N}\).
  • On dilution: \(\kappa\) decreases, \(\Lambda_m\) and \(\Lambda_{eq}\) increase.
Q1(A)(ii)Coordination Compounds[1]

(ii) Ferricyanide ion is ____ magnetic while ferrocyanide ion is ______ magnetic in nature.

Answer

Ferricyanide ion is paramagnetic while ferrocyanide ion is diamagnetic in nature.

\(\ce{[Fe(CN)6]^3-}\): \(\ce{Fe^3+}\), 3d5; \(\ce{CN-}\) is strong field, so the electrons pair into \(t_{2g}^5\) — one unpaired electron (paramagnetic, d2sp3). \(\ce{[Fe(CN)6]^4-}\): \(\ce{Fe^2+}\), 3d6 → \(t_{2g}^6\), no unpaired electron (diamagnetic).

para; dia

Concepts to recall

  • \(\ce{CN-}\) is a strong-field ligand: \(\Delta_o > P\), low-spin complexes.
  • Any unpaired electron ⇒ paramagnetic; all paired ⇒ diamagnetic.

Q1(A)(iv)Coordination Compounds[1]

(iv) The number of unpaired electrons in [CuCl4]−2 is ______ and is ______ in shape.

Answer

The number of unpaired electrons in \(\ce{[CuCl4]^2-}\) is one and is tetrahedral in shape.

\(\ce{Cu^2+}\) is 3d9: whatever the arrangement, one electron stays unpaired (\(\mu \approx 1.73\) BM). \(\ce{Cl-}\) is a weak-field ligand and four bulky \(\ce{Cl-}\) ions are arranged tetrahedrally (sp3); in crystals the tetrahedron is somewhat flattened.

one; tetrahedral

Some school keys give "square planar" (dsp2) for Cu(II). The isolated \(\ce{[CuCl4]^2-}\) ion, as in \(\ce{Cs2CuCl4}\), is a (flattened) tetrahedron; the number of unpaired electrons is one in either case.

Concepts to recall

  • 3d9 always has one unpaired electron.
  • Four weak-field halide ligands ⇒ usually tetrahedral (sp3), e.g. \(\ce{[NiCl4]^2-}\), \(\ce{[CoCl4]^2-}\).

Q1(B)(ii)Solutions[1]

(ii) Three samples of aqueous solution of KCl are labelled as 10−1 M[A], 10−2 M[B] and 10−3 M[C] respectively. Which of the following represents the correct order of van't Hoff factor for these solutions.

(a) iA < iB < iC   (b) iA > iB > iC   (c) iA = iB = iC   (d) iA < iC < iB

Answer

KCl is a strong electrolyte, \(\ce{KCl -> K+ + Cl-}\), so the ideal value of \(i\) is 2. In a real solution interionic attraction makes the ions behave as if fewer particles were present, so \(i\) is a little less than 2; the effect weakens on dilution and \(i\) approaches 2. Hence the most dilute sample (C) has the largest \(i\).

(a) iA < iB < iC

Concepts to recall

  • \(i\) = observed colligative property ÷ calculated (normal) value.
  • For dissociation \(i = 1 + (n-1)\alpha\); \(\alpha\) (or the effective value for strong electrolytes) rises on dilution, so \(i \to n\).

Q1(B)(iv)Biomolecules[1]

(iv) Which of the statements about denaturation given below are correct?

1. Denaturation causes loss of secondary and tertiary structures of the protein
2. Denaturation leads to conversion of double strand of DNA into single strand.
3. Denaturation affects primary structure which gets distorted.

(a) 1, 2 and 3   (b) 1 and 3   (c) 2 and 3   (d) 1 and 2

Answer

Denaturation (heat, pH change, etc.) breaks hydrogen bonds and other weak interactions: a protein loses its secondary and tertiary structure (statement 1), and the two strands of DNA, held by hydrogen bonds between bases, separate (statement 2).   • Denaturation: loss of 2°/3° structure and biological activity; 1° (amino-acid sequence) intact. e.g. coagulation of egg white.

Q1(B)(vi)Haloalkanes and Haloarenes[1]

(vi) Assertion: C-X bond in aryl halides is less polar than the C-X bond in alkyl halides.
Reason: In aryl halides, the electron withdrawing inductive effect is opposed by electron releasing resonance effect.

(a) Both assertion and reason are true and the reason is the correct explanation of assertion.
(b) Both assertion and reason are true but reason is not the correct explanation for assertion.
(c) Assertion is true but reason is false.
(d) Assertion is false but reason is true.

Answer

In an aryl halide a lone pair of the halogen is delocalised into the ring (+R effect), which partly cancels the −I pull of the halogen, and the C–X bond acquires some double-bond character. So the bond is less polar — e.g. dipole moment of chlorobenzene 1.69 D against about 2.0 D for chloroalkanes. The reason is true and is the explanation (the sp2 carbon, being more electronegative, adds to the effect).

(a) Both true; reason is the correct explanation

Concepts to recall

  • Haloarenes: C–X shorter, stronger, less polar (resonance + sp2 carbon) ⇒ inert to nucleophilic substitution.
Q1(B)(vii)d- and f-Block Elements[1]

(vii) Assertion: Separation of Zr and Hf is difficult.
Reason: Zr and Hf have almost equal atomic radii.

(a) Both assertion and reason are true and the reason is the correct explanation of assertion.
(b) Both assertion and reason are true but reason is not the correct explanation for assertion.
(c) Assertion is true but reason is false.
(d) Assertion is false but reason is true.

Answer

Because of the lanthanoid contraction, Hf (5d series, after the 14 lanthanoids) has almost the same radius as Zr (4d) — 160 pm and 159 pm. With the same valence configuration and nearly the same size, their chemical properties are almost identical, so they occur together and are very hard to separate. The reason explains the assertion.

(a) Both true; reason is the correct explanation

Concepts to recall

  • Lanthanoid contraction: poor shielding by 4f electrons ⇒ steady decrease in size from La to Lu; 4d and 5d members of a group (Zr/Hf, Nb/Ta, Mo/W) have nearly equal radii.
Q1(C)Chemical Kinetics[3]

(C) Read the passage given below carefully and answer the questions that follow: [3]

The rate of the reaction is proportional to the concentration of the reactant. Hydrogenation of ethene results in the formation of ethane. The rate constant k for the reaction was found to be 2.5 × 10−15 s−1. The concentration of the reactant reduces to one-third of the initial concentration in 5 minutes.

(i) State the order of the reaction.

(ii) The rate law equation is:
(a) Rate = k [C2H6]   (b) Rate = k [C2H4]2   (c) Rate = k [C2H4]   (d) Rate = k [C2H4]2

(iii) What is the half life for the reaction?

(iv) What is the rate constant of the reaction after 5 minutes?

[Options (b) and (d) are printed identically in the original.]

Answer

(i) Rate ∝ [reactant] (first power), and k has the unit s−1: first order

(ii) The reactant is ethene: (c) Rate = k[C2H4]

(iii) For a first-order reaction

\[t_{1/2} = \frac{0.693}{k} = \frac{0.693}{2.5\times10^{-15}\ \text{s}^{-1}} = 2.77\times10^{14}\ \text{s}\]

\(t_{1/2} = 2.77\times10^{14}\) s

(iv) The rate constant does not depend on time or concentration (only on temperature), so after 5 minutes it is still \(k = 2.5\times10^{-15}\ \text{s}^{-1}\)

The passage's data are not consistent with each other. If the concentration really fell to one-third in 5 min, then \(k = \dfrac{2.303}{300}\log 3 = 3.66\times10^{-3}\ \text{s}^{-1}\) and \(t_{1/2} = 189\) s. The answers above use the k the passage states, as the questions ask.

Concepts to recall

  • First order: \(k = \dfrac{2.303}{t}\log\dfrac{[A]_0}{[A]}\); unit of k = s−1.
  • \(t_{1/2} = 0.693/k\), independent of concentration.
  • k changes only with temperature (and catalyst), not with time.

Q2Electrochemistry[2]

(i) Conductivity of two electrolytic solutions A and B each one having a concentration of 0.1 M are 8.5 × 10−2 S cm−1 and 4.1 × 10−4 S cm−1 respectively. Which of the two offers less resistance to the flow of current? Explain.

(ii) In each of the following pair which will allow better conduction of electricity. Justify

a. Copper wire at 25°C and copper wire at 40°C

b. 0.1 M acetic acid and 1 M acetic acid.

Answer

(i) Resistivity \(\rho = 1/\kappa\). A has the larger conductivity (8.5 × 10−2 > 4.1 × 10−4 S cm−1), so for the same cell \(R = \dfrac{1}{\kappa}\cdot\dfrac{l}{A}\) is smaller. A offers less resistance — at equal concentration it furnishes far more ions, i.e. A is a strong electrolyte and B a weak one.

(ii) a. Copper wire at 25 °C — metallic (electronic) conduction falls as temperature rises, because the vibrating kernels scatter the moving electrons more, so resistance increases with temperature.

b. 1 M acetic acid conducts better (higher conductivity): although its degree of ionisation is lower, 1 L of it contains more ions in all (\([\ce{H+}] \approx \sqrt{K_ac}\): about \(4.2\times10^{-3}\) M against \(1.3\times10^{-3}\) M for 0.1 M).

The molar conductivity (conducting power per mole) is greater for 0.1 M acetic acid, because it is more ionised (\(\alpha \approx 1.3\%\) against 0.42% for 1 M). The NCERT Exemplar-style key for this pair answers "0.1 M acetic acid" on exactly that basis, so in the examination say which quantity you are comparing: per mole (\(\Lambda_m\), degree of ionisation) — 0.1 M; per unit volume (\(\kappa\), what a pair of electrodes sees) — 1 M. Stating both, with the reason, earns the marks whichever reading the examiner intends.

Concepts to recall

  • \(R = \rho\dfrac{l}{A}\), \(\kappa = 1/\rho\).
  • Metallic conductance decreases with rise in temperature; electrolytic conductance increases.
  • Weak electrolyte: \(\alpha \approx \sqrt{K_a/c}\); \([\ce{H+}] = c\alpha \approx \sqrt{K_ac}\).

Q4Haloalkanes and Haloarenes[2]

(i) Haloalkanes react with KCN to give alkyl cyanide as the main product while with AgCN they form isocyanide as main product. Give reason.

(ii) Give balanced chemical equation: C6H5N2+Cl− treated with Cu/HCl

Answer

(i) The cyanide ion is ambident, \(\ce{[:C#N:]-}\), able to bond through C or N. KCN is ionic, so free \(\ce{CN-}\) ions are available; attack takes place through the carbon atom, which forms the stronger C–C bond → \(\ce{R-C#N}\) (alkyl cyanide). AgCN is mainly covalent (Ag–C bond), so the carbon lone pair is tied up; only the nitrogen lone pair is free to attack → \(\ce{R-N#C}\) (isocyanide).

\(\ce{R-X + KCN -> R-CN + KX}\)    \(\ce{R-X + AgCN -> R-NC + AgX}\)

(ii) Gattermann reaction:

\(\ce{C6H5N2+Cl- ->[Cu/HCl] C6H5Cl + N2 ^}\)

\(\ce{C6H5N2Cl ->[Cu/HCl] C6H5Cl + N2}\) (chlorobenzene)

Concepts to recall

  • Ambident nucleophiles: \(\ce{CN-}\), \(\ce{NO2-}\). Ionic salt → C (or O) attack; covalent silver salt → N attack (isocyanide, nitroalkane).
  • Sandmeyer: CuCl/HCl; Gattermann: Cu powder/HCl.
Q5Chemical Kinetics[2]

A hydrogenation reaction is carried out at 500 K. If the same reaction is carried out in presence of a catalyst at the same rate, the temperature required is 400 K. Calculate the activation energy of the reaction if the catalyst lowers the activation barrier by 20 kJ mol−1.

Answer

Arrhenius equation \(k = Ae^{-E_a/RT}\). Same rate ⇒ same k; the catalyst does not change A:

\[Ae^{-E_a/R(500)} = Ae^{-(E_a-20)/R(400)} \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{E_a}{500} = \frac{E_a - 20}{400}\] \[400\,E_a = 500\,E_a - 10000 \;\Rightarrow\; E_a = 100\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}\]

\(E_a\) (uncatalysed) = 100 kJ mol−1 (catalysed: 80 kJ mol−1)

Concepts to recall

  • \(k = Ae^{-E_a/RT}\); equal rates ⇒ \(E_a/T\) equal.
  • A catalyst lowers \(E_a\) by providing an alternative path; A unchanged (assumed).
Q6Organic Compounds containing Nitrogen[2]

(i) How can the following conversion be brought about — Ethylamine to Methylamine.

(ii) Dimethylamine is more basic than trimethylamine in an aqueous solution. Explain.

Answer

(i) Step down by one carbon through the amide (Hofmann bromamide):

\[\ce{C2H5NH2 ->[HNO2][(NaNO2+HCl)] C2H5OH ->[K2Cr2O7/H+][\text{oxidation}] CH3COOH}\] \[\ce{CH3COOH ->[NH3] CH3COONH4 ->[\Delta][-H2O] CH3CONH2 ->[Br2/KOH] CH3NH2}\]

\(\ce{CH3CONH2 + Br2 + 4KOH -> CH3NH2 + K2CO3 + 2KBr + 2H2O}\)

(ii) Basicity in water depends on (1) +I effect of alkyl groups, which raises electron density on N; (2) solvation of the conjugate acid by hydrogen bonding with water; (3) steric hindrance. \(\ce{(CH3)2NH2+}\) has two N–H bonds to H-bond with water, \(\ce{(CH3)3NH+}\) only one, so the trimethylammonium ion is less stabilised; three methyl groups also crowd the nitrogen and hinder approach of \(\ce{H+}\) and of water. These outweigh the extra +I effect, so dimethylamine is the stronger base (pKb 3.27 against 4.22).

Concepts to recall

  • Hofmann bromamide degradation: \(\ce{RCONH2 -> RNH2}\) (one C less).
  • Order in water (methyl): 2° > 1° > 3° > \(\ce{NH3}\) — +I, solvation and steric effects together.

b. In acetic acid the solvent itself is different: its normal boiling point is 118.1 °C and \(K_b \approx 3.07\) K kg mol−1 (water 0.52). With 500 mL ≈ 525 g of acetic acid, \(\Delta T_b = 3.07\times\dfrac{10/180}{0.525} \approx 0.33\) K, so the solution boils at about 118.4 °C — very different.

The boiling point is not the same in either case

(iii) Osmotic pressure. For a polymer (large M) a reasonable mass gives a very low molality, so \(\Delta T_b\) and \(\Delta T_f\) are too small to measure accurately, whereas \(\pi\) is measurable (e.g. a few mm of liquid column). It is measured at room temperature, so polymers/biomolecules that are unstable at high temperature are not decomposed, and molarity rather than molality is used.

Concepts to recall

  • Henry: \(p = K_H x\); higher \(K_H\) ⇒ less soluble.
  • \(\Delta T_b = K_b m\): depends on the solvent (\(K_b\)) and on the number of solute moles.
  • Osmotic pressure is the method of choice for macromolecules.
