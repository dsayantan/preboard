Q1Titrations (KMnO₄ / oxalic acid)[8]

You are provided with two solutions as follows:

(a) C-10 is a solution containing 1.1 gms of potassium manganate(VII), \(\ce{KMnO4}\) per litre.

(b) C-11 is a solution containing 3.0 gms of impure sample of oxalic acid, \(\ce{H2C2O4.2H2O}\) per litre.

PROCEDURE: Rinse and fill the burette with the given solution C-10 of potassium manganate(VII). Pipette out 20 ml or 25 ml of oxalic acid solution C-11 into a clean conical flask. To this, add 20 ml of dilute \(\ce{H2SO4}\) solution C-12, specially provided for this purpose. Warm the contents of the flask to 60–70°C (till the first bubble appears at the bottom of the flask).

Remove from fire and titrate against C-10 with constant shaking, until a permanent faint pink colour is obtained. Ensure that the pink colour obtained does not disappear on shaking the contents of the conical flask.

Repeat the above procedure of titration to get at least two concordant readings.

Tabulate your readings. State (i) The capacity of the pipette used (ii) The titre value you intend to use in your calculations.

Show the titre value to the examiner

The equation for the above reactions are as follows:

\[\ce{2KMnO4 + 3H2SO4 + 5H2C2O4 -> K2SO4 + 2MnSO4 + 8H2O + 10CO2}\] \[\ce{2MnO4- + 5C2O4^2- + 16H+ -> 2Mn^2+ + 10CO2 + 8H2O}\]

Relative atomic masses: K = 39, Mn = 55, C = 12, O = 16, H = 1

Calculate the following: (i) The molarity of potassium manganate(VII) solution C-10 (ii) The molarity of oxalic acid solution C-11 (iii) The strength of oxalic acid solution in gms per litre. (iv) The percentage purity of the sample of oxalic acid.

Answer

The burette readings below are sample readings, generated for a sample of about 90% purity and read to the burette least count of 0.05 mL (two decimal places as ISC requires). Your own titre will differ; the method of calculation is what matters.

Pipette used: 20.0 mL (C-11, oxalic acid). Burette: C-10 (\(\ce{KMnO4}\)); \(\ce{KMnO4}\) acts as its own indicator — end point colourless → permanent faint pink.

S. No.Initial burette reading (mL)Final burette reading (mL)Volume of C-10 used (mL)
1 (rough)0.0024.9024.90
20.0024.6024.60
30.5025.1024.60

(i) Capacity of pipette = 20.0 mL   (ii) Titre value (concordant) = 24.60 mL

(i) Molarity of C-10. \(M(\ce{KMnO4}) = 39 + 55 + 64 = 158\) g mol⁻¹

\[M_1 = \frac{1.1}{158} = 0.006962\ \text{M}\]

(ii) Molarity of C-11. From the ionic equation, 2 mol \(\ce{MnO4-}\) ≡ 5 mol \(\ce{C2O4^2-}\):

\[\frac{M_1V_1}{M_2V_2} = \frac{2}{5}\;\Rightarrow\; M_2 = \frac{5}{2}\times\frac{M_1V_1}{V_2} = \frac{5\times0.006962\times24.60}{2\times20.0} = 0.02141\ \text{M}\]

(iii) Strength of C-11. \(M(\ce{H2C2O4.2H2O}) = 2 + 24 + 64 + 36 = 126\) g mol⁻¹

\[\text{strength} = 0.02141\times126 = 2.697\ \text{g L}^{-1}\]

(iv) Percentage purity (3.0 g of the impure sample per litre):

\[\%\ \text{purity} = \frac{2.697}{3.0}\times100 = 89.9\%\]

M(C-10) = 0.006962 M; M(C-11) = 0.02141 M; 2.697 g L⁻¹; purity ≈ 89.9%

Concepts to recall

  • \(\dfrac{M_1V_1}{n_1} = \dfrac{M_2V_2}{n_2}\) with \(n_1 : n_2\) from the balanced equation (2 \(\ce{MnO4-}\) : 5 \(\ce{C2O4^2-}\)).
  • Warm oxalic acid to 60–70 °C: the reaction is slow cold; \(\ce{Mn^2+}\) formed autocatalyses it.
  • Molarity to at least 4 decimal places; concordant = two identical titres (not an average).
  • % purity = (strength found ÷ mass dissolved per litre) × 100.
Q2(a)Identification of organic compounds (ketonic group)[2½]

[Question 2 carries 5 marks for (a) and (b) together; shown here as 2½ each.] Substances C-13 and C-14 are organic compounds. Carry out the following experiments and note down all the changes taking place at each step of the experiment.

Note the smell of the substance formed, colour of the substance obtained, colour of the precipitate formed and solubity in excess of the reagent and any other observations you may have. State the identity of each compound on the basis of the experiments and observational changes.

PROCEDURE: (a) Substance C-13

(i) Take 2ml of C-13 in a test tube and add a concentrated solution of sodium bisulphite and shake the contents thoroughly.

(ii) Take 2ml of C-13 in a test tube and mix it with freshly prepared solution of sodium nitroprusside. Add ammonium hydroxide solution and shake the contents, warm and cool.

(iii) Take 2ml of C-13 in a test tube and add 1 cm³ of mercuric chloride solution. To this add sodium hydroxide solution dropwise. Shake the contents.

Answer

ExperimentObservationInference
(i) C-13 + saturated \(\ce{NaHSO3}\), shakeWhite crystalline precipitate separates; the tube becomes warm Carbonyl compound (aldehyde or methyl ketone) — bisulphite addition compound
(ii) C-13 + sodium nitroprusside, then \(\ce{NH4OH}\); warm and coolRed / wine-red colouration Methyl ketone (CH₃CO–) — acetone
(iii) C-13 + \(\ce{HgCl2}\), then NaOH dropwise, shakeThe yellow precipitate of HgO that NaOH forms dissolves on shaking (no ppt persists)Acetone dissolves the HgO — confirms acetone
\[\ce{CH3COCH3 + NaHSO3 -> (CH3)2C(OH)SO3Na}\] \[\ce{HgCl2 + 2NaOH -> HgO v + 2NaCl + H2O}\]

C-13 is acetone (propanone), \(\ce{CH3COCH3}\) — ketonic group

Concepts to recall

  • Bisulphite test: crystalline adduct with aldehydes and methyl ketones.
  • Sodium nitroprusside + base: red colour with acetone (methyl ketone); aldehydes do not give it.
  • HgO (from \(\ce{HgCl2}\) + NaOH) dissolves in acetone.
Q2(b)Identification of organic compounds (amino group)[2½]

(b) Substance C-14

(i) Take 2ml of C-14 in a test tube and add 5 cm³ of water, stir it and add 2–3 drops of sodium hypochlorite (NaClO) solution. Shake the mixture thoroughly.

(ii) Take 2ml of C-14 with 5 cm³ of water in a test tube. To this add few drops of bleaching powder solution and shake the contents thoroughly. Dilute the contents with 1 cm³ of water and add few drops of ammonium sulphide solution.

(iii) Take 2ml of C-14 with 1 cm³ of concentrated hydrochloric acid in a test tube. To this add a few drops of Ferric chloride solution. Shake the contents and dilute with some water.

Answer

ExperimentObservationInference
(i) C-14 (oily, sparingly soluble in water) + NaOClPurple / violet colouration Aromatic primary amine — aniline
(ii) C-14 + water + bleaching powder solution, shake; dilute, add a few drops of ammonium sulphide Violet (purple) colouration with bleaching powder. Record honestly any further change on adding ammonium sulphide (a reducing agent); it is not used for the inference.Aniline (oxidation of the \(\ce{-NH2}\) group by \(\ce{OCl-}\) gives the violet colour)
(iii) C-14 + conc. HCl + neutral \(\ce{FeCl3}\); dilute with waterThe oil dissolves in conc. HCl (anilinium chloride); with \(\ce{FeCl3}\) a green colouration develops, darkening on standingAnilinium salt oxidised by \(\ce{Fe^3+}\) (green oxidation products of aniline) — aniline
\[\ce{C6H5NH2 + HCl -> C6H5NH3+Cl-}\]

C-14 is aniline (aminobenzene), \(\ce{C6H5NH2}\) — amino group

The decisive observations are the violet colour with hypochlorite/bleaching powder and the solubility of the oil in HCl. The colour change on adding ammonium sulphide in (ii) is not a standard recorded observation and could not be confirmed, so no inference rests on it. Two standard confirmatory tests for aniline, if more evidence is wanted: (1) C-14 + dil. \(\ce{H2SO4}\) + \(\ce{K2Cr2O7}\), warm — green, then blue, finally black (aniline black; the test set in ISC 2019); (2) azo-dye test — dissolve in dil. HCl, cool to 0–5 °C, add cold \(\ce{NaNO2}\) solution, then alkaline β-naphthol — orange-red dye. (Carbylamine test is not performed in the ISC practical.)

Concepts to recall

  • Aniline: colourless oil turning brown, insoluble in water, dissolves in dil. HCl.
  • Bleaching powder / NaOCl test: violet colour (oxidation of \(\ce{-NH2}\)).
  • Oxidising agents (\(\ce{FeCl3}\) in HCl; \(\ce{K2Cr2O7}\)/dil. \(\ce{H2SO4}\)) give green-to-black colours (aniline black).
  • Azo-dye test: diazotise at 0–5 °C, couple with alkaline β-naphthol → orange-red dye.
