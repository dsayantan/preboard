Q1(A)(i)Solutions[1]

(A) Fill in the blanks by choosing the appropriate word(s) from those given in the brackets: [substitution, decreases, carbocation, tripled, 2-chloro propane, tertiary, halved, allyl chloride, Raoult's, does, 2-propanone, elimination, doesn't, iodoform, primary, first, doubled, Lucas, free-radical, Hinsberg, doubled, secondary, remains same, vinyl chloride, zero, second] [4×1]

(i) Ideal solution obeys ________ law and they ________ form azeotropic mixture.

Answer

Ideal solution obeys Raoult's law and they doesn't [do not] form azeotropic mixture.

An ideal solution obeys Raoult's law at all compositions (\(\Delta H_\text{mix} = 0\), \(\Delta V_\text{mix} = 0\)); azeotropes arise only from large positive or negative deviations, so ideal solutions can always be separated by fractional distillation.

Raoult's; doesn't

Concepts to recall

  • Ideal: \(p_A = p^\circ_Ax_A\) at all compositions; A–B forces = A–A and B–B forces.
  • Azeotrope: boils at constant temperature with the same composition in liquid and vapour (non-ideal only).
Q1(A)(ii)Chemical Kinetics[1]

(ii) When the concentration of reactant of first order reaction is doubled, the rate becomes ______ but for ______ order reaction, the rate remains same.

Answer

When the concentration of reactant of first order reaction is doubled, the rate becomes doubled but for zero order reaction, the rate remains same.

Rate \(= k[\mathrm{A}]^1\) doubles; rate \(= k[\mathrm{A}]^0 = k\) is independent of concentration.

doubled; zero

Concepts to recall

  • Rate \(= k[\mathrm{A}]^n\): changing [A] by a factor \(x\) changes the rate by \(x^n\).

Q1(B)(vi)Solutions[1]

(vi) Given below are two statements marked Assertion and Reason. Read the two statements carefully and select the correct option.

Assertion: ΔHmix and ΔVmix are greater than zero for non-ideal solution.
Reason: The interactions between the particles of the components of a non-ideal solution are different from that of particles in the liquids.

(a) Both assertion and reason are true, and reason is the correct explanation of assertion.
(b) Both assertion and reason are true, but reason is not the correct explanation for assertion.
(c) Assertion is true, but reason is false.
(d) Assertion is false, but reason is true.

Answer

The assertion is true only for solutions with positive deviation; for negative deviation (e.g. chloroform + acetone) \(\Delta H_\text{mix} < 0\) and \(\Delta V_\text{mix} < 0\). So as a general statement about non-ideal solutions it is false. The reason correctly describes non-ideal behaviour.

(d) Assertion is false, but reason is true

Concepts to recall

  • Positive deviation: weaker A–B forces, \(\Delta H, \Delta V > 0\). Negative deviation: stronger A–B forces, \(\Delta H, \Delta V < 0\).

Q1(C)Electrochemistry[3]

C. Read the passage given below carefully and answer the questions that follow:

The study of the conductivity of electrolyte solutions is important for the development of electrochemical devices, for the characterization of the dissociation equilibrium of weak electrolytes, and for the fundamental understanding of charge transport by ions. The conductivity of the electrolyte is measured for electrolyte solution with concentrations in the range of 10−3 to 10−1 mol L−1, as a solution in this range of concentrations can be easily prepared. The molar conductivity (Λm) of strong electrolyte solutions can be nicely fit by the Kohlrausch equation.

Λm = Λ°m – K √C …(i)

Where Λ°m is the molar conductivity at infinite dilution and C is the concentration of the solution. K is an empirical proportionality constant to be obtained from the experiment. The molar conductivity of weak electrolytes, on the other hand, is dependent on the degree of dissociation of the electrolyte.

(i) What happens to molar conductivity of weak electrolyte on dilution?

(ii) If Λ°m of HCl, NaCl and CH3COONa is 426.1, 126.4 and 91.0 S cm2 mol−1 respectively, calculate Λ°m of CH3COOH.

(iii) 0.05 M NaOH solution offered a resistance of 31.6 ohm in a conductivity cell at 298 K having conductivity 0.01161 S cm−1. If the same cell filled with 0.02 M HCl solution, the resistance changed to 29.2 ohm. Find the conductivity of the acid solution.

Answer

(i) It increases steeply on dilution, especially at low concentration: the degree of dissociation \(\alpha\) increases, so the number of ions per mole rises (\(\Lambda_m = \alpha\Lambda^\circ_m\)). The curve of \(\Lambda_m\) against \(\sqrt{C}\) is not linear, so \(\Lambda^\circ_m\) cannot be found by extrapolation.

(ii) Kohlrausch's law of independent migration:

\[\Lambda^\circ_m(\ce{CH3COOH}) = \Lambda^\circ_m(\ce{CH3COONa}) + \Lambda^\circ_m(\ce{HCl}) - \Lambda^\circ_m(\ce{NaCl})\]

\[= 91.0 + 426.1 - 126.4 = 390.7\]

\(\Lambda^\circ_m(\ce{CH3COOH}) = 390.7\) S cm2 mol−1

(iii) Cell constant \(G^* = \kappa R = 0.01161\times31.6 = 0.3669\) cm−1.

\[\kappa_\text{HCl} = \frac{G^*}{R} = \frac{0.3669}{29.2} = 1.256\times10^{-2}\ \text{S cm}^{-1}\]

\(\kappa\)(HCl) \(\approx 0.0126\) S cm−1

Concepts to recall

  • \(\kappa = G^*/R\); cell constant \(G^* = l/A\) is fixed for a given cell.
  • Kohlrausch: \(\Lambda^\circ_m\) of a weak electrolyte from those of strong electrolytes.

Q2Chemical Kinetics[2]

The rate of reaction becomes 4 times when the temperature changes from 293 K to 313 K. Calculate the activation energy of the reaction. What will be the value of the rate constant at very high temperature?

Answer

\[\log\frac{k_2}{k_1} = \frac{E_a}{2.303R}\left(\frac{T_2-T_1}{T_1T_2}\right)\]

\[\log 4 = \frac{E_a}{2.303\times8.314}\times\frac{313-293}{293\times313}\]

\[0.6021 = \frac{E_a}{19.147}\times2.181\times10^{-4}\]

\[E_a = \frac{0.6021\times19.147}{2.181\times10^{-4}} = 5.29\times10^{4}\ \text{J mol}^{-1}\]

\(E_a \approx 52.9\) kJ mol−1

At very high temperature \(E_a/RT \to 0\), so \(k = Ae^{-E_a/RT} \to A\): k approaches the pre-exponential (frequency) factor A

Concepts to recall

  • Arrhenius: \(k = Ae^{-E_a/RT}\); as \(T\to\infty\), \(k \to A\).
  • Rate ratio = rate-constant ratio at fixed concentrations.

Q5Solutions[2]

A 10 % aqueous solution of cane sugar (mol. wt. 342) is isotonic with 1.754 % aqueous solution of urea. Find the molecular mass of urea. If the experimental molecular mass of urea is 60.06 g/mol, what is the value of van't Hoff factor of urea?

Answer

Isotonic solutions have equal molar concentrations (\(\pi = CRT\) at the same T). For equal volumes (100 mL):

\[\frac{10}{342} = \frac{1.754}{M} \;\Rightarrow\; M = \frac{1.754\times342}{10} = 59.99\ \text{g mol}^{-1}\]

Molecular mass of urea (from the isotonic data) = 59.99 ≈ 60.0 g mol−1

Taking the paper's wording as given — 59.99 g mol−1 is the value calculated above and 60.06 g mol−1 the experimental (observed) value:

\[i = \frac{\text{calculated (normal) molar mass}}{\text{experimental (observed) molar mass}} = \frac{59.99}{60.06} = 0.999 \approx 1\]

\(i \approx 1\) — urea neither dissociates nor associates in water.

Strictly the roles are the other way round: 60.06 g mol−1 is the formula mass of \(\ce{CO(NH2)2}\) (the normal value) and 59.99 g mol−1 is what the colligative (isotonic) measurement gives (the observed value), so \(i = 60.06/59.99 = 1.001\). Either reading gives \(i \approx 1\).

Concepts to recall

  • Isotonic: \(\pi_1 = \pi_2\) ⇒ \(C_1 = C_2\) (same T, non-electrolytes).
  • \(i\) = normal / observed molar mass.

Q7Electrochemistry[2]

Conductivity of 0.00241 M acetic acid is 7.896 × 10−5 S cm−1. Calculate the dissociation constant of acetic acid. Given the molar conductivity of acetic acid at infinite dilution is 390 S cm2 mol−1.

Answer

\[\Lambda_m = \frac{\kappa\times1000}{C} = \frac{7.896\times10^{-5}\times1000}{0.00241} = 32.76\ \text{S cm}^2\,\text{mol}^{-1}\]

\[\alpha = \frac{\Lambda_m}{\Lambda^\circ_m} = \frac{32.76}{390} = 0.0840\]

\[K_a = \frac{C\alpha^2}{1-\alpha} = \frac{0.00241\times(0.0840)^2}{1-0.0840} = 1.86\times10^{-5}\]

\(K_a \approx 1.86\times10^{-5}\) mol L−1

Concepts to recall

  • \(\Lambda_m = 1000\kappa/C\); \(\alpha = \Lambda_m/\Lambda^\circ_m\); \(K_a = C\alpha^2/(1-\alpha)\).
Q8d- and f-Block Elements[2]

(i) Cr2+ is reducing but Mn3+ is oxidizing agent when both have d4 configuration. Explain.

(ii) Why is copper considered as transition element but zinc is not?

Answer

(i) \(\ce{Cr^2+}\) (3d4) loses an electron to become \(\ce{Cr^3+}\) (3d3), which has a stable half-filled \(t_{2g}^3\) set — so it readily gives electrons: a reducing agent (\(E^\circ_{\ce{Cr^3+/Cr^2+}} = -0.41\) V). \(\ce{Mn^3+}\) (3d4) gains an electron to become \(\ce{Mn^2+}\) (3d5), the extra-stable half-filled d5 configuration — so it readily accepts electrons: an oxidising agent (\(E^\circ_{\ce{Mn^3+/Mn^2+}} = +1.57\) V).

(ii) A transition element has an incompletely filled d subshell in the atom or in a common ion. Copper: Cu is 3d104s1 but its common ion \(\ce{Cu^2+}\) is 3d9 — incomplete d ⇒ transition element. Zinc: 3d10 in the atom and in \(\ce{Zn^2+}\), its only ion — never an incomplete d subshell ⇒ not a transition element.

Concepts to recall

  • Half-filled (d5, t2g3) and full (d10) configurations are extra stable; ions move towards them.

Q10Chemical Kinetics[2]

The rate constant of a first order reaction at T K is given by the following expression

\[\log k = \frac{-3163}{T} + 11.9\]

K is expressed in min−1. What is the activation energy and half-life period of the reaction at 20 oC?

Answer

Compare with the Arrhenius equation in log form: \(\log k = \log A - \dfrac{E_a}{2.303RT}\).

\[\frac{E_a}{2.303R} = 3163 \;\Rightarrow\; E_a = 3163\times2.303\times8.314 = 6.06\times10^{4}\ \text{J mol}^{-1}\]

\(E_a \approx 60.6\) kJ mol−1

At 20 °C, T = 293 K: \(\log k = -\dfrac{3163}{293} + 11.9 = -10.795 + 11.9 = 1.105\) ⇒ \(k = 12.7\) min−1.

\[t_{1/2} = \frac{0.693}{k} = \frac{0.693}{12.7} = 0.0544\ \text{min}\]

\(t_{1/2} \approx 5.4\times10^{-2}\) min (about 3.3 s)

Concepts to recall

  • \(\log k = \log A - \dfrac{E_a}{2.303RT}\): slope of log k vs 1/T is \(-E_a/2.303R\).
  • First order: \(t_{1/2} = 0.693/k\).

Q16(i)Electrochemistry[3]

(i) (a) A cell is prepared by dipping copper rod in 1 M copper sulphate solution and zinc rod in 1 M zinc sulphate solution. The standard reduction potential of copper and zinc are 0.34 V and –0.76 V respectively. Write the cell reaction and calculate the standard free energy change of the reaction.

(b) What is the role of salt-bridge in a galvanic cell?

Answer

(a) Zn (lower E°) is the anode, Cu the cathode.

Anode: \(\ce{Zn -> Zn^2+ + 2e-}\); cathode: \(\ce{Cu^2+ + 2e- -> Cu}\).

Cell reaction: \(\ce{Zn(s) + Cu^2+(aq) -> Zn^2+(aq) + Cu(s)}\)

\(E^\circ_\text{cell} = E^\circ_\text{cathode} - E^\circ_\text{anode} = 0.34 - (-0.76) = 1.10\) V.

\[\Delta G^\circ = -nFE^\circ_\text{cell} = -2\times96500\times1.10 = -2.123\times10^{5}\ \text{J mol}^{-1}\]

\(\Delta G^\circ = -212.3\) kJ mol−1

(b) Role of the salt bridge:

  1. It completes the internal circuit: ions move through it, so current can flow between the two solutions without the solutions mixing.
  2. It maintains electrical neutrality of the two half-cells: as the anode solution gains cations (or the cathode solution loses them), anions of the bridge electrolyte migrate to the anode side and cations to the cathode side. Without it, charge would build up and the cell would stop within moments.
  3. It minimises the liquid-junction potential (an inert electrolyte such as KCl or \(\ce{KNO3}\) in agar, whose ions have nearly equal mobilities).

Concepts to recall

  • \(\Delta G^\circ = -nFE^\circ_\text{cell}\), F = 96500 C mol−1.
  • Anode: oxidation (− in a galvanic cell); cathode: reduction (+).

iv) Which colligative property is preferred for the molar mass determination of macromolecules? Give reasons.

(i) \(\Delta T_b = 68.04 - 61.7 = 6.34\) °C.

\[M_B = \frac{K_b\times w_B\times1000}{\Delta T_b\times w_A} = \frac{3.63\times6.21\times1000}{6.34\times24.0} = 148\ \text{g mol}^{-1}\]

(iv) Osmotic pressure.

  • Even very dilute solutions of a macromolecule give an osmotic pressure large enough to measure accurately, whereas \(\Delta T_b\) and \(\Delta T_f\) would be tiny (fractions of a millikelvin).

  • Molarity is used, not molality, and the solution need not be heated or cooled.

(a) \(\ce{Co^3+}\) is 3d6. (b) \(\ce{Fe^3+}\) is 3d5. \(\ce{H2O}\) is a weak-field ligand (\(\Delta_o < P\)): configuration \(t_{2g}^3e_g^2\), five unpaired electrons, \(\mu \approx 5.92\) BM ⇒ strongly paramagnetic. \(\ce{CN-}\) is a strong-field ligand (\(\Delta_o > P\)): electrons pair in \(t_{2g}\), giving \(t_{2g}^5e_g^0\), one unpaired electron, \(\mu \approx 1.73\) BM ⇒ weakly paramagnetic.

complexions in solution
\(\ce{[Co(NH3)3Cl3]}\)0 (non-electrolyte)
\(\ce{[Co(NH3)4Cl2]Cl}\)2
\(\ce{[Co(NH3)5Cl]Cl2}\)3
\(\ce{[Co(en)3]Cl3}\)4

[Co(NH3)3Cl3] < [Co(NH3)4Cl2]Cl < [Co(NH3)5Cl]Cl2 < [Co(en)3]Cl3

(d)1. A gives AgCl, so \(\ce{Cl-}\) is the counter ion and sulphate is coordinated; B gives \(\ce{BaSO4}\), so sulphate is the counter ion.

A = \(\ce{[Co(NH3)5(SO4)]Cl}\); B = \(\ce{[Co(NH3)5Cl]SO4}\)

(d)2. Ionisation isomerism — same formula, different ions in solution.

  • Inner orbital (d2sp3) needs two empty (n−1)d orbitals; outer orbital (sp3d2) uses nd.
  • Weak field: \(\Delta_o < P\), high spin; strong field: \(\Delta_o > P\), low spin.
  • Ionisation isomers exchange a ligand with a counter ion.
